# Documents on the desk

A tab group's desk can hold documents, not only pages: a file dropped on the desk joins the group's context (the Stack, `docs/desk-agent.md`) and opens as a window of its own, in a viewer for its kind, beside the group's tabs. A document is edited where it lies — text, markdown, Word documents, CSV cells — and the edits are saved into the file in the context, where the agent reads them and the person's other Macs sync them. It is collapsed into the Stack as a tab is collapsed into its icon, and comes out of the Stack again. In the Bar, `@` and a file's name attach that file to the message.

Desktop only (the desk is). Builds on `docs/desk.md` (the desk and its windows) and `docs/desk-agent.md` (the Bar, the Stack, the group's context).

## 1. What a person does

**Drop files on the desk.** Drag files from Finder (or another app) over the desk and it shows where they can go, each lighting up as the drag comes over it:
- *The workspace* — the whole desk beside the sidebar, the Bar's notch over its foot, a dashed frame in the group's colour with *Drop to open on the desk*. Let go there, and each file joins the group's context and comes out as a window centred where it was let go (several fan out from there), in use, sized for its kind (a page is tall, a sheet wide).
- *The Bar* — outlined, its placeholder *Drop here to attach to your message*: as before, a picture, a PDF or a text file is attached to the next message; a Word or Excel file (anything the Bar cannot attach as it is) joins the context instead, and the message mentions it (`@name`).
- *The Stack* — the file joins the context without opening.

The drag may come over a live page first: the page tells the desk (§3), the targets go up, and every page gives way to its still, so the drag is the desk's wherever it goes. A drag that leaves the window, or is called off, takes the targets down. Files the desk does not take (an archive, a movie) are said so in a notice; the rest open.

**The kinds.** Each has a viewer (`components/desk/files/`):

| Kind | Files | The viewer |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Text | `.txt`, and `.json` and `.ics` as text | The words, edited in place. Tab types a tab. A file not in UTF-8 is shown, not edited. Line endings and a byte-order mark are written back as they came. |
| Markdown | `.md` | The document it describes, edited as a note is (the notes' editor, `lib/notes-markdown.ts`), or, in *Markdown*, its source. Front matter is kept aside and written back as it was. A file never edited is never rewritten. |
| Word | `.docx`, `.doc` | Its page (the width and margins the file says), zoomed to fit the window, in its own styles: headings, fonts, sizes, colours, bold and italics, alignment and indents, lists with their numbers and bullets (counted afresh as it is edited), tables with their widths, fills and borders, pictures, links. Its text is edited in place (§3). A `.doc` is shown and edited as the `.docx` macOS makes of it, and written back as a `.doc`. |
| Sheet | `.xlsx`, `.csv` | The cells under column letters and beside row numbers, which stay as it scrolls; one cell selected (the arrows, Tab, Enter, ⌘C). A workbook shows each sheet as a tab and each cell as Excel shows it — numbers and dates through their formats, formulas as their last values, bold, colours, fills, alignment, column widths, merged and hidden cells — and is not edited. A CSV's cells are edited in place (Enter, a double click, or typing; Delete clears one; a row and a column past the end to write into), and every row nobody edited is written back as it was. |
| Picture | PNG, JPEG, GIF, WebP, SVG, BMP, AVIF, HEIC, TIFF | Fitted to the window, or zoomed (the bar's − and +, ⌘+ ⌘− ⌘0, pinching) about the pointer and moved by dragging; a chequerboard behind transparent pixels. HEIC and TIFF are shown as the PNG macOS makes of them. The frame says its size. |
| PDF | `.pdf` | Its pages one under another, fitted to the width or zoomed (the same keys and bar), each drawn as it comes into view at the screen's resolution, its text selectable and copyable (pdf.js). The bar counts the page in view. |

**A document's window** is a window like a tab's: moved, thrown, snapped, resized, filled, tiled and cascaded with the tabs, stacked among them, the drop rail and the Bar's notch as for any window (the notch a hole through it where it is under it). Its title bar has the file's glyph, its name, and what its viewer says — its kind, its pages or size — or where its edits stand: *Edited*, *Saving…*, *Saved*, *Not saved*, *Changed elsewhere*. Its controls: *Open in its app* (a private copy, in the app the Mac opens it with), fill the desk, minimize, collapse. Minimized, it is scaled to 50% by the shell, the same viewer throughout (docs/desk.md, Minimize). Clicking into it uses it: it comes to the top and its content takes the keyboard; the browser's tab in use stays as it was (a document is no tab). There is no mask, and no address.

**Saving.** An edit is saved a moment after the person stops (700 ms), and at once with ⌘S, or as the window is collapsed. It is saved over the version it was made to: if the file changed meanwhile (on another Mac, say), the window says so and asks — *Keep mine* writes over it, *Load theirs* gives the edit up. A file changed elsewhere while its window has no edits simply shows the new version. An edit is never lost with its window: collapsed (or carried off as the desk passes to another group) with an edit waiting, the edit is written as the window goes; if that is refused, it is kept, and the file's next window shows it again and asks.

**Collapsed and back.** The window's − (or the drop rail's Collapse, or a fling at the leading edge) sends it into the Stack, which bounces as it takes it. Letting go on the rail's Close closes the window; the file stays in the Stack either way — it leaves only by its × on the Stack's card. On the card, a click on a file brings its window out (or, out already, to the top), flying from the Stack; a dot marks each file whose window is out, and *Open in its app* is beside the ×. Leaving the desk, its documents go home with it; opened again, they come back where they were left (they are saved with the arrangement, `SavedDeskWindow`).

**@mentions.** In the Bar, `@` (at the start or after a space) lists the group's files, best matches first as more is typed; the arrows choose, Enter or Tab inserts `@name ` (names with spaces are fine), Escape closes the list. Each file mentioned in a message is lit behind the field's text, and rides with the message as the agent would read it (§4): its text (a Word document's and a workbook's too), or the file for the model to look at (a picture, a PDF) — too large to attach, a line saying where it is. The thread shows each as a chip, not its whole text, and the conversation is titled by what was written, not by what came with it.

## 2. The window primitive

A desk window is known to the engine by an id, and the id says what it holds (`lib/desk/windows.ts`):

- **`tab`**: a tab's own id (a UUID). Its page is the tab's native view, or a page the shell draws (home, a note); its home is its row in the sidebar.
- **`file`**: `file:<context item id>`. A document: the shell's own DOM throughout. Its home is the Stack (the Context row).

The engine treats every window alike except where the difference is the point: a window of the shell's own is always *drawn* (it never has a native page, so it covers the pages under it as any drawn window does, and lifts, tilts and flies as itself); main never hears of it (`setDesk` and `setLayout` list tabs only); it goes home to its kind's element in the sidebar (the host's `homeOf`); the pane the desk leaves as is always a tab's (`leave` and the entry at `start` pick tabs); and the host decides what choosing one means (`DeskHost.select` is the browser's for a tab and nothing for a document; `focusWindow` gives a document's content the keyboard). Which documents a desk may show is the host's to say (`setShellWindows`: every file of the group's context with a viewer); until it knows — the context loads after the desk mounts — saved documents come out, and go if they turn out not to be.

The frame — moving, resizing, filling, putting away — is `DeskWindow`'s for every window. What a shell window shows is its kind's (`components/desk/window-kinds.tsx`): the surface hands each window its *subject* (for a document, its context file: `DeskFileSubject`), and `shellWindowParts` gives the frame its title, its page and any controls of its own. A document's title is `DocumentTitle` (the file's glyph, its name, and its viewer's word or its save state) and its page `FileWindow` (loading, saving, conflicts, and an error boundary, so a failing viewer blanks only its own window), which picks a viewer by `fileViewerKind` and loads it on first use. **A new kind of window** is a new id prefix in `lib/desk/windows.ts`, a subject and a case in `shellWindowParts`, a home in the sidebar (the host's `homeOf(kind, id)`), and what the host's `select`/`focusWindow`/`close` mean for it; the engine needs nothing else. **A new kind of file** is a media type in `GROUP_CONTEXT_MEDIA_TYPES` (and the sync record's mirror), a `fileViewerKind` for it, and a viewer (`ViewerProps`: the file's bytes, `onEdit(serialize)` to save, `onDetail` for the frame, `focusSignal`).

## 3. How it works

**The session** (`lib/desk/document-session.ts`, `DocumentSession`; `FileWindow` makes one per window, in an effect, so React's rehearsal unmount in development disposes one and the mount makes the next). It is bound to the file its window opened on, in the group whose context holds it (`fileOf`: a window of the group the desk has just passed from keeps its own file as it goes home). It keeps which version is on show (only the latest load asked for is shown: a slower one that finishes after is dropped), which version the edits are made to, and the edit waiting. Disposed with an edit waiting, it reads the edit at once (the viewer is still there: a parent's cleanups run before its children's) and writes it, keeping it as a draft until the write succeeds; a draft left by a refused write is shown by the file's next session, over the file as it then is, asking if it moved on. Drafts live as long as the shell does.

**The bytes.** A document's file is a context item (`GroupContextFile`); its bytes stay in main's store (`group-context-store.ts`). The window loads them by version (`readGroupFile` → `fileContent`, cached by blob in the shell, `lib/desk/group-files.ts`), with `shown` for what the shell cannot draw itself: a `.doc` as a `.docx` (`textutil`), a HEIC or TIFF as a PNG (`sips`); conversions run on private temporary copies and are kept a while (`document-convert.ts`, `#conversion`). A save (`writeGroupFile` → `writeFile`) names the version it was made to (`baseBlobId`) and is refused as `changed` if the file has moved on, unless forced; it writes the new bytes (content-addressed), points the item at them (`editedAt`), drops the old bytes and their sync register once nothing names them, and tells the sync lane (`group-blob:` for the new bytes, `group-context:` for the item). A `.doc`'s edits arrive as a `.docx` (`as: "docx"`) and are written back as a `.doc`.

**`@pistachio/documents`** (`packages/documents`, pure TypeScript, fflate for zips and xmldom for XML, so main and the shell read files alike and tests run under node): `csv.ts` (read with every row's own text kept; written back row by row), `xlsx.ts` (the workbook: shared and rich strings, styles through the theme and legacy palettes with tints, number formats — General, fixed with required digits, grouped, percent, scientific, currency, dates, times and elapsed durations in either date system — widths, merges, hidden rows and columns, frozen panes; and `xlsxText` for the agent, which walks the cells there are — a single far cell makes a sheet's rectangle enormous — and names a cell past column 64 by its reference), `docx/` (below), `opc.ts` (relationships), `zip.ts` (refuses a file that would inflate past 64 MB a part or 256 MB in all).

**Editing a Word document in place.** `openDocx` reads the body into ProseMirror JSON (`docx/json.ts`): paragraphs, runs of text with their look resolved through the styles (defaults, a paragraph's style chain, a run's character style, its own properties, `docx/styles.ts`), line breaks, pictures, tables of rows and cells, and — shown but not edited — fields (a page number, a cross-reference: from begin to end, one piece), footnote numbers, symbols, drawings and text boxes, content controls (a table of contents), equations, and paragraphs with tracked changes (editing one would silently accept them). Every node names the element it came from by its place in the body read in order (`indexBody`), and each paragraph and table carries a signature of what it held. The viewer's schema (`docx-schema.ts`) is that JSON's; the list markers are decorations counted afresh from the paragraphs' lists on every change (`ListCounter`). `saveDocx` rebuilds the body from the edited JSON over a fresh reading of the original part (the same text, so the same keys): a paragraph or table whose content matches its signature is the original element, untouched — bookmarks, comments and revision ids with it; an edited paragraph keeps its properties, and each run of text the properties of the run it came from (text typed where there was none takes the paragraph mark's), inside the hyperlink it was in; a paragraph split in two is two with the same properties, the section break going with the last, the copy without a paragraph id; kept pieces are the original elements, or copies where they appear twice (a copied picture gets a drawing id of its own); what was taken out is gone. Every other part of the file is the bytes it was. Saves in one editing session are all written over the file as it was opened, so the keys hold. A key pasted from another document is dropped (`docId`).

**Drops from a live page.** A page is a native view: a drag over it is the page's, and the shell hears nothing. The tab's preload listens for a trusted `dragenter` carrying files that the page did not start itself (`preload/tab.ts`), and tells main (`tabFileDrag`); main passes it on only for a desk window's page (`acceptFileDrag`, `DeskPageInput` `"fileDrag"`). The drop zone goes up *armed*: it is a cover over the whole stage (`file-drop`), so every page gives way to its still, the pages' views are taken down, and AppKit hands the drag to the shell's view under the pointer, where the zone takes it. With no drag event over the shell within 1.5 s it goes down again. Over the shell, the zone follows the window's own drag events (in the capture phase), and goes down 450 ms after the last `dragover`, on a drop anywhere, or on `dragend`.

**Mentions** (`lib/desk/mentions.ts`, pure): where a mention being typed starts (`mentionQuery`), which files it offers (`mentionCandidates`), the text with one inserted (`insertMention`), and every mention in a message, by the files' names, the longest first (`mentionsIn`). The field's mentions are lit by a mirror behind the textarea (the same box and type, its text transparent; the textarea on top keeps the caret and selection). On sending, each file mentioned is read in main as the agent reads it (`groupFileForMessage` → `forMessage`) and rides with the message: text in a fenced block (`formatAttachmentText`), a picture or a PDF as an attachment (at most six files a message), else a line naming it and its item id. The menu is drawn beside the Bar, not in it, so its glass blurs the desk (an element with `backdrop-filter` inside another's blurs only its parent), and is a cover (`mentions`).

## 4. The agent

A desk turn's block lists document windows among the windows (`- document file:<id> “name” — box`), and the context's lines say a file opens as `file:<its id>`. `desk_arrange` places, brings out and puts away documents by that id, as it does tabs (`arrangeFor` checks a window is one of the desk's: a group tab, or one of its documents); `desk_note` pins a note to a document's frame; Undo layout puts documents back too. `context_read` reads every kind: a Word document as markdown (`docxText`: headings, list markers, tables), a workbook as each visible sheet's rows, tab-separated with row numbers (`xlsxText`), a `.doc` as the text macOS makes of it, an SVG as its source, a HEIC, TIFF, BMP or AVIF picture as a PNG. A mentioned file comes with the person's message (§1), and `DESK_RULES` say it need not be read again.

## 6. Not done / open questions

- A drag that starts over a live page is taken from the page: a file cannot be dropped into a web page on the desk (an upload, an attachment in a mail). Taking the drop and handing it to the page under the pointer (`Input.dispatchDragEvent` with the file's path) would give that back. That the drag passes from the page's view to the shell's once the page gives way is AppKit's; the e2e drives the page's side with trusted CDP drag events and the shell's with synthetic ones, not a real drag from Finder.
- Word: text is edited; formatting is not (⌘B does nothing), nor are tables, pictures or lists restructured (no new rows, no indenting a list item). A new paragraph after a heading is a heading (it copies the paragraph it split). Headers, footers, footnotes' text, comments and text boxes are not shown; page breaks are a dashed line; the document is one long page. An edited paragraph loses the bookmarks and comment anchors inside it. Word's own rendering (kerning, exact line heights, floating pictures, tab stops) is approximated.
- A `.doc` goes through macOS's converter both ways, which simplifies what it cannot represent. The same for HEIC and TIFF, shown as PNGs.
- Workbooks are not edited. Rows are all one height; frozen panes are not kept frozen (the headers are); theme fonts, borders, conditional formats, charts, comments and hyperlinks are not shown; a date format not in the formatter's small grammar shows its number.
- PDFs: no search, no links or form fields (no annotation layer), no outline; fonts a PDF does not embed are drawn in the system's; JPEG 2000 and JBIG2 images, which need pdf.js's WebAssembly decoders, may not show.
- Markdown: rich editing re-spells what it saves (`*` for `_`, a list's markers) — only once edited; what the notes' shape of a document has no node for (HTML, footnotes) is lost from a file edited in *Document* (edit those in *Markdown*). Images by relative path do not load.
- A text file not in UTF-8 is read-only. Very large text files are one textarea.
- A draft whose write was refused is kept only in memory: quitting the app before the file's window is opened again loses it.
- An older Pistachio on another Mac drops a context item of a kind it does not know (a Word file) when it reads the synced context, and a write from it afterwards takes the item away everywhere.
- A mention is found by the file's name; two files of the same name mention the first.
- Only the person's files are mentionable (not facts, links or tabs).

# The desk

An experiment in a different browser. A tab group can open as a **desk**: its tabs are free windows, dragged, thrown, resized and stacked inside the page area, and the group's tabs sit down the side as icons in a dock, as apps sit in the macOS Dock on the left of the screen. It asks what browsing feels like when a tab is a window you place rather than a button that fills the screen. It ships with switchable **variants**, because the point is to feel the options side by side.

Desktop only (it needs native page views to move). Branch `desk`.

The desk has an agent: the Co-worker in a Bar at the desk's foot, with the group's context as a Stack in the dock — `docs/desk-agent.md`. The Bar's band shrinks the room windows have (`#usable` and `#reach` stop above it).

Windows coming and going can lay the desk out anew: a layout model (Jev) judges from the windows' titles whether to close a gap up, set a new window beside the one it goes with, tile, or give one window the main place, and ⌘⌥L asks outright — `docs/desk-layout.md`.

Not every window is a tab's: a file dropped on the desk opens as a document window of the shell's own, in a viewer for its kind, whose home is the Stack — `docs/desk-documents.md`. The engine knows windows by id (`lib/desk/windows.ts`); what follows says "tab" for a window, and holds for documents except where it concerns a native page.

## 1. What a person does

**Open it.** Hover a tab group's row in the sidebar and press the window button (or right-click the group → *Open as desk*; the top layout's group chip has the same menu item). The sidebar goes first: pinned or compact, it slides away as the compact sidebar does, and the page reflows to fill the row. The macOS window buttons stay where they are, over the top of the dock's column; the dock's shelf keeps clear of them (and of as much at its foot, so it stays centred), and the drop rail starts below them. Then the page in view lifts off into a window without a flicker, and the dock slides in from the left. The group's last arrangement comes back if there is one. The sidebar stays away, with no edge to bring it out, until the desk is left.

**The dock.** A glass shelf down the desk's leading edge, 60px wide, with every tab in the group as an icon:
- *The icon* is the tab as an app: the large icon the site declares for itself (its `apple-touch-icon`, or a declared icon of 96px or more, an SVG counting as any size), full bleed; otherwise its favicon on a tile; the shell's own pages (home, the brief, a note) their own mark; failing all of those, its initial on a tile in a colour of its own. Main reads the app icon from the page's `<link>`s (`src/main/app-icon.ts`, `BrowserTabInfo.appIconUrl`).
- *A dot* beside a tab whose window is out on the desk, as beside a running app; the window in use has a longer one in the group's colour.
- *Hover* an icon: it grows away from the edge, and a preview of the tab (its latest still, title, site, and whether it is on the desk or in the dock) opens beside it, gliding from icon to icon. A page the shell draws (home, a note, the brief) has no still — no view of its own is ever drawn — so its preview is the page itself (`ShellPageMiniature`), laid out at its window's page box (in the dock, a window filling the desk's: `DeskEngine.pageSize`) and scaled to cover the picture's box from its corner, as a still does. It is inert and a preview of itself (`PagePreview`, `components/page-preview.ts`): it takes no pointer or keyboard, and starts or writes nothing that opening the page would — the brief makes no report and files no ticks, a note is read only and never saved, and nothing renames the tab. Press **⇧⌫** while an icon is under the pointer and its tab closes, whether the keyboard is in a page, the shell or neither: a window it has out on the desk goes with it (Reopen closed tab brings it back). Held down, it closes one tab, not one per repeat. The keyboard never sees the key; off the icons, ⇧⌫ is the page's as ever.
- *Click* an icon: a window that is out comes to the top and takes the keyboard. A tab that is not out comes out, flying from its icon, where the desk's layout has room for it (`placeNewWindow`): on an empty desk, a comfortable window in the middle; on a **tiled** desk (every window a tile, lying along two of the desk's edges or a cell of a tiled arrangement, none overlapping), the hole it has left if that is room enough (beside a half, the other half; beside three quarters, the fourth), and with no such hole the window in use splits along its longer side and gives the new one its second half, so the desk stays tiled (one window filling the desk becomes the two halves); among windows set down freely, the size windows come out at, where it covers the least of them.
- *Drag* an icon: it comes away in hand as the icon. Moved up or down the dock, the other icons slide apart to make room for it, the gap opening where it would go (passing an icon's middle moves the gap past it); let go there and the tab takes that place among the group's tabs, in the sidebar and the tab row too, the icon flying into the gap. Let go where it started, nothing changes.
- *Into another group*, as an app is dropped into a folder: carried over another group's icon (below the tabs), that icon swells and rings in its colour, the icon in hand draws in a little, and the tabs close up behind it, as without it. Let go and the tab goes into that group, at the end of its tabs, where the group stands in the sidebar (it does not move to where the tab was); the icon shrinks and fades into the group's, which bounces as it takes it, and the group's pile shows the tab. A window the tab had out on the desk flies into the group's icon too, as a group's windows do when the desk passes from it. Were it the window in use, the window under it is in use instead; with no other window out, the group's tab used last comes out. The group's last tab taken away ends the group, and with it the desk, as ungrouping does.
- *Pulled out*: pulled 24px clear of the dock, the icon becomes the tab's window (the dock's icons go back as they were, its place there faint), held by its title bar near the bar's leading end, so the window reaches out across the desk from the hand. A tab not out comes out at the size windows come out at, growing out of the icon under the pointer. A window already out flies from where it was to the hand, its title bar under the pointer, at its own size, or, too big to carry (a full-height half, the whole desk), scaled down to fit in two thirds of the desk's width and four fifths of its height, its shape kept (`carrySize`). From there it is any window in hand: magnets, edge zones, Shift's snap, a throw, the drop rail. Let go while it is still an icon and nothing changes.
- *Other groups*, below a divider under the tabs: every other tab group in the Space, in the order the sidebar lists them, drawn as the sidebar draws a group (its tabs' icons in a small pile, `FaviconCluster`), made large on a tile tinted with its colour. *Hover* one: a card opens beside it, as a tab's preview does, showing that group's desk drawn small: the desk's dotted ground in the desk's shape, and each window where it would come out if the desk passed to the group now (where it was last left; never on a desk, its tab used last alone in the middle), framed as the Frame variant frames it, bottom to top, the one it would come up on ringed in the group's colour. Each window shows the latest picture of its page (a masked one its region, cropped from a whole-page picture if that is what there is); a shell page shows itself, drawn small as a tab's preview draws it; a sleeping tab or one never pictured shows its app icon. Under the sketch: the group's name, its colour, and how many of its tabs would be out. Hovering also fetches its tabs' pictures, so the sketch fills in and its windows come out as themselves. Clicking one passes the desk to that group (below). *Dragged* up or down, a group's icon moves among the groups, the others making room for it as the tabs do; it stays in the dock (pulled out over the desk, it follows the pointer only a little way), and let go it takes that place, in the sidebar too. A drag is never a click: the desk stays where it is.
- *Right-click* an icon for the sidebar's menu for it (the same builders, `chrome/tab-menu.tsx` and `chrome/tab-group-menu.tsx`, so the dock, the sidebar and the tab strip never drift apart). A tab's icon: first what the desk does with its window (**Open on the desk** for a tab in the dock; **Bring to front** for a window out but not in use; **Minimize** and **Collapse** for one out, or **Expand** and **Collapse** for one minimized), then the tab's own entries: pin, favorite, move to a folder, out of its group, into a new group or another group, duplicate, reader view, ask Pistachio, suspend, force focus, close. There is no split view (a desk lays its windows out by hand), a tab whose window is out cannot be suspended (it is on screen), and **Add to** another group sends the tab as a drop on that group's icon does (its window flies into the icon). **Duplicate** puts the copy beside the tab in the group and brings it out on the desk, in use. Another group's icon: **Rename** (a field on a card beside the icon, the sidebar's own name field; ↵ or leaving it keeps the name, Escape the old one), its colour, **New tab in group** (the desk passes to that group, and the tab comes out there), open as split view (which leaves the desk), **Open as desk** (the desk passes to it in place, out of its icon), ungroup, close. Holding the sidebar's row open has no row here, so that entry is left out. While a menu is up the previews, tooltips and ⇧⌫ stand down and the dock stands.
- *Below another divider*: a new tab (+), and **More** (⋯). The + says what it does in a tooltip beside the dock (shadcn's Base UI Tooltip, `components/ui/tooltip.tsx`), after 350ms, and none shows while the dock is busy or aside. More has no tooltip: the pointer on it brings up its **card** beside the dock, its foot level with the button's (`DockMoreCard`): **Tile the windows**, **Cascade the windows** and **Arrange for me** (the smart layout, `docs/desk-layout.md`), each with its keyboard shortcut (⌘⌥T, ⌘⌥C and ⌘⌥L by default, changed in Settings › Keyboard); **Feel**, the variants (below), with Glide's deceleration under the throw; and **Leave the desk**. The card stays while the pointer is on the button or on it, with 300ms of grace to cross the gap between (and while a press held in it, such as the deceleration slider in hand, lasts), and goes a moment after the pointer leaves both. A click on More pins it up until a press anywhere else (on a live page too) or Escape. Tile, Cascade and Leave put it away as they act; the Feel settings leave it up, so several can be tried in a row. While it is up the dock stands, and the previews, the tooltip and ⇧⌫ stand down.

**Windows.**
- *Move* by the frame (title bar, tab or handle, depending on the frame variant). A press raises the window; a click gives its page the keyboard.
- *Edit the address*: click a window's title (its favicon, title and site, lit on hover) and the address palette opens on that tab, as ⌘L does, and the tab is the one in use. Dragged from the title, the window moves as from anywhere on the bar; a double click there does not fill the desk (the empty part of the bar does). ⌘L opens the same palette on the window in use, wherever the keyboard is.
- *Let go of the whole desk*: a window that fills the desk (90% of it or more both ways) shrinks as soon as it is dragged, the way a maximized window lets go of the screen when its title bar is pulled. It goes back to the size it had before it filled the desk, or else to the size windows come out of the dock at (58% × 74% of the desk), and it springs to that size around the point the pointer holds (a title bar stays under the pointer). Small enough to carry to a half or a quarter. A window spanning the desk only one way lets go of that span alone: a tall one (a left or right half) keeps its width and comes down to 74% of the desk's height; a wide one (a top or bottom half) keeps its height and comes in to 58% of its width (`letGoSize`).
- *Hold Shift to snap* (any window in hand: moved by its frame, grabbed from its page, or dragged out of the dock). The desk reads the pointer in thirds and lights the tile the window will land in: the corners are quarters and the sides halves; the middle column is a comfortable window in the centre, except along its top edge (the top 7% of the desk, `SNAP_TOP_SHARE`), which is the whole desk. A window is carried by its title bar, so through the middle of the desk the pointer is high in the top third: that still lands it centred, and only taking the window up to the top fills the desk (the Snap throw's map, shown before letting go). The tile holds until the pointer is 16px past a line, so it never flickers. Let go and the window springs into the tile, whatever the Throw variant or the pointer's speed; a fling at the dock while aiming does not put it away. Shift can go down or up at any moment of the drag, with the pointer still: the tile lights or goes out at once. While a tile is lit (Shift's, or an edge zone's), a carried window that is drawn thins a little so the tile reads through it.
- *Hold Shift and drag anywhere on a window*, including on its live page. The press never reaches the page. The key is a variant: Shift, Option, Command or off. With Shift (the default) the grab starts in snap mode, since Shift is held; let go of Shift once the window is in hand to place it freely.
- *Stick*: an edge within 14px of the desk's edge, or of a neighbour's edge (beside it with the gap, or flush), snaps onto it and a guide line shows. The dock's edge is one of these edges too, so a window pushed to it still lines up beside the dock.
- *Behind the dock*: the dock floats over the desk, and a window may lie behind it, as a window can slide under the Dock. Moved, thrown or resized, a window may go into the dock's column, as far as the desk's leading edge, and stays where it is let go (carry it by its title bar well along the bar, so the pointer stays clear of the leading edge's zones and the drop rail). Tiles, filling the desk, the arrangements and where a new window comes out still keep clear of the dock. A window behind the dock shows its still under the dock's glass, like a window behind another window (a live page would paint over the dock). The one exception is the window in use. Click into a window behind the dock, or choose its tab from the dock, the sidebar or a shortcut, and the dock slides away, as it does for a carry, so that window's page can be live. The dock comes back when the pointer comes to its place (its column, level with its shelf), when another window is used, or when that window is moved out from behind it; while it is back, the window under it is a still again. A window only let go behind the dock is not chosen by that, so the dock comes back over it.
- *Edge zones*: push the pointer into the desk's left or right edge for that half, the top edge for the whole desk, and the corners for quarters. The zone lights in the group's colour; let go and the window fills it.
- *Throw*: release while moving and what happens depends on the Throw variant (below).
- *Resize* from any edge or corner. Edges stick the same way.
- *Resize a split from its gutter*: where two windows stand side by side (or one above the other) the desk's gap apart, as tiling, a zone or the magnets leave them, the gutter between them is the split's, as between the panes of a split view. The pointer in it finds a resize edge (either window's; they meet there), and dragged, the gutter moves: one window grows as the other shrinks, the gap between them kept and their far edges still. Every window on that gutter moves with it, so nothing comes to overlap or leaves the line: a half beside two stacked quarters takes both quarters along, while the gutter between the quarters is theirs alone. In a grid, an edge moves the gutter of its own row or column; a corner at a crossing (where three or four windows are cornered) moves both gutters, and every window cornered there with them. The gutter goes no further than leaves each window on it its least size (300 × 200), and on the way it sticks to the edges of windows not on it, so a grid's two gutters can be lined up again. It is a split only the desk's gap apart, give or take 2px (`SEAM_SLACK`): flush, overlapping or further apart, each edge is its own window's, as before. Masked and minimized windows are never on a split, nor is a window still settling into its place. The desk resized (the app's window, the Bar or the shelf's band at its foot), windows a gutter apart stay a gutter apart.
- *Double-click* the frame, or press its ⤢ button, to fill the desk and back. (A minimized window, double-clicked, expands.)
- *The dock steps aside*: as soon as a window is carried (moved, grabbed from its page, or dragged out of the dock), the dock's shelf slides off the desk's edge, clipped there so it never passes over the sidebar, and it slides back once the window is let go.
- *The leading edge, read in depth*: so that no two targets overlap, the edge a carried window is pushed into offers three things at three depths. The desk's first 30px (a deeper band than the other edges' 18px) are the split-screen snaps: the left half, with the top-left and bottom-left quarters at the band's ends (96px). Past the desk's edge, in the column the dock slid out of or further out over the sidebar, a **drop rail** stands in the dock's place: the dock's own glass, cut in two by a hairline, **Collapse** above, taking most of the height, and **Close** below, the smaller segment (28% of the height, 120–220px), where the Dock keeps its Trash. The rail slides in from behind the desk's edge once the pointer is within 180px of it. Nothing on it has colour until a release would go somewhere: then that segment fills with its colour (the group's for Collapse, red for Close), the hairline goes, and its mark (a filled badge, a little larger) follows the pointer up and down the segment, so the target is always beside the hand. The segment chosen stays lit as the rail slides away on letting go. Over the rail, a drawn window shrinks and fades into it (the lit segment stands for it) and comes back if the pointer leaves.
- *Collapse*: let go on the Collapse segment, fling the window hard at the desk's edge, or press its − button: it goes to its icon as the dock slides back, and the icon gives a little bounce as it takes it. (A document's goes into the Stack.)
- *Minimize*: press the window's minimize button (the picture-in-picture mark, beside −), or choose Minimize from its icon's menu. The window stays on the desk, small (the least a window may be, 300 × 200) and with its page shown as if zoomed to 50%: the page lays out as it would on a window twice the size and is drawn at half scale, so a responsive site shows its desk layout at a glance rather than its narrowest one (a navbar). It shrinks to the desk's foot and parks there beside the dock, peeking up half its height, the rest below the desk's edge, like a file peeking from a folder; the next window minimized parks to its right, overlapping it by half and over it. While any window is parked, the desk keeps above their band (as it keeps above the Bar's): windows filling the desk, tiles and hand-placed windows stop above it, so nothing is ever under the shelf. The window in use, minimized, passes the keyboard to the window under it.
  - *Hover* a parked window and it rises into full view, over its neighbours and the windows it covers; the others stay down. It goes back down a moment (80ms) after the pointer leaves it. Rising, going down and moving along the shelf are timed motions, not springs, never past their place whatever the Feel's spring: on transitions.dev's motion tokens (its transitions-polish skill, matched by usage), rising is a hover lift in, 250ms; going down is a close, quicker, 150ms; moving along as the shelf closes up is a position change, 250ms; all on the smooth ease-out, `cubic-bezier(0.22, 1, 0.36, 1)` (`EASE_SMOOTH_OUT`, `cubicBezier` in `lib/desk/motion.ts`). The 80ms before going down is the skill's intent gate: long enough that the pointer crossing between the window's frame and its live page is not leaving, and no more. Shrinking into the shelf and growing back out ride the Feel's spring.
  - It is *live* like any window, peeking and raised: its page scrolls, clicks, types. A click lands where the page is drawn.
  - *Expand* (its ⤢ button, a double click on its frame, or its icon in the dock): it grows back to the box it had before it was minimized, in use, its page at its own size again.
  - *Drag* it by its frame (or the grab key anywhere on it) and it comes out of the shelf, still minimized, a small zoomed window anywhere on the desk; the shelf closes up behind it. Let go at the desk's foot and it parks again, at the place in the shelf nearest the pointer. Out on the desk it can be *resized* from any edge or corner, its page still at 50% (a parked one is dragged out first: half of it is below the edge). Magnets and a throw work as for any window.
  - *Snapped* as any window is — an edge zone pushed into, Shift's tile, the Snap throw, the top edge to fill the desk — it lands in the tile as a window at its own size again, its page no longer zoomed (filled, letting go of the desk gives it back the box it had before it was minimized). Over the shelf's band at the desk's foot no tile is lit for it, even in the corner where a window would light the bottom-left quarter: the shelf is where it goes there. Tile and Cascade leave it as it is.
  - A masked window is a picture of part of a page and is not minimized. A shell page (home, a note, the brief) and a document are zoomed out the same way, by the shell.
- *Close*: let go on the Close segment: the window goes into it, then its tab is closed (Reopen closed tab brings it back). A window dragged out of the dock and let go on Close closes that tab too.

**Mask: keep part of a page.** For when only part of a page matters (a chart, a ticker, a player): shrinking the window would not do, because the page is responsive and its content reflows, shrinks or moves.
- *Choose*: the window's crop button (in its frame's controls; a web page only, not the shell's own pages). The page freezes and dims, the cursor becomes a crosshair, and a drag draws the region to keep, as a screenshot's selection is drawn, with its size shown in a corner. Let go and the window becomes that region. A drag under 16px, Escape, or the crop button again calls it off.
- *Masked*: the window is the region, where it was on the page and at the size it was, with the bare frame's handle above it; the rest of the window fades from around it. It stays live and usable: main keeps the page laying out at the box it had, shows only the region, and maps the pointer on it to the page's own point, so a click on a masked video plays or pauses it, hovering shows the player's controls, a drag scrubs, and the keyboard works as it always does. The player's own fullscreen button makes the page itself on the whole screen, and it is masked again when it leaves fullscreen. The handle's controls, shown on hover or while the window is in use, are Unmask and Collapse, and, while the window is in use, Edit mask.
- *Edit mask*: the window's whole page shows around the region, where it lies on the page and at the region's scale, faded so the desk shows through; the region stays at full strength in a white frame with knobs on its corners and edges. Drag an edge or corner to change the region (never off the page, never under 16px a side, its size shown while dragged), or drag inside it to move it over the page. Done (the bar above the page, Enter, or a press anywhere else) and the window becomes the new region, where it lies on the page, at the same scale, the rest fading as when it was first masked; Cancel or Escape and it stays as it was. The page is shown by its still (the whole page, taken as the edit begins), so it is not live meanwhile.
- *Resize* from an edge or corner: the region keeps its shape and scales like a picture, not a page. The page never reflows; the region is re-rendered sharp at the new size once it is let go (while it is dragged, its still stretches). A region may be as small as 16px a side.
- *Move* by its handle, or with the grab key held anywhere on it, as any window. A press on the region is the page's.
- *Kept out of the layouts*: filling the desk, the edge zones, Shift's snap and the Snap throw would stretch the picture, so they leave a masked window alone, as do Tile and Cascade; and a tab brought out of the dock never takes half of a masked window.
- *Unmask*: the whole window grows back around the region, at the page's own scale, the region staying where it is: the window is drawn all the way, its page's picture revealed around the region as the frame grows (nothing in it is stretched), and it is live again once it has landed. The page is never given a size between: it goes on laying out at its own box until its view is shown there. Leaving the desk, the window in use comes back as the whole pane.
- *Kept*: masks are saved with the desk's arrangement (on this device, as the arrangement is). Reopened, a masked window that comes out of the dock comes out masked, where it was and at its size, its region's shape kept whatever the desk's shape is now. A masked window whose tab is the one in view when the desk opens lifts off whole, as any first window does, lands as the window it was cut from, and is cropped back to its region (the rest fading, as when it was first masked) once a still of the page as it then stands is up, then goes where it was left. A masked tab that slept while the desk was away gets its mask back when it wakes.

**Switch group** (another group's icon in the dock, or *Open as desk* on another group while a desk is up): the desk passes to that group in place, without leaving. The group left is saved as it was, and its windows fly into its own icon, which has just appeared among the other groups, shrinking into it; it bounces as the last one lands. The chosen group's windows come out of the icon clicked, to where they were last left on a desk, and the one on top then is in use again. A group never on a desk comes out as its tab used last, alone, a window in the middle, as a first desk does. Chosen again before its windows are home, a group takes them back: they turn round to where they were left. The dock's tabs become the new group's, popping in one after another.

**New tab** (+ at the foot of the dock, or ⌘T while the desk is up): a new tab in the group, on the home page. It joins the dock and comes out onto the desk as the window in use, where the desk's layout has room for it (as a clicked icon does). On a desk ⌘T ignores the General new-tab setting (an address palette, or a set URL), as the + does; so does any other new-tab command there, such as the top layout's tab strip +.

**Arrange** (the More card, or ⌘⌥T and ⌘⌥C wherever the keyboard is, a page's or the shell's): tile every window, or cascade them. With no desk up, the keys do nothing.

**Leave**: Leave the desk, on the More card. The window in use grows back into the whole surface and becomes the pane again, as its live page, already laid out at the pane's size (a quick 250ms, never past the pane); the rest fly home. Then the sidebar slides back in beside the pane (a compact sidebar comes back hidden). Choosing a tab outside the group (the tab switcher, the address palette's open tabs) also leaves the desk, straight away. The group being closed, ungrouped or left in another Space ends it too.

## 2. Variants

Under **Feel** on the More card (the ⋯ at the foot of the dock). Click a row to cycle it; Shift-click goes back. Choices persist on this device.

| Axis | Choices | What changes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Throw | **Glide** · Snap · Free | Glide: momentum that fades (at the Deceleration below); meeting an edge ends the coast in a rebound that settles flush against it. Snap: every release lands in a tile, read from where the throw would carry the pointer, on the desk in thirds (corners → quarters, sides → halves, the middle column's top edge → full, the rest of the middle → a centred window). Free: stays where it is dropped (magnets only). |
| Deceleration | 6–60%, **28%** | Glide's alone (a slider under the throw, dimmed under any other throw): the share of its speed a coasting window loses every 100 ms. 28% is a coast of 0.3 s (the time to lose 63% of its speed, τ = −0.1 s / ln(1 − d)); 60% stops a throw almost where it is let go, 6% sends it across the desk. `glideTauFor` in `lib/desk/motion.ts`; the coast, the rebound it aims at, and the spring that finishes a throw off the desk all use it. The fling that puts a window away keeps its own reading of a throw. |
| Spring | **Snappy** · Bouncy · Smooth · Eased | The spring every settle rides: response 0.26 s / damping 0.86, 0.42 / 0.56, 0.48 / 1. Eased has no spring: every settle and flight is a timed ease on transitions.dev's motion tokens (below). |
| In hand | **Lifted** · Live | Lifted: a carried window shows its still. It lifts (scale 1.035, deeper shadow), tilts with the swing about the point it is held by, shrinks when held over the dock, and may travel anywhere. Live: the page stays live and flat in hand, and stays on the desk. |
| Frame | **Title bar** · Tab · Bare | A title bar above the page; a folder tab on its shoulder; or just the page with a handle pill above it. |
| Grab key | **⇧ Shift** · ⌥ Option · ⌘ Command · Off | The key that grabs a window from anywhere on it. Shift is also the snap key (not a variant), so a Shift grab starts snapping; Option or Command grab without snapping, and Shift added mid-drag snaps. |
| Layout | **Smart** · By hand | Whether a window coming or going may lay the others out anew, as the layout model judges (`docs/desk-layout.md`). By hand, nothing moves on its own; ⌘⌥L still arranges on request. |

**Eased** follows the [transitions.dev](https://transitions.dev) skill (`Jakubantalik/transitions.dev`, installed in `.agents/skills/`: transitions-dev's transitions and transitions-polish's motion tokens and rules, matched by what each motion does, never by the nearest number). Every window motion is a timed ease on the smooth ease-out, `cubic-bezier(0.22, 1, 0.36, 1)` (`EASE_SMOOTH_OUT`), started from wherever the window is when its target is set, whatever set it (`#easeTo`, from `#step`):
- a window come to rest elsewhere at its own size (let go, stuck to an edge, the end of a throw, Undo layout) is a position change, `--duration-fast`, 250ms;
- one whose size changes (filled, tiled, cascaded, snapped, let go of the desk, unmasked, expanded) is the card resize transition, its 300ms;
- out of the dock, growing from its icon, is a dropdown opening from its trigger, 250ms; into the dock, the Close pad or another group's icon, a dropdown closing into it, `--duration-quick`, 150ms — a close quicker than its open, as the polish rules have every close; a flight's scale is eased with its position;
- an arrangement's windows, and a desk's coming out, set off `--duration-stagger` (40ms) apart, the whole run under 300ms (`#beat`);
- nothing overshoots: a throw meeting an edge comes to rest against it rather than rebounding (the coast itself is the Throw's), and what still follows the pointer — a window letting go of the desk in hand, one flying to the hand, the lift and the tilt — settles critically damped (`EASED_SPRING`). The shelf's motions and the leave's grow were already timed on the same tokens, whatever the Spring.
With reduced motion every one of them is instant (the skill's reduced-motion guard is `transition: none`).

Reduced motion replaces every spring with a quick critically damped one and drops the tilt.

## 3. How it works

A desk window is two things drawn by two processes: its **frame** is the shell's DOM, and its **page** is the tab's native view, placed by main over the hole the frame leaves. A native view paints over everything the shell draws, so:

- **Covered windows are drawn by the shell.** A window with anything stacked above it shows its *still* where the page would be, so the stacking is the DOM's and always right. Only uncovered windows go live (`uncoveredWindows` in `lib/desk/geometry.ts`). An armed zone counts as a cover, so the pages under it give way. So does the dock's shelf where it stands (`setDockShelf`, its resting box, whatever its slide), and the drop rail while it shows. So does whatever the dock draws over the desk: the icon in hand, and the preview, the More card (with the gap between it and the dock, so the pointer crossing it is the shell's) and the + button's tooltip, which the dock declares with `setCover(key, rect)` and shows only once the engine lists the key in `clearCovers` (no live page left under it). The tooltip's cover is the band beside the tools, held 200ms past it closing; it is rendered at once but transparent until the band is clear. The preview's cover is the whole band beside the dock, held for as long as an icon is hovered, so moving from icon to icon the preview never waits; a group's card is wider than a tab's preview (312px against 232px), so the band widens while a group is hovered.
- **Transformed windows are drawn too.** A native view cannot scale or rotate, so a lifted, flying or (in Lifted) settling window is its still.
- **A window growing into a larger box is its live page** (`Win.growTo`, `#latchGrowth`, `#keepInGrowth`): filled, snapped into a tile or an edge zone, tiled, arranged, expanded from minimized, grown back into the shelf's band. Before 2026-10-01 it was its still, a picture of the smaller window stretched to the larger, so the page looked zoomed in for the length of the settle, and then laid out anew once there. Now, as it sets out, the engine tells main the box it is growing to (`DeskZoomedPage` at zoom 1), so the page lays out there at once, unseen, and the window grows as itself, revealing the page as it is to be: the view is never larger than that box (one dimension may be shrinking meanwhile), and the window goes no further than where it set out from and where it is going, so the Feel's spring never carries it past (an overshoot stops at the edge, its speed there spent). It comes off the list as it lands, at the box it is already laid out at. A window shrinking keeps its still, scaled down, which never looks zoomed in; a masked or minimized one, or one coming off its mask, have their own ways; one covered on the way is its still, and that still is of the page as it will be (main sizes a zoomed view that is down to its box).
- **A still must be fresh before it stands in.** Each window records when it began wanting one, and only a still requested after that (and painted) may replace the live page. Until then the page stays up. Stills are captured at the start of every gesture for all live windows, so they are ready in time.
- **Live pages stack in the windows' order.** `BrowserLayout.stacked`: main re-adds the shown views bottom to top when the order changes (a raise), so even during the moments above the pages overlap correctly.

The motion is imperative (`components/desk/desk-engine.ts`): rects, springs, the coast and the tilt run per frame outside React, write transforms straight to the elements, and report the live pages' boxes to main (`setLayout`, deduped). React re-renders only when a window is added, raised, becomes drawn or live, or starts or ends a flight. Gestures use the drag layer (`lib/pane-drag.ts`), as pane resizes do, so the pointer is never lost over a page.

**Grabbing a live page** (`@pistachio/shell-contracts/desk`). The shell tells main which views are desk windows and which key grabs (`setDesk`). Main follows the key from every view's key events (`noteDeskKey`), because the mouse events `before-mouse-event` delivers carry no modifiers. A press on a desk page with the key held is taken from the page, the shell hears `onDeskGrab`, and the rest of the press is relayed on the drag-sample channel. Points are anchored to the screen at the press, since the view moves under the pointer. While the key is held, every desk page shows an open hand (a user-origin `!important` cursor rule). A plain press on a desk page makes it the active tab.

**The sidebar is put away while a desk is up** (`layouts/SidebarLayout.tsx`). `toggleDesk` opens with `afterSidebar` in the sidebar layout, so the desk store holds the group as `opening` rather than `groupId`, and nothing mounts yet. The layout treats either as `away`: the column hides, the slot narrows to the compact edge's width (the desk keeps that 10px clearance from the window's edge), and the compact edge and its reveal watch are off. A pinned slot has no width transition of its own (it follows resize drags frame by frame), so `useAwaySlide` gives it the compact clock for the slide away and back. When the slot's width transition ends (at once if it had nothing to narrow, or under reduced motion), `sidebarGone` turns `opening` into `groupId` and the desk mounts on a pane that already fills the row. Leaving needs no ordering: the desk hands the row back to panes (`finishLeave`), `away` goes false, and the sidebar slides back beside them. Main hears it as `ShellState.sidebarAway`, which keeps the compact edge's native watch off; the traffic lights stay (`applyWindowButtons`). The stage then reaches the window's top-left corner, so the engine measures the band the buttons take over the dock's column (`TRAFFIC_LIGHTS_H` less the stage's top, while the stage starts left of `TRAFFIC_LIGHTS_W`) as `DeskView.dockClear`: the dock's clip box is inset by it top and bottom, and `dockDrops` starts the rail below it. While away, a screen share that begins says so as a notice, as with the compact sidebar hidden.

**Entering and leaving are seamless on purpose.** The first window starts as a window whose *page* is exactly the pane's box, so main's view does not move. It shrinks into place only once its still is up. Leaving, the window in use goes back to the pane's box, and the surface goes back to panes only after that window is live there again. It grows there as its live page, not its still: before 2026-09-30 it was its still (a picture of the window at its own, smaller size), stretched to the pane, and then the page laid out anew at the pane's size once it was live there — the page looked zoomed in for most of a second, then flashed blank. Now main lays the page out at the pane's box the moment the leave begins (`DeskZoomedPage` at zoom 1, the zoom minimized windows use, so the view's own size does not matter), and the window grows there live, revealing the page as it is to be laid out, on a timed ease (`LEAVE_GROW_MS`, 250ms on the smooth ease-out) rather than the Feel's spring, which could carry a live page past the pane and over the window's edges. It waits on none of the desk's covers (the More card it was left from, the Bar, the dock: all are going), so it is live from the first frame; its emulation comes off with the desk (`setDesk(null)`), at the box it is already laid out at. A masked window in use grows back whole as when it is unmasked (main's mask release).

**Passing to another group** (`DeskEngine.switchGroup`; the store's `switchTo`; `DockGroups` in `DeskDock.tsx`). One engine runs the whole passing, so both groups' windows can be in flight at once and main hears one layout: the surface is keyed by the desk's `instance` (bumped when a desk is opened afresh), not by the group, and `groupId` changes under it. The click keeps where the icon stood (`chooseGroup`), since the dock re-renders without it; the surface's layout effect then saves the old group under its own id (the host's `shownGroup` moves on only after), marks its windows `#departing`, and brings the new group's out with `#flyingIn(…, from)`. A departing window that is live wants a still like any window in motion, and flies (`#sendAway` to the group's icon, `#departFor`) once it is drawn, or after 400ms; so it flies as what it showed. Meanwhile it is not the group's any more: `syncTabs` leaves departing and homebound windows alone, `hasLivePage` looks in every tab, the surface finds the tab for a window outside the group (`strays`), and main's desk state lists the new group's windows first, capped at `MAX_DESK_WINDOWS` (`isDeskState` refuses more). The old group's tab stays active until the new one's is selected (`opener`), so the surface does not leave. Where a group's windows come out is one routine (`#laidOut`: saved boxes in the desk as it is now, the entry added and put on top, capped at `MAX_DESK_WINDOWS`), used by `start`, `switchGroup` and `sketchGroup`, the last drawing a group's card (`DockGroupCard`); the entry a passed-to group comes up on is `passedEntry` (`lib/desk/store.ts`), shared by the surface and the card, so the card shows exactly what a click would bring out. A window of the group come back to that is still departing or flying home is taken back (`#takeBack`: its flight turned round to its saved box), never made anew; and a window flying home counts as leaving in main's desk state, so the incoming group's masks go first. The phase is `"entering"` until everything has landed; then the new group is saved. The mask editor's whole-page picture is asked for no larger than `MAX_DESK_STILL_WIDTH` on its longer side, its shape kept: a small region shown large would otherwise ask for a page wider than `isDeskState` accepts, and main would drop the whole desk state.

**Rearranging the dock** (`#readDock`, `#aimDock`, `#dropInDock` in the engine; `dockShift`, `DockGroups` in `DeskDock.tsx`; `lib/desk/dock-order.ts`). An icon's drag is the `"icon"` gesture either way: a tab's (as before, turning into its window once pulled clear), or another group's (`pressGroup`; the gesture's `tabId` is empty, and it never becomes a window). At the press the engine reads the icon's section as it stands, each icon's resting box top to bottom as if the section were scrolled to the top (so a scroll during the drag moves nothing it is read against), and, for a tab, the other groups' icons. Each move, the icon in hand's middle is read against those middles: the place it would go is how many of the others it is past (`to`, counted without it, as main counts `addTab`'s index), or, a tab's over a group's icon grown by 4px, `into` that group. The view says so (`DeskView.dockDrag`: the section's order, `to`, `into`, the pitch), and the dock translates each icon by a pitch (`dockShift`: CSS `translate`, transitioned only while `data-reordering` is on the section, so the drop that re-orders the list and takes the room away in one render has nothing to animate). The icon in hand's own item goes to the gap, unseen. Let go, the order is the browser's to keep: a tab moved among the group's tabs is `addTab` into its own group at `to`; a tab let go on a group is `addTab` into that group (after the tab in use has moved on: the host selects the window left on top, or the group's tab used last, first, so the desk never stands on a tab not its own); a group moved among the groups is `move` at the row-unit index that puts it before the group after it in the dock, or straight after the one before (`groupMoveIndex`). The dock shows the order the drop made at once (`DeskView.dockSettle`, applied by `settledOrder`) until the browser's says the same (the tabs: when `syncTabs` brings it; the groups: 1.5 s), so nothing flickers back while main answers. The ghost then flies to its place (or into the group's icon, shrinking and fading) on a CSS transition, aimed on the frame after the drop so that it goes where the icon is once the dock has been drawn without the tab (the shelf is shorter, and centred again); its place in the dock stays faint until it lands (`#landing`). The groups are listed in the row's order (`groupsInRowOrder`), not the order main keeps them in. Main's `addToTabGroup` sets a tab it brings in from elsewhere down beside the group's tabs before the row is regrouped: a group sits where its first tab sits in the row (`groupedTabOrder`), so a tab from a group above would otherwise have pulled the group up to where it was (this held for every way of adding a tab to a group, the sidebar's drag and Tidy's included).

**The dock's menus** (`openTabMenu`, `openGroupMenu`, `DockRename` in `DeskDock.tsx`). A menu is the shell's context menu (`useContextMenu`), a shell overlay: main takes the live pages down for it and the desk paints the pictures main took of them. Such a picture is the whole page (`stillShows: "page"`) whether or not the engine had a still of its own; before, the window in use, live since it landed and so with no still of the engine's, showed blank under a menu or the address palette. The shared builders take a `desk` option (`TabMenuOptions.desk`: whether a tab is out, and how it goes to another group, the engine's `moveTabToGroup`; the group menu's `desk.open`, the engine's `chooseGroup`). The rename card is a cover (`"rename"`), shown once no live page is under it; it holds the dock and silences ⇧⌫ while it is up, and has the keyboard because main hands the shell the keyboard while an overlay is up and leaves it there when it comes down. A tab can leave the group in use and stay open in more ways than a drop now (pinned, made a favorite, taken out of its group, put in a new one): the surface never leaves the desk on such a tab, but has another of the group's tabs used instead (the window left on top, or the tab used last, which comes out), unless the desk has just passed to another group, which chooses its own. Main opens a duplicate beside its tab, in the tab's group, before it is shown (`#createManagedTab`'s `beside`): it used to be set down after its page had loaded, and a copy of a group's last tab never joined the group, so on a desk the copy came up as a tab outside the group and the desk left (this holds everywhere a tab is duplicated, the sidebar included).

**The dock and the window in use behind it** (`#yielding`, `DeskView.dockAside`). The engine keeps two boxes: `#usable`, the desk beside the dock, where layouts go, and `#reach`, the whole stage, where a window may be (moves, coasts, rests, resizes, the Live variant's rubber band). The window the person chooses (a click into it, its icon, an outside selection that `activeChanged` sees as not the desk's own raise) is `#asideFor`; while that window is the one in use, has a live page and overlaps the shelf, the dock steps aside. The shelf's cover stays up for the 240ms the shelf takes to slide away, so the page never paints over it on the way. Coming back runs the other way round: the cover goes up, the page gives way to its still, and only then (`dockAside` false) does the shelf slide in. The dock stands while the pointer is at its place (the shell's own pointer events on the stage, `notePointer`) or its More card is up (`holdDock`). Over the live page the shell hears no pointer, so while the dock is aside the shell tells main where its place is (`DeskState.dock`), and main, which sees every mouse move on a desk page (`before-mouse-event`), says once when the pointer comes there (`DeskPageInput` `"dock"`). The same goes for the keyboard: it is as often a page's as the shell's, so while a tab's icon is hovered (not while the dock is busy or aside) the dock says so (`setDockHover` → `DeskState.dockHover`), and main takes ⇧⌫ (`isDockCloseKey`) from whichever view it is struck in, in its relay of every view's keys after the tab switcher's (`takeDeskDockKey`), and tells the shell (`DeskPageInput` `"close"`); the dock closes the hovered tab through the store's `closeTab` (which asks first if the agent's task is using it). A still asked for too soon after the last one (`STILL_RETRY_MS`) schedules a render for when it may be asked (`#renderIn`), and a window that has just begun to want a still asks at once, so a cover never waits for good on a still nobody asks for.

**Minimized windows** (`minimize`, `expand`, `hoverMini` in the engine; `Win.mini`; `DeskZoomedPage` and `DeskHover` in `@pistachio/shell-contracts/desk`; main's "Desk zooms" section).
- *The zoom is Chromium's device emulation, Electron's own* (`webContents.enableDeviceEmulation`: `viewSize` the window's page box over `DESK_MINI_ZOOM`, `scale` the zoom), not the zoom factor, which Chromium keeps per site (⌘+ on one tab zooms every tab of that site), and not the debugger's `Emulation.setDeviceMetricsOverride`, which would hold a debugger session (forced focus and masks share it). Probed in Electron 43 (a standalone app): the page lays out at the emulated size and its media queries follow; input on the view is mapped to the page by Chromium itself (a click at view (100, 50) lands on the page at (200, 100)), unlike a mask's viewport override, so nothing is re-sent; a view cut shorter than the page box changes nothing the page can see (no resize, the same `innerWidth`/`innerHeight`), and a click in it is still mapped; another tab of the same site is untouched. A document loaded afresh in another renderer does not carry the emulation over, so main sets it again on every `did-navigate`. (Synthetic wheel events do not scroll any page there, so the wheel was not probed.)
- *The shell tells main* each minimized tab's page box and zoom (`DeskState.zoomed`), by where it is headed, reported before the layout as masks are. Main sets the emulation at once, while the window is drawn for its shrink, so the page lays out at its new size unseen. Growing back (`miniMotion "out"`), the window is listed at zoom 1 with the box it grows to, so its page lays out once, there, and comes off the list (and the emulation off) as it lands. A zoomed page in fullscreen has its emulation off until it comes back (as a mask's override). The off-screen viewport main gives a page read before it was ever drawn (`#ensureViewport`) is also device emulation: a zoomed page never takes it, and a release of it sets the zoom again.
- *The shelf* (`#parked`, `#layShelf`, `#shelfRect`): the parked windows left to right, each `MINI_SIZE.w / 2` after the last (closer when they would run past the desk), at `height − h/2`, or raised, `height − h − gap`. They are the top of the stack (`#stackShelf`, kept by `#raise`), the raised one over all. The band the desk keeps above (`#footBand`: the Bar's, or while any is parked, `h/2 + gap`) is part of `#usable` and `#reach`, and a change to it springs the windows into the desk as it is now (`#reband`, the Bar's own path); a minimized window out on the desk keeps its size as a masked one does.
- *Peeking live.* A parked window's box runs past the desk's edge, and peeks up from the window's own edge, below the desk's gutter (`#underStage`: how far the surface runs below the stage), so nothing shows between it and the edge. Its element is clipped there (`clip-path`, written with its box; the stage itself is not clipped, which would flatten the dock's and the Bar's glass), and the view the engine reports is cut short at the edge: main shows the top of the zoomed page. Rising and going down, a parked window stays live (no still is wanted for that motion; its page never changes size); shrinking into the shelf and growing back it is drawn.
- *Hover.* The shell hears the pointer on a window's frame, and on its page while the page is drawn; over a live page it hears nothing, so main follows its mouse hook (`before-mouse-event`) for the zoomed pages and says when the pointer comes onto one and leaves (`onDeskHover`, once each way). The engine keeps both sources for the hovered window and lowers it only once both have let go for `MINI_LOWER_MS`, so crossing from its frame to its page is not leaving.
- *Stills.* A still is the page as its view shows it, drawn at the page's own shape from the top (`height: auto`), never stretched to the window: one taken while it peeked is its top half. Down (drawn), main sizes a zoomed view to its whole page box for its still, and captures it as a mask's (`#captureAtViewSize`, a frame apart until it is at the view's size); the emulation draws the page into the corner of whatever the view's surface is, so a capture from a surface not yet resized is cropped to the page box (`zoomedStill`). Seen before that crop: a covered window's still showed its page a quarter the size, in its corner.
- *Snapping* (`#placeCarried`, `#gestureEnd`, `#overShelf`): a minimized window in hand lights zones and Shift's tiles as any window (a masked one still does not); let go in one, it is unminimized (`#unminimize`, so its page is held at the tile's box as it grows there) and sent to the tile. Below the line the desk keeps above with it parked (`#reachFor` one more parked), no zone is read for it, and a release parks it.
- *Kept*: `SavedDeskWindow.mini` (the box it grows back to, and whether it is parked). A desk reopened brings its minimized windows back minimized, parked in the saved order; the window it opens on lifts off at its own size. Undo layout puts back whether each was minimized (`DeskLayoutSnapshot`); the agent sees `minimized` on each window, and placing one gives it its size back.

**Masks** (`DeskMask` in `@pistachio/shell-contracts/desk`; the engine's `startMask`, `applyMask`, `unmask`, `editMask`; `MaskSelector` and `MaskEditor` in `DeskWindow.tsx`; main's desk-mask section). Chromium can show part of a page without the page knowing: `Emulation.setDeviceMetricsOverride` over the tab's debugger session holds the page's layout at a given size (the box it had when masked), and its `viewport` draws a region of the document into the view's top-left at a page scale. So a masked window's view is just the region's box, its page still lays out at its old width (no responsive reflow), and a resize changes only the scale, which Chromium re-rasterizes (sharp, unlike a stretched bitmap). What had to be learned by trying it in Electron 43 (a standalone probe):
- The viewport's offset is in document px, and Chromium paints only what lies inside the page's own viewport: a page scrolled away from the region shows blank. So the region is kept as a part of the page's BOX, and re-aimed by the page's scroll, which a script in an isolated world reports through `Runtime.addBinding` (the page cannot see the binding), installed for new documents with `Page.addScriptToEvaluateOnNewDocument` (a reload re-reports) and for the current one in a world from `Page.createIsolatedWorld`. Offsets are the shell's px (DIP); the page's scroll is CSS px, times the zoom factor.
- Input is not mapped: a click on the view lands where it would on the unmasked page. So main maps it (`#forwardMaskedMouse`): every mouse event on a masked view that goes on to the page (after the desk's own grab key has had its say) is stopped and re-sent with `sendInputEvent` at the page's point, the region's corner plus the point on the view over the scale. Re-sent events are delivered at points outside the view's (smaller) box, and `before-mouse-event` sees them synchronously, so a flag lets them through. Those events carry no modifiers, so the keys down (followed in `noteDeskKey`) and the buttons held are put back on them: Shift- and ⌘-clicks stay those, and a move with a button held stays a drag. The wheel never passes `before-mouse-event`; the compositor scrolls what is under the pointer as drawn, which is right. A page gone fullscreen has its override taken off until it comes back (`#suspendDeskMask`).
- `capturePage` of a masked view, shown or hidden, is the region at the view's size. Main sizes a masked view that is down to the size its window shows it at (`DeskState.masks[].width/height`), so its still is right, and tags stills with the mask they show (`PaneStill.mask`, `deskMaskKey`). The engine keeps which mask each still shows: a still of the whole page (taken before masking) is cropped to the region with CSS; one of another mask stands for nothing.
- Order matters, or the page shows at the wrong scale, or reflows at the region's size, for a frame: the shell reports the desk (with masks) before the layout; main places a masked view only once the override for its size is in place (`#placeMaskedView`; the window's still, painted under every live page, shows meanwhile); taking a mask off, main takes the view down at its old box and keeps the page at that box until the view is shown there (below); a still waits for any of that (`#deskMaskWork`).
- Taking a mask off (`#clearDeskMask`) does not take the override off. Its window grows back through every size between the region and the page, and a window waiting for a still stays live meanwhile, so main would have placed the plain view at each of those sizes and the page would have laid out at every one (a recorded run: 550×289, 562×303, …, 884×686, media queries flipping). Now the override stays, set to the page's own box with no region (`DeskMaskRelease`), until the view is shown at that box, and comes off a frame later (`#settleDeskRelease`, `#endDeskRelease`); shown at another size, it comes off once that size has held 250 ms (the window really was resized). The engine draws an unmasking window from its first frame (`Win.unmasking`, forced drawn, only a whole-page still counts as fresh) and places its still at the page's place with `--reveal-*` properties written each frame (`#revealBox`: from the region's box at its scale to the page's landing box at its own, as far as the window has grown), so the region stays put. A capture of a masked or releasing page is taken again a frame apart (up to 8 times) until it comes at the view's size (`#captureAtViewSize`): a view resized while down draws at its new size a frame or so later, and a still taken before is the old box's picture at the new scale (the Edit mask glitch, a zoomed crop of the page's top-left, 2026-09-29). Every override main sends for a mask carries `dontSetVisibleSize: true` (`deskMaskMetrics`): otherwise Chromium notes the view's size when an override begins and puts that size back when it is cleared, and a mask set up again after its page left fullscreen came off with the page laid out a screen wide (probed: cleared, a 1200-wide start came back as 1200 though the view was 800; with the flag it stays 800, and the masked picture is unchanged).
- The page must never learn it is masked. The first override sent WITH a `viewport` lays the page out at the viewport's size for a frame (a `resize` to the region, media queries flipping) before `width`/`height` take hold, and a responsive app (Notion) reflows for good: its sidebar, wrapping and scroll change, so the region shows other content. Main turns the override on at the page's own box first, with no viewport (nothing changes), and only then sends the region (`#aimDeskMask`, whenever no override is in place: a new mask, a woken tab, back from fullscreen). Probed: then no `resize`, no media-query change and no `ResizeObserver` report — nor when the region, its scale or the view's size change after. `screen.availHeight` still reads the screen's full height under emulation (no layout depends on it). The e2e test "a mask never lets its page see a resize" records all four from inside the page.
- Editing a mask: the engine reports the window's mask as the WHOLE page box (`x = y = 0`, the page's size) at the size the editor shows it, so main re-aims the same override at all of the page (the same box, so the page sees nothing) and its still is the whole page; `stillShows` takes a still of the whole-page mask for the page. A mask that changes but keeps its page box is re-aimed where it stands (`#syncDeskMasks`), never taken off and set up again, and the override's aim carries the mask's key, so a view is shown, and a still tagged, only for the mask actually in place. The editor's page and bar are a cover (`maskedit`), shown once no live page is under them.
- The session is shared with forced focus (`debugger-hold.ts`): each holds it, and it is detached only when neither does.
- Kept across desks: `SavedDeskWindow.mask` (checked with `isDeskMask` on reading; a bad one leaves the window whole). The engine's `start` gives a window from the dock its mask at once, and asks for the dock's stills only after the first report has told main of the masks, so they come back as the region. The first window carries the mask it is to get back (`maskWanted`): landed whole, it asks for a still, and `#putMaskBack` masks it once that still is painted (or after 1.5 s without one) and sends it where it was saved. A sleeping tab's page is a new one when it wakes, so main drops the mask state of the old page and sets up the new one (`#syncDeskMasks` from the wake).

**From icon to window** (`pressIcon`, `#beginIconDrag`, `#takeInHand`). A press on an icon is a click until it travels 4px; then the gesture is `"icon"`: the engine moves a ghost of the icon (`attachGhost`) and nothing on the desk changes. The pointer passing 24px beyond the dock's edge turns the gesture into a window in hand. A tab not out gets a new window, `"spawn"` (drawn throughout, and sent back into the dock if let go over it), whose scale starts at the icon's width and springs up with the lift. A window already out becomes a `"move"`, and a `lag` (its offset from where the hand holds it) springs to zero, so it flies to the hand; magnets wait until it arrives. Either way the hand holds the bar at `pinTop` (half the bar's height), at `clamp(pointer − desk's left, 28, width / 2)` along it.

**The drop rail** (`dockDrops`, `dockDropAt` in `lib/desk/geometry.ts`; `DropRail` in `DeskDock.tsx`). The engine lays the two segments out from the stage's height (`view.drops`), and while a window is carried it tells the dock whether the rail should show (`dropsShown`) and which segment a release would go to (`dockDrop`); it writes the pointer's height to the rail as `--pointer-y` for the lit mark to follow, without a render. The shelf and the rail slide behind the desk's edge by an `overflow-x: clip` box, not a `clip-path`: an element under a clip-path has nothing behind it for `backdrop-filter` to blur, and the glass goes flat. The dock hides its shelf whenever the view's gesture is a carry (`move` or `spawn`). A window put away flies to where its icon rests, not where it is while the shelf slides (`#iconRect` subtracts the shelf's current transform), so it meets the icon as the dock comes back. Closing is the host's `close` (the store's `closeTab`), called once the window has gone into the Close segment.

**Splits** (`seamsAt`, `seamTravel`, `alongSeam` in `lib/desk/geometry.ts`; `#jointAt` and `#placeResized` in the engine; `Gesture.joint`). A desk has no tree of panes, only free windows, so a split is read from where they are, at the press: the windows that meet across the gutter beside the edge in hand are the desk's gap apart (within `SEAM_SLACK`) and run side by side for some of the way (more than a pixel), and the pointer is along the stretch they share (give or take the gutter's width, so a press at a corner counts). From that pair the seam spreads to every window meeting one already on it across the same gutter, on either side, at the same line. A corner takes hold of both its gutters if each is a seam, and where they cross each seam takes in the windows of the other cornered there (an edge of theirs on its line), and spreads again, until neither grows: so the four corners at a grid's crossing are the same press. The seams and every window on them, as they stood at the press, ride on the gesture; each frame the pointer's travel along each seam's axis is clamped to what leaves every window on it its least size (never less than now, for one smaller already), stuck to the edges of the windows not on it (an edge lined up with theirs, or a gutter from them, within the magnets' 14px), and applied to them all: those before the gutter end that much further on, those after begin there. An edge of a corner with no seam its way is the window's own, resized and stuck as any. Windows on a seam are stopped where they are at the press (a settling one is not on it). All of them are live through the drag (only the edge's own window is raised), each laid out by main at its new box every frame, and saved when it is let go. A stage resize or a change in the desk's foot band carries windows to the new desk with `rescaleRect`, which counts each window with the gutter after it, so a gutter stays the desk's gap; a desk reopened at another size is denormalized from its saved fractions as before, its gutters scaled with it.

**Shift, the snap key.** The engine keeps whether Shift is down from every source that knows: a press's own event, each pointer sample (the drag layer's and the shell's pointer events say; `DragSample.shift`), the shell's own key events, and main, which follows Shift from every view's keys (`noteDeskKey`, the same relay as the grab key) and tells the shell when it changes while a desk is up (`onDeskShift`). The key events are what make a Shift pressed or let go with the pointer standing still count at once. A sample's reading is passed on once (`lib/pane-drag.ts`), never repeated, so a stale one cannot outvote a later key event. While the drag layer holds the pointer, main's double-Shift detector stands down, so trying Shift twice mid-drag never bookmarks the page.

Main also keeps desk windows from being suspended as idle (`suspendTab` skips any tab in the layout).

State: which group has its desk up, the variants, and each group's arrangement (as fractions of the desk, bottom to top, each masked window with its `DeskMask`). These are the shell's, in `lib/desk/store.ts` and this device's localStorage (`pistachio.desk.v1`). Main never stores an arrangement.

## 5. Not done / open questions

- The feel numbers (coast τ, restitution, magnet reach, lift, tilt, spring presets) are first guesses, tuned against screenshots rather than a hand on a trackpad.
- **Live** in hand: the frame (DOM) and the page (native) are separate compositors, so during a fast drag the title bar may run a frame ahead of the page. Not measured.
- A covered window's still is refreshed every 3 s; it is not a live page.
- A tab dragged from the sidebar onto the desk still offers the old split zones; the desk does not take sidebar drags yet.
- Split groups among the desk's tabs are ignored while the desk is up (they come back on leaving).
- With Shift as the grab key, a desk page loses Shift-click (extend selection, open in new window), and every page grab starts in snap mode. Option or Command avoid both.
- Splits: a gutter is a split only while its windows are the desk's gap apart, give or take 2px. A desk reopened at a size other than the one it was left at scales its saved arrangement, gutters with it (saved as fractions of the desk, `normalizeRect`): from a much smaller or larger window (beyond about a quarter either way) the gutters are no longer the desk's gap and stop being splits until the windows are tiled again or moved against each other. Nothing marks a split before it is dragged (the cursor is the resize cursor either way), there is no way to resize just one of two split windows from the gutter (its far edge, or moving it off the gutter, does), and the gutter does not stick at halves or thirds of the desk.
- Shift snaps moves only. A resize with Shift held is a plain resize (no snapping of edges to fractions of the desk, no kept aspect ratio).
- The drop rail only takes a carried window; a tab dragged from the sidebar has nowhere on it to go. Flinging a window at the leading edge minimizes it; there is no fling to close.
- Dragged by its title bar, only a window spanning the desk (both ways, or its whole height or width) lets go of its size; a quarter or a free window keeps it. A tall or wide half lets go to a fixed share of the desk, not to a size it had before it was tiled (no size is kept for a half). (Taken by its icon, a window too big to carry comes scaled down, `carrySize`.)
- Keyboard: Tile and Cascade have shortcuts (⌘⌥T, ⌘⌥C); there are none yet for snapping, cycling windows, or leaving the desk (its way out is on the More card). The address palette does not list Tile or Cascade. An icon in the dock takes Enter or Space (bring out, or to the top); there is no arrowing between icons. ⇧⌫ closes a tab by its icon only under the pointer, not a keyboard-focused icon. With the pointer resting on an icon (after clicking one, say), ⇧⌫ typed into a page closes that tab rather than deleting. After a close the shelf re-centres, so the icons close in from both sides, and the pointer may rest between two of them.
- The dock does not magnify its neighbours as the Dock can (only the hovered icon grows). With more tabs than fit its height, the icons scroll; a drag near the section's top or bottom does not scroll it, so a far place in a long, scrolled section is reached in two drags.
- Menus: the desk's own group has no icon in the dock, so no menu there; only one icon's tab is acted on (no multiple selection). While a group's name is edited on its card the page under the card is a still.
- Rearranging: a tab goes into another group at the end of its tabs (no place among them is chosen from the dock), and there is no dragging a group into another (merging them), a group's icon onto the desk, or a window (rather than its icon) onto a group's icon. Nothing is rearranged from the keyboard. With the pointer at rest on a group's icon after a drop, its card shows again, as after any hover.
- A drag's native layer relays where the real pointer is: in e2e runs, whose pointer is Playwright's, a real cursor resting over the window pulls a drag to it (an icon comes out as a window; the first desk spec's Shift grab of a page never lit its tile, a long-standing flake). The first desk spec, the rearranging one and the one with a window behind the dock call `clearOfCursor` first, which moves the window off the real cursor (the last failed every time with a real cursor resting at the dock's place: the dock came back over the window it had stepped aside for); the others do not yet.
- App icons: only what the page declares in `<link>`s. A web app manifest's icons are not read, nor the conventional `/apple-touch-icon.png` a site serves without declaring it. An `http:` site's icons do not show at all (the shell loads images over https only), as with its favicon elsewhere.
- Where a clicked tab goes is read once, when it comes out; nothing re-tiles the desk around it afterwards.
- Behind the dock, a window not in use is a still, refreshed every 3 s like any covered window. The window in use can never be seen live with the dock over it: the dock steps aside instead. A native view cannot be clipped to show a page with the dock over part of it.
- The pointer brings the dock back only at the dock's place, level with its shelf. Pushing into the leading edge above or below the shelf does not. A window let go behind the dock is the window in use, but it takes the keyboard only once it is chosen (clicked into).
- A mask put back shows the same part of the page's box as before, whatever the page shows there now (a page that changed its layout, or a tab that has gone to another page).
- On a masked page, what the page positions from its own idea of where an element is lands off the region: a `<select>`'s native popup, and a page's own JS wheel handling (a map zooming about the pointer) sees an unmapped point. Clicks, hovers, drags, the wheel's scrolling and the keyboard are right. Scrolling the page scrolls it under the region, as it would in the whole window.
- The region is the same part of the page's box, re-aimed as the page scrolls. A page zoomed (⌘+) while masked is not followed, and neither is a page whose layout moves the content under the region.
- Editing a mask shows the page as a still: it is not live while the region is adjusted.
- Minimized: scrolling a zoomed page with the wheel or a trackpad is not covered by the tests (synthetic wheel events scroll no page in these runs); Chromium maps the emulated view's input itself, clicks included. Only the shelf's top window is live where it peeks: one overlapped by the next, or under the Bar, is its still (refreshed every 3 s like any covered window). The shelf is one row from beside the dock, however many are parked (they close up to fit, not wrap), and it may run under the Bar, which covers them there. A window collapsed from minimized comes back out of the dock at its own size. There is no keyboard shortcut to minimize, and the agent sees minimized windows but does not minimize them itself. Shrinking into the shelf, the window shows the still it had; its page is already laid out at the zoom by the time it lands, and comes up so.
- Unmasking shows only the region, with the page area around it blank, until a still of the whole page arrives (a capture, ~100 ms); on the first frames the new title bar covers the top few pixels of the region.
- With the sidebar away, nothing but the More card's Leave the desk (or something that chooses a tab outside the group, such as the tab switcher) leaves the desk; ⌘T no longer does (it makes a window on the desk). A screen share that was already running shows no indicator while the sidebar is away (only one that begins meanwhile says so). In the top layout nothing is put away.
- Opening waits for the sidebar's slide (180ms) before the desk enters.
- Stepping aside, the dock waits 240ms for its shelf to slide away before the page goes live, so the keyboard reaches a window chosen behind the dock that much later.

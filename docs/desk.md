# The desk

(Until 2026-10-09 the UI called a space a "tab group", and a Profile a "Space"; identifiers keep the old words.)

An experiment in a different browser, now the desktop browser itself. A space (`docs/spaces.md`) is shown as its **desk**: its tabs are free windows, dragged, thrown, resized and stacked inside the page area, and the sidebar beside them is its dock: the space's tabs are its rows, each marked while its window is out as an app is in the macOS Dock, and it narrows to a rail of icons so the desk has the row. It asks what browsing feels like when a tab is a window you place rather than a button that fills the screen. It ships with switchable **variants**, because the point is to feel the options side by side.

Desktop only (it needs native page views to move). Branch `desk`; since 2026-10-09 (branch `spaces`) the desk is always up, `docs/spaces.md`.

The desk has an agent: the Co-worker in a Bar at the desk's foot, with the space's context as a Stack, the last row under its tabs in the sidebar — `docs/desk-agent.md`. The Bar is a nub in the desk's trailing foot corner, a droplet of the shell's ground over the windows there (see *The foot* below), out of which its menu, its pill and its answer come: the windows have the whole desk.

Windows coming and going can lay the desk out anew: a layout model (Jev) judges from the windows' titles whether to close a gap up, set a new window beside the one it goes with, tile, or give one window the main place, and ⌘⌥L asks outright — `docs/desk-layout.md`.

Not every window is a tab's: a file dropped on the desk opens as a document window of the shell's own, in a viewer for its kind, whose home is the Stack — `docs/desk-documents.md`. The engine knows windows by id (`lib/desk/windows.ts`); what follows says "tab" for a window, and holds for documents except where it concerns a native page.

## 1. What a person does

**Open it** (until 2026-10-09). A space's row had a window button, its menu *Open as desk*, and **⌥⌘\\** (Toggle desk) opened the desk of the space in use: the sidebar narrowed to its rail first, the page reflowed, and the page in view lifted off into a window, the space's last arrangement coming back. Since 2026-10-09 there is nothing to open: the app opens on the current space's desk, its windows where they were left, masked and minimized ones as they were, with no lift-off, and an empty space shows its name, New tab and *drop files here* (`DeskEmpty`) — `docs/spaces.md` §1, §2 *The desk follows main*. The desk is a soft well in the page area: the shell's ground barely pressed in (a soft shade inside its top and leading edges, a light inside the others, rounded corners), set off from the sidebar and the window's edges; its windows may fill it to its edges, as a page fills its pane. The macOS window buttons stay in the whole sidebar's toolbar; while the sidebar is its rail they are hidden (they would hang over the desk's corner), and the rail's head starts at the top in their place.

**The sidebar, the desk's dock.** The column beside the desk is the sidebar itself — its favorites, its pinned pages and folders, the day's tabs and every space, the address and the footer — drawn by the same components as ever (`layouts/SidebarLayout.tsx`'s `SidebarColumn`, `components/SidebarChrome.tsx`), whole or as its rail:
- *The rail* (⌘S on a desk, or the button at its head; kept on this device): the same column 48px wide, every row its icon alone, centred and a size up (20px favicons); what a row says beside its icon (its title, counts, controls) is in its tooltip. The tab in use has no card behind it there: its mark at the row's edge says it. A section's header is a hairline (three dots while the section is folded), the address a search button, the footer its menu button, background media is the desk's now playing (below) rather than the stack's cards, and a screen share's card is not drawn (one that begins says so as a notice). A space is a capsule of its colour, its mark above its tabs': a tile of its colour with its first four tabs' icons in a 2×2 and how many tabs it holds on the corner, as the favorites folder's (the same mark the whole sidebar's space row has, there without the tile and the count). Its sections clip only their height as they fold, so the count can stand past the tile's corner, and the rail's list has no scrollbar. The rail's head holds the desk's two buttons, under the window's: the whole sidebar back, and the desk's card. Renaming a space or a folder on the rail brings the whole sidebar back to edit the name in. In the whole sidebar the two buttons sit in its toolbar, where the pin button is otherwise.
- *Since 2026-10-09 the rail is a mode of the sidebar's own* (`layout.sidebar: "whole" | "rail" | "hidden"`, a setting, docs/spaces.md §3), not the desk's: ⌘S cycles whole → rail → hidden → whole, the rail's head brings the whole sidebar back, the whole sidebar's toolbar collapses it to the rail, and renaming on the rail sets the whole sidebar as a setting. The desk store keeps no `rail` (a stored one is ignored). Hidden, the column comes out OVER the desk at the window's left edge (5px trigger), a cover the windows give way to, and its toolbar's button keeps it open.
- *The favorites on the rail* (`RailFavorites`, chosen 2026-10-02 from the Rail favorites lab's concepts and popout styles): one folder tile in place of a row per favorite — the first four icons in a 2×2 and how many there are — so ten favorites take 32px of the rail rather than ten rows. Under the folder stand the favorites whose pages are open, a row each with the grid's open dot under the icon (the one in view, if any, a longer dot), as concept D of the lab had them: shown on a click, closed with the middle button, the tile's own menu on a right-click. They come and go as their pages open and close, and keep the favorites' order: the one whose space is current stays where it is, its space's tabs and Stack under its row (TabList's `entryTabs`, drawn into FavoritesGrid's `rail-favorite-entry` through a portal; `useDeskChrome.favoriteEntry` says where), as a pin's are under its row. Until 2026-10-07 its row was taken out to stand with them below the folder's rows, so choosing one moved it to the end. The pointer resting on it (120ms, so one passing over does not), a click, or Enter (or →) slides a sheet out of the rail's edge over the desk: a piece of the whole sidebar on its own ground (the surface and the theme's gradient, or with the desktop glass its tint and gradient over a frost of the desk), holding the favorites grid itself — `FavoritesGrid`'s tiles, so their menus, the dot of a favorite with its page open, the brand-tinted tile of the page in view and the organization's presets are all as in the whole sidebar. It is a cover over the desk (`favorites`): the live pages under it give way to their stills, and it shows once they have. It goes a moment after the pointer leaves it and the folder (300ms, the desk's cards' grace), unless a click pinned it; a press elsewhere (a live page's too), Escape, choosing a favorite, or a window taken in hand puts it away. In it the arrow keys move through the favorites, three to a row, and Escape goes back to the folder. A row dragged onto the folder opens the sheet, and its grid takes the drop (the drag's drop target is the folder while the sheet is shut, its grid while it is out); the sheet stays while the drag lasts. The column is drawn above the desk while the sheet is out, as for the footer's menu.
- *Marks*: a dot at the leading edge of a tab's row while its window is out on the desk — on the space's line, in the whole sidebar — as beside a running app; the window in use has a longer one in the space's colour. The tab the desk's agent is working in wears the agent's ring on its row, as its window does.
- *Click* a row of the space: it is the window's, as the Dock's icon was (`TabList`'s `activate` → `engine.add(tabId, { focus: true })`). A window that is out comes to the top and takes the keyboard; a minimized one grows back, in use; out and in use already, the click is the address's, as on any tab in use (the palette opens on it — `inUse`). A tab that is not out comes out — its tab may still be the one in use, its window put away, and it comes back out all the same — flying from its row's icon, where the desk's layout has room for it (`placeNewWindow`): on an empty desk, a comfortable window in the middle (or, the space's one tab — a loose space's, a page's, a space of one — the whole desk); on a **tiled** desk (every window a tile, lying along two of the desk's edges or a cell of a tiled arrangement, none overlapping), the hole it has left if that is room enough (beside a half, the other half; beside three quarters, the fourth), and with no such hole the window in use splits along its longer side and gives the new one its second half, so the desk stays tiled (one window filling the desk becomes the two halves); among windows set down freely, the size windows come out at, where it covers the least of them. A row of a tab in another space chooses that tab, and the desk passes to that space (below).
- *⇧⌫* with the pointer on a row of the space closes its tab, whether the keyboard is in a page, the shell or neither: a window it has out on the desk goes with it (Reopen closed tab brings it back). Held down, it closes one tab, not one per repeat. The keyboard never sees the key; off the rows, ⇧⌫ is the page's as ever.
- *Drag* a row: within the sidebar it moves as it always does (among the rows, into a folder or a space, onto the favorites), but the desk has no split zones. *Pulled out* past the column's edge, a row of the space becomes its tab's window, held by its title bar near the bar's leading end, so the window reaches out across the desk from the hand. A tab not out comes out at the size windows come out at, growing out of the row's icon under the pointer. A window already out flies from where it was to the hand, its title bar under the pointer, at its own size, or, too big to carry (a full-height half, the whole desk), scaled down to fit in two thirds of the desk's width and four fifths of its height, its shape kept (`carrySize`). From there it is any window in hand: magnets, edge zones, Shift's snap, a throw. A row of a tab in another space, let go over the desk, joins this space, and its window comes out where it was let go, its title bar under the pointer (kept on the desk).
- *Other spaces* are the sidebar's own rows. A click on another space's header — its icon on the rail — passes the desk to it (below), as a Dock folder's would; the current space's header folds and unfolds its rows. (Until 2026-10-09 a click folded any space in the whole sidebar, and a desk button on hover passed the desk.)
- *Right-click* a row for the sidebar's menu for it (the same builders, `chrome/tab-menu.tsx` and `chrome/tab-group-menu.tsx`, so the sidebar's rows and the desk's never drift apart). A tab of the space gets first what the desk does with its window (**Open on the desk** for a tab not out; **Bring to front** for a window out but not in use; **Minimize** and **Collapse into the sidebar** for one out, or **Expand** and **Collapse into the sidebar** for one minimized), then the tab's own entries: pin, favorite, move to a folder, out of its space, into a new space or another space, duplicate, reader view, ask Pistachio, suspend, force focus, close. There is no split view (a desk lays its windows out by hand), a tab whose window is out cannot be suspended (it is on screen), and **Add to** another space sends its window flying into that space's row. **Duplicate** puts the copy beside the tab in the space and brings it out on the desk, in use. A space's menu is the sidebar's: **Rename** (in its row), its colour, **New tab in space** (the desk passes to that space, and the tab comes out there), Keep open, **Release the tabs** (each its own space again), **Close space** (`docs/spaces.md` §1). (Until 2026-10-09 it also had *Open as split view*, which left the desk, and *Open as desk*.)
- *Context*: the last row under the current space is its context — the Stack (`docs/desk-agent.md` §1): a pile of sheets and how many things it holds (on the rail, a count on the pile). Files dropped on it go into the context; a click opens its card beside the sidebar, over the desk.
- *The desk's card* (⋯, at the rail's head or in the whole sidebar's toolbar): the pointer on it brings the **More card** up beside the sidebar, level with the button (`DeskMoreCard`): **Tile the windows**, **Cascade the windows** and **Arrange for me** (the smart layout, `docs/desk-layout.md`), each with its keyboard shortcut (⌘⌥T, ⌘⌥C and ⌘⌥L by default, changed in Settings › Keyboard); **Feel**, the variants (below), with Glide's deceleration under the throw (until 2026-10-09 also **Leave the desk**). The card stays while the pointer is on the button or on it, with 300ms of grace to cross the gap between (and while a press held in it, such as the deceleration slider in hand, lasts), and goes a moment after the pointer leaves both. A click on ⋯ pins it up until a press anywhere else (on a live page too) or Escape. Tile and Cascade put it away as they act; the Feel settings leave it up, so several can be tried in a row. A window taken in hand puts any card away.

**Windows.**
- *The frame's controls*: ⋯ (the frame's menu), ⤢ (fill the desk), − (collapse into the sidebar) and × (close). What is wanted less often is on the menu: a document's *Open in its app*, *Mask*, *Minimize*. A right-click on the frame brings up the same menu with the buttons' actions under them, Close last. While the menu is up, the window's controls stay shown.
- *Not in use*: a window other than the one in use dims its title bar as macOS dims an inactive window's: its title and site fade back, its icon loses its colour, and its controls grey out until the pointer comes to them (150ms each way).
- *Move* by the frame (title bar, tab, handle or drawer, depending on the frame variant). A press raises the window; a click gives its page the keyboard.
- *The Drawer frame* (Feel › Frame › Drawer; 2026-10-06, the default since 2026-10-07): a window is its page alone, edge to edge. Its page controls (back and forward, dimmed with no history, and reload, as the pane toolbar's), title, site and controls are on a 34px strip (`DRAWER_H`) behind its top edge, as wide as the window, which slides up out of it (`DeskEngine`'s `#stepDrawer`: out in 250ms, in in 150ms, on the smooth ease-out, 120ms after the pointer has gone). With that much room above the window, the drawer is out while the pointer is anywhere on it, or while the window is in use — in use, only where it would lie over no other window (`#drawerOverOthers`): in a split one above the other, the drawer of the one below would lie over the foot of the one above, and that window, under it in the stack, was its still for as long as the one below was in use (2026-10-07: a window left a picture, dead to the pointer, until something else happened). A press on the desk's own surface, between its windows, leaves the window in use (`pressDesk`, `#blurred`): its tab stays the browser's tab in use, but the window looks out of use (its shadow shallower, its drawer in) until it is pressed again — its frame, or its page (main's `DeskPageInput` "press") — or chosen, or given the keyboard. Until 2026-10-07 the Title bar was the default; a desk Feel saved before then had it whether chosen or not (every save wrote all the variants), so it is read as the Drawer once (`readPersistedDesk`, saved `version` 2). With less (a filled or tiled window, one near the desk's top edge), it comes out only for the pointer at the window's top: the top 10px of its page (`DESK_DRAWER_TRIGGER`, main's mouse hook, `DeskHover.top`), a strip there over a drawn page, or the drawer itself. It then rises as far as the room allows and the window's page slides down the rest, as the main window's page slides down for its pane toolbar, within the window's own box: cut at the window's foot (or the desk's) and held at its own size (`DeskZoomedPage` at zoom 1) until a moment after it is back, so it never lays out anew. (Until 2026-10-07 the whole window slid down, cut only at the desk's foot: the window above in a split one above the other then reached over the one below it, and one of the two was the other's still while the pointer stayed.) The drawer is its window's in the stack: live pages under it give way to their stills before it slides out over them. Masked windows keep the handle; a parked window has its drawer only while raised. Out, the drawer is the window's top bar: its top corners round, the page square against it and round at its foot (2026-10-08). Main cuts a live page round at all four corners alike (`setBorderRadius` has one radius), so the page's box behind it squares off as the drawer comes out (`--drawer-t`) and is painted at its top in the page's own colours (`deskPageCorners`, read from the page's styles in an isolated world, `main/page-corners.ts`; not a capture: a capture of a rect of a page held its masked layout through its fullscreen), what shows through the page's top corners. The Title bar and Tab frames' pages meet their bars the same way. No window has a ring or outline round it, in use or not: its shadow sets it on the desk.
- *Edit the address*: click a window's title (its favicon, title and site, lit on hover) and the address palette opens on that tab, as ⌘L does, and the tab is the one in use. Dragged from the title, the window moves as from anywhere on the bar; a double click there does not fill the desk (the empty part of the bar does). ⌘L opens the same palette on the window in use, wherever the keyboard is.
- *Let go of the whole desk*: a window that fills the desk (90% of it or more both ways) shrinks as soon as it is dragged, the way a maximized window lets go of the screen when its title bar is pulled. It goes back to the size it had before it filled the desk, or else to the size windows come out at (58% × 74% of the desk), and it springs to that size around the point the pointer holds (a title bar stays under the pointer). Small enough to carry to a half or a quarter. A window spanning the desk only one way lets go of that span alone: a tall one (a left or right half) keeps its width and comes down to 74% of the desk's height; a wide one (a top or bottom half) keeps its height and comes in to 58% of its width (`letGoSize`).
- *Hold Shift to snap* (any window in hand: moved by its frame, grabbed from its page, or dragged out of the sidebar). The desk reads the pointer in thirds and lights the tile the window will land in: the corners are quarters and the sides halves; the middle column is a comfortable window in the centre, except along its top edge (the top 7% of the desk, `SNAP_TOP_SHARE`), which is the whole desk. A window is carried by its title bar, so through the middle of the desk the pointer is high in the top third: that still lands it centred, and only taking the window up to the top fills the desk (the Snap throw's map, shown before letting go). The tile holds until the pointer is 16px past a line, so it never flickers. Let go and the window springs into the tile, whatever the Throw variant or the pointer's speed. Shift can go down or up at any moment of the drag, with the pointer still: the tile lights or goes out at once. While a tile is lit (Shift's, or an edge zone's), a carried window that is drawn thins a little so the tile reads through it.
- *Hold Shift and drag anywhere on a window*, including on its live page. The press never reaches the page. The key is a variant: Shift, Option, Command or off. With Shift (the default) the grab starts in snap mode, since Shift is held; let go of Shift once the window is in hand to place it freely.
- *Stick*: an edge within 14px of the desk's edge, or of a neighbour's edge (beside it with the gap, or flush), snaps onto it and a guide line shows. The desk's leading edge, beside the sidebar, is one of these.
- *Edge zones*: push the pointer into the desk's left or right edge for that half, the top edge for the whole desk, and the corners for quarters. The zone lights in the space's colour; let go and the window fills it.
- *Throw*: release while moving and what happens depends on the Throw variant (below).
- *Resize* from any edge or corner. Edges stick the same way.
- *Resize a split from its gutter*: where two windows stand side by side (or one above the other) the desk's gap apart, as tiling, a zone or the magnets leave them, the gutter between them is the split's, as between the panes of a split view. The pointer in it finds a resize edge (either window's; they meet there), and dragged, the gutter moves: one window grows as the other shrinks, the gap between them kept and their far edges still. Every window on that gutter moves with it, so nothing comes to overlap or leaves the line: a half beside two stacked quarters takes both quarters along, while the gutter between the quarters is theirs alone. In a grid, an edge moves the gutter of its own row or column; a corner at a crossing (where three or four windows are cornered) moves both gutters, and every window cornered there with them. The gutter goes no further than leaves each window on it its least size (300 × 200), and on the way it sticks to the edges of windows not on it, so a grid's two gutters can be lined up again. It is a split only the desk's gap apart, give or take 2px (`SEAM_SLACK`): flush, overlapping or further apart, each edge is its own window's, as before. Masked and minimized windows are never on a split, nor is a window still settling into its place. The desk resized (the app's window, the sidebar beside it), windows a gutter apart stay a gutter apart.
- *Double-click* the frame, or press its ⤢ button, to fill the desk and back. (A minimized window, double-clicked, expands.)
- *The leading edge is the desk's like the others* (2026-10-08): pushed into, it offers the split-screen snaps as the right edge does — the left half, with the top-left and bottom-left quarters at the band's ends (96px) — in the same 18px band, and a pointer carried on past it, out over the sidebar, still lights the left half, as one carried off the window's right side lights the right. Nothing over the sidebar takes a window: let go there, it lands in the left half, its tab kept. (Until 2026-10-08 a **drop rail** stood over the sidebar while a window was carried near the edge — Collapse above, Close below — the desk's band there was 30px so the two never overlapped, and a window flung hard at the edge was put away.)
- *Collapse*: press its − button (*Collapse into the sidebar*; a document's says *into the Stack*): it flies into its row's icon in the sidebar (the space's cluster, if its rows are folded), which gives a little bounce as it takes it. (A document's goes into the Stack, the Context row.)
- *Minimize*: choose Minimize on the window's menu (⋯, or a right-click on its frame), or on its row's menu. The window stays on the desk, small (the least a window may be, 300 × 200) and with its page shown as if zoomed to 50%: the page lays out as it would on a window twice the size and is drawn at half scale, so a responsive site shows its desk layout at a glance rather than its narrowest one (a navbar). It shrinks to the desk's foot and parks there beside the sidebar, peeking up a quarter of its height (its title bar and a strip of its page), the rest below the desk's edge, like a file peeking from a folder; the next window minimized parks to its right, overlapping it by half and over it. The shelf lies over the windows at the desk's foot, as the Bar's notch does: a window filling the desk still fills it, its whole page live, and over a live page the parked windows are drawn by main's shelf view (see *The foot*). The window in use, minimized, passes the keyboard to the window under it.
  - *Hover* a parked window and it rises into full view, over its neighbours and the windows it covers; the others stay down. It goes back down a moment (80ms) after the pointer leaves it. Rising, going down and moving along the shelf are timed motions, not springs, never past their place whatever the Feel's spring: on transitions.dev's motion tokens (its transitions-polish skill, matched by usage), rising is a hover lift in, 250ms; going down is a close, quicker, 150ms; moving along as the shelf closes up is a position change, 250ms; all on the smooth ease-out, `cubic-bezier(0.22, 1, 0.36, 1)` (`EASE_SMOOTH_OUT`, `cubicBezier` in `lib/desk/motion.ts`). The 80ms before going down is the skill's intent gate: long enough that the pointer crossing between the window's frame and its live page is not leaving, and no more. Shrinking into the shelf and growing back out ride the Feel's spring.
  - It is *live* like any window, peeking and raised: its page scrolls, clicks, types. A click lands where the page is drawn.
  - *Expand* (its ⤢ button, a double click on its frame, or its row in the sidebar): it grows back to the box it had before it was minimized, in use, its page at its own size again.
  - *Drag* it by its frame (or the grab key anywhere on it) and it comes out of the shelf, still minimized, a small zoomed window anywhere on the desk; the shelf closes up behind it. Let go at the desk's foot and it parks again, at the place in the shelf nearest the pointer. Out on the desk it can be *resized* from any edge or corner, its page still at 50% (a parked one is dragged out first: most of it is below the edge). Magnets and a throw work as for any window.
  - *Snapped* as any window is — an edge zone pushed into, Shift's tile, the Snap throw, the top edge to fill the desk — it lands in the tile as a window at its own size again, its page no longer zoomed (filled, letting go of the desk gives it back the box it had before it was minimized). Over the shelf's band at the desk's foot (where they peek up, and a gap above) no tile is lit for it, even in the corner where a window would light the bottom-left quarter: the shelf is where it goes there. Tile and Cascade leave it as it is.
  - A masked window is a picture of part of a page and is not minimized. A shell page (home, a note, the brief) and a document are zoomed out the same way, by the shell.
- *Close*: press the window's ×, or let go on the Close segment. Pressed, the window draws in a little where it stands (to 96%) and fades, a modal's close (150ms); let go on the segment, it goes into it. Then its tab is closed (Reopen closed tab brings it back). A document's window only closes: the file stays in the Stack. A window dragged out of the sidebar and let go on Close closes that tab too.

**Mask: keep part of a page.** For when only part of a page matters (a chart, a ticker, a player): shrinking the window would not do, because the page is responsive and its content reflows, shrinks or moves.
- *Choose*: *Mask* on the window's menu (⋯; a web page only, not the shell's own pages; *Cancel mask* there while choosing). The page freezes and dims, the cursor becomes a crosshair, and a drag draws the region to keep, as a screenshot's selection is drawn, with its size shown in a corner. Let go and the window becomes that region. A drag under 16px, Escape, or the crop button again calls it off.
- *Masked*: the window is the region, where it was on the page and at the size it was, with the bare frame's handle above it; the rest of the window fades from around it. It stays live and usable: main keeps the page laying out at the box it had, shows only the region, and maps the pointer on it to the page's own point, so a click on a masked video plays or pauses it, hovering shows the player's controls, a drag scrubs, and the keyboard works as it always does. The player's own fullscreen button makes the page itself on the whole screen, and it is masked again when it leaves fullscreen. The handle's controls, shown on hover or while the window is in use, are its menu (⋯), Unmask, Collapse and Close; *Edit mask* is on the menu.
- *Edit mask*: the window's whole page shows around the region, where it lies on the page and at the region's scale, faded so the desk shows through; the region stays at full strength in a white frame with knobs on its corners and edges. Drag an edge or corner to change the region (never off the page, never under 16px a side, its size shown while dragged), or drag inside it to move it over the page. Done (the bar above the page, Enter, or a press anywhere else) and the window becomes the new region, where it lies on the page, at the same scale, the rest fading as when it was first masked; Cancel or Escape and it stays as it was. The page is shown by its still (the whole page, taken as the edit begins), so it is not live meanwhile.
- *Resize* from an edge or corner: the region keeps its shape and scales like a picture, not a page. The page never reflows; the region is re-rendered sharp at the new size once it is let go (while it is dragged, its still stretches). A region may be as small as 16px a side.
- *Move* by its handle, or with the grab key held anywhere on it, as any window. A press on the region is the page's.
- *Kept out of the layouts*: filling the desk, the edge zones, Shift's snap and the Snap throw would stretch the picture, so they leave a masked window alone, as do Tile and Cascade; and a tab brought out of the sidebar never takes half of a masked window.
- *Unmask*: the whole window grows back around the region, at the page's own scale, the region staying where it is: the window is drawn all the way, its page's picture revealed around the region as the frame grows (nothing in it is stretched), and it is live again once it has landed. The page is never given a size between: it goes on laying out at its own box until its view is shown there. Leaving the desk, the window in use comes back as the whole pane.
- *Kept*: masks are saved with the desk's arrangement (on this device, as the arrangement is). Reopened, a masked window that comes out of the sidebar comes out masked, where it was and at its size, its region's shape kept whatever the desk's shape is now. A masked window whose tab is the one in view when the desk opens lifts off whole, as any first window does, lands as the window it was cut from, and is cropped back to its region (the rest fading, as when it was first masked) once a still of the page as it then stands is up, then goes where it was left. A masked tab that slept while the desk was away gets its mask back when it wakes.

**Switch space** (another space's header in the sidebar — its icon on the rail — a tab of it chosen anywhere, or *New tab in space* on its menu): the desk passes to that space in place. The space left is saved as it was, and its windows fly into its row in the sidebar, shrinking into it; it bounces as the last one lands. The chosen space's windows come out of its row, to where they were last left, and the one on top then is in use again. A space never on a desk comes out as its tab used last, alone: filling the desk when it is its one tab, else a window in the middle. Chosen again before its windows are home, a space takes them back: they turn round to where they were left. **Choosing a tab** passes it the same way: a tab of another space (its row in the sidebar, the tab switcher, the address palette's open tabs, a favorite's page) brings that space's desk up on that tab — its windows where they were left, and the chosen tab's out and on top, in use. Which space is current is main's (`snapshot.currentGroupId`), and the desk follows it (`docs/spaces.md` §2). (Until 2026-10-09 this was *Switch group*: a desk button on another group's row or *Open as desk* on its menu while a desk was up, and off the desk a chosen tab filled the pane.)

**A loose space** (2026-10-02 as a loose tab's desk; `TabGroupInfo.loose`). A day tab in no named space is a space of one, the loose space, which main makes for it the moment it is listed (until 2026-10-09 the shell made one, by `deskFor` in `lib/desk/open.ts`, only when the tab was chosen on a desk). Its desk is the tab's one window, filling the empty desk the first time (since 2026-10-05; before, in the middle) and where it was left after that. It has everything a space has — the Bar (asking about the tab, by its title), its conversation, the Stack under the tab's row, files dropped on the desk, the agent's desk tools — but is never drawn as a space: the sidebar, the switcher and the menus show its tab alone (main lists these apart, the snapshot's `looseGroups`), it is grey, and Tidy treats its tab as loose. **New tab** there (⌘T, the sidebar's +), a row let go over it, a duplicate, a link opened beside the tab: the space has two tabs, and from then on it is a space like any other — drawn, coloured beside its neighbours, named from its tabs (main, `addToTabGroup`); the desk stays its, the window where it was. The tab put in another space (its row dragged into one, its menu's Add to) is that space's, and its loose space, emptied, goes unless it holds a Stack or a conversation (`isPersonsGroup`); the desk passes to the space it joined, on the tab (in place, the window staying, when that space was never on a desk).

**A page's space** (2026-10-05; `TabGroupInfo.anchorId`). A favorite's, an organization preset's or a pinned page's tab is in its entry's space, led by its page, which stays the entry's; main makes it as the page opens (until 2026-10-09 the shell made it when the page was chosen on a desk, `deskFor`'s `anchored` create). Its desk has all a space's has — the Bar, its conversation, the Stack, files dropped on it, the agent's desk tools — and ⌘T, a link or palette tab opened there, or a row let go over it, adds a day tab to the space, its window out on that desk. The space is never drawn among the day's tabs: while it is current the sidebar draws its page and its other tabs under the entry (a pin's under its row; a favorite's at the head of the list, under the favorites, the rail's open row for it hidden meanwhile), the Stack after them (`entry-tabs`; rows that are no drop slot); while it is not, the entry counts the tabs waiting there (`EntryTabCount` on the tile, the rail's open row, the pin's row). Chosen again, its windows come out where they were left. Its page let go of its entry — brought down (below), closed, moved to another Profile — the space is one like any other: drawn, coloured, named from its tabs, or titled after the entry if it is the person's (main's `#reconcileTabGroups`; of one tab, a loose space). Tidy's favorites reset archives a favorite's space whole or brings it down into the day's tabs, the favorite left closed (`docs/tab-tidy.md` §3.7); a pin's stays until it is dragged down. (Until 2026-10-09 a tab that was neither an entry's page nor a day tab came up on a page's own desk, its id `tab:` and the tab's, with no Bar, Stack or file drops; nothing listed is outside a space now, and a hidden tab is in none.)

**Bringing an entry's page down** (SidebarCommand `bringDown`, 2026-10-05). A favorite or pin dragged into the day's tabs — its tile, its row, the rail's open row, its page's row under it — brings its page down there, its page's space with it as a space like any other, and the entry stays, closed: opened again, it is a page of its own, in a space of its own (filling the desk). Dragged into a space (its header, between its tabs), or let go over the desk, its page — and its space's tabs — join that space, the window coming out where it was let go (`dropTabOnDesk`); one with no page open does the same with a fresh page of it at its address (`dropEntryOnDesk`). Removing an entry is its menu's (Remove from favorites, Unpin). Main sets these down by the day's row units (`placeDayTab`, as a space's move does; until then an unpinned or unfavorited page was placed by counting tabs, and landed off by a space's or split's worth).

**New tab** (the sidebar's New tab, ⌘T, a palette choice, Reopen closed tab, a link opened from the home page): a new tab in the current space — on the home page for ⌘T and the sidebar's New tab; in a loose space, making it one of two (above) — out on the desk as the window in use, where the desk's layout has room for it (as a clicked row does). An empty space's desk takes it the same way. The General new-tab setting (an address palette, or a set URL) does not apply on the desktop. A link a page opens beside itself joins its page's space; a background tab from any other path is a loose space and never takes the desk (`docs/spaces.md` §1). (Until 2026-10-09 a new tab joined the desk's space only while a desk was up, and the shell added it.)

**A Glance on the desk** (2026-10-08; `GlanceOverlay`'s `desk`, `DeskEngine.receiveGlance`). A link Glanced from a desk window comes up over the desk as anywhere (the windows dimmed on the pictures main took), and its two actions take it in as one of the desk's windows rather than a tab over the panes. **Fill the desk** (⤢): the live page grows from the card to the box of a window filling the desk, the windows coming back up under it as it goes. **Add as a tile** (the grid, the desk's Tile icon; in place of split view, which a desk does not have): it flies to a tile, and the windows make room for it at the same time. On a tiled desk that is the hole the tiles have left, or else half of the window it was Glanced from (`placeNewWindow`'s tiled answer, the window it came from first), so the rest stay where they are; among windows set down freely, every window is tiled (⌘⌥T's tiles), it beside the window it came from. Either way it is the same live page throughout, never a still: its tab joins the space as any new tab on the desk does (above), and its window is made where the page landed, in use, with no flight out of its row and no smart-layout moment (the person chose where it goes). Main needs nothing new for it: the promoted page stays up where it landed (`promoteGlance`'s staged reveal), and the desk's next layout already places it there (measured 2026-10-08: 6–10ms after the promotion, no layout pass between). A page's own desk (no space) has the pane's actions, as its tab would join no space.

**Arrange** (the More card, or ⌘⌥T and ⌘⌥C wherever the keyboard is, a page's or the shell's): tile every window, or cascade them. The web, which has no desk, does not offer the keys (`ShortcutDefinition.surfaces`).

**Now playing** (2026-10-02; `components/desk/DeskNowPlaying.tsx`, `PipApp.tsx`, `lib/desk/now-playing.ts`). Several pages are in view on a desk at once, so media does not leave its window when another tab is chosen, as it leaves the pane: a window whose page plays something (not a call, nor a page presenting itself whole) has **Pop out** on its frame (the picture-in-picture mark for a video, sound bars for audio), first of its buttons. Pressed, the window goes into its row and its media plays on with no window of its own — as anything playing does whose window is not out (a tab is in view on a desk while its window is out on it, not while main counts it visible). A window behind one filling the desk is out but out of sight: a video it was playing with its sound on floats, as a tab's does that is left in the background, and is its window's again once that window is in sight (the other let down, or it brought up) — 2026-10-07, `DeskSurface`'s `behind` (`useDeskChrome`); behind, the window draws no live picture of it, and the player takes its page once the window is drawn (its view out of main's layout). In the whole sidebar that is the media stack's card, the video's picture in it as in the browser. On the rail:
- *A video* floats over the desk as its page's own picture (main's media preview — the tab's view, presenting only its video — raised above the desk's pages), 320×180 as it opens, frameless, with nothing over it until the pointer is on it or it is paused: then its controls over the picture, as a browser's picture in picture has them (the "pip" chrome view, `PipApp`): back to the desk and the title at its head, close (the stack's dismiss) at the other end; previous track, back 15 s, play or pause, forward 15 s, next track across the middle; the playhead and times (or Live), mute and its own volume at its foot: a slider that slides out beside the speaker while the pointer or the keyboard is on it, setting that page's player alone (its element's volume, `setVolume`), whatever the system's or another player's; above zero it unmutes, all the way down it is muted, and Unmute from there brings it back to where it was taken from (2026-10-08). A press on the picture away from the controls moves it, wherever it is let go (kept on this device as a fraction of the window). Its edges and corners resize it as a masked window's do, its shape kept (the picture is the video's, drawn whole): the edge or corner in hand follows the pointer, the opposite one holds still, from 240 wide (its controls still in their rows) to as large as the window holds (`lib/desk/pip-box.ts`; its width kept on this device with its place). They straddle the picture's border, mostly outside it, as a window's do: the pip view stands out from the picture all round (`DESK_PIP_OUTSET`), a clear ring that takes the pointer over anything under it. It first stands beside the rail, above the desk's foot. Anything the shell raises over the window (a menu, the address palette, settings) or a cover over it on the desk (a card, the Bar's answer) takes the picture down while it lies there (and its shadow, the shell's, which would lie over what covers it); the desk shows such a cover only once the picture is down (`DeskEngine.setFloat`, the view's `floatCovered`). Back to the desk brings its window out, in use, and the picture goes back into it.
- *Audio* (and anything else the stack would draw) is a button in the rail above its footer: four bars that move with how loud it is as it plays — its page measures that from a capture of the playing element while the rail asks (`setMediaMeters`; a protected or cross-origin stream that cannot be heard that way, or a capture that hears nothing while Chromium says it sounds, moves the bars as sound would) — and a play button while it does not. A click plays or pauses it. The pointer resting on it (120 ms) opens a card beside the rail at its height with the media stack's own card, every control out (seek, rate, back and forward, tracks, mute, the tab); it goes 300 ms after the pointer leaves both, unless a rate is being set. The card is a cover over the desk (`now-playing`).
A popped-out page's picture waits for its window to be gone from the desk (`useDeskWindowKey`): main takes a preview back while the page is still in its layout. A tab sent this way is sent until its window is out again or its media ends, and none once the desk is left.

**Leave** (until 2026-10-09): *Leave the desk* on the More card and ⌥⌘\\ (Toggle desk) grew the window in use back into the whole surface as the pane, flew the rest home, and gave the column back what it was without a desk; a space gone from under its desk passed it to the tab in use's own, and with no tab in use the desk was left. There is nothing to leave now: a space that goes passes the desk to the space of the tab used last, with none left in the Profile the desk shows a fresh empty space, and `toggleDesk` is retired, its id kept and its binding null (`docs/spaces.md` §1).

## 2. Variants

Under **Feel** on the More card (⋯, at the rail's head or in the whole sidebar's toolbar). Click a row to cycle it; Shift-click goes back. Choices persist on this device.

| Axis | Choices | What changes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Throw | **Glide** · Snap · Free | Glide: momentum that fades (at the Deceleration below); meeting an edge ends the coast in a rebound that settles flush against it. Snap: every release lands in a tile, read from where the throw would carry the pointer, on the desk in thirds (corners → quarters, sides → halves, the middle column's top edge → full, the rest of the middle → a centred window). Free: stays where it is dropped (magnets only). |
| Deceleration | 6–60%, **28%** | Glide's alone (a slider under the throw, dimmed under any other throw): the share of its speed a coasting window loses every 100 ms. 28% is a coast of 0.3 s (the time to lose 63% of its speed, τ = −0.1 s / ln(1 − d)); 60% stops a throw almost where it is let go, 6% sends it across the desk. `glideTauFor` in `lib/desk/motion.ts`; the coast, the rebound it aims at, and the spring that finishes a throw off the desk all use it.  |
| Spring | **Snappy** · Bouncy · Smooth · Eased | The spring every settle rides: response 0.26 s / damping 0.86, 0.42 / 0.56, 0.48 / 1. Eased has no spring: every settle and flight is a timed ease on transitions.dev's motion tokens (below). |
| In hand | **Lifted** · Live | Lifted: a carried window shows its still. It lifts (scale 1.035, deeper shadow), tilts with the swing about the point it is held by, and may travel anywhere. Live: the page stays live and flat in hand, and stays on the desk. |
| Frame | Title bar · Tab · Bare · **Drawer** | A title bar above the page; a folder tab on its shoulder; just the page with a handle pill above it; or just the page, its controls on a strip that slides out from behind its top edge. |
| Grab key | **⇧ Shift** · ⌥ Option · ⌘ Command · Off | The key that grabs a window from anywhere on it. Shift is also the snap key (not a variant), so a Shift grab starts snapping; Option or Command grab without snapping, and Shift added mid-drag snaps. |
| Layout | **Smart** · By hand | Whether a window coming or going may lay the others out anew, as the layout model judges (`docs/desk-layout.md`). By hand, nothing moves on its own; ⌘⌥L still arranges on request. |

**Eased** follows the [transitions.dev](https://transitions.dev) skill (`Jakubantalik/transitions.dev`, installed in `.agents/skills/`: transitions-dev's transitions and transitions-polish's motion tokens and rules, matched by what each motion does, never by the nearest number). Every window motion is a timed ease on the smooth ease-out, `cubic-bezier(0.22, 1, 0.36, 1)` (`EASE_SMOOTH_OUT`), started from wherever the window is when its target is set, whatever set it (`#easeTo`, from `#step`):
- a window come to rest elsewhere at its own size (let go, stuck to an edge, the end of a throw, Undo layout) is a position change, `--duration-fast`, 250ms;
- one whose size changes (filled, tiled, cascaded, snapped, let go of the desk, unmasked, expanded) is the card resize transition, its 300ms;
- out of the sidebar, growing from its row's icon, is a dropdown opening from its trigger, 250ms; into the sidebar or another space's row, a dropdown closing into it, `--duration-quick`, 150ms — a close quicker than its open, as the polish rules have every close; a flight's scale is eased with its position;
- an arrangement's windows, and a desk's coming out, set off `--duration-stagger` (40ms) apart, the whole run under 300ms (`#beat`);
- nothing overshoots: a throw meeting an edge comes to rest against it rather than rebounding (the coast itself is the Throw's), and what still follows the pointer — a window letting go of the desk in hand, one flying to the hand, the lift and the tilt — settles critically damped (`EASED_SPRING`). The shelf's motions and the leave's grow were already timed on the same tokens, whatever the Spring.
With reduced motion every one of them is instant (the skill's reduced-motion guard is `transition: none`).

Reduced motion replaces every spring with a quick critically damped one and drops the tilt.

## 3. How it works

A desk window is two things drawn by two processes: its **frame** is the shell's DOM, and its **page** is the tab's native view, placed by main over the hole the frame leaves. A native view paints over everything the shell draws, so:

- **Covered windows are drawn by the shell.** A window with anything stacked above it shows its *still* where the page would be, so the stacking is the DOM's and always right. Only uncovered windows go live (`uncoveredWindows` in `lib/desk/geometry.ts`). An armed zone counts as a cover, so the pages under it give way. So does a card the sidebar opens over the desk (the More card, the Stack's: `DeskSideCard`), declared with `setCover("card", rect)` — with the gap between it and the sidebar, so the pointer crossing it is the shell's — and shown only once the engine lists the key in `clearCovers` (no live page left under it).
- **Transformed windows are drawn too.** A native view cannot scale or rotate, so a lifted, flying or (in Lifted) settling window is its still.
- **A window growing into a larger box is its live page** (`Win.growTo`, `#latchGrowth`, `#keepInGrowth`): filled, snapped into a tile or an edge zone, tiled, arranged, expanded from minimized, grown back into the shelf's band. Before 2026-10-01 it was its still, a picture of the smaller window stretched to the larger, so the page looked zoomed in for the length of the settle, and then laid out anew once there. Now, as it sets out, the engine tells main the box it is growing to (`DeskZoomedPage` at zoom 1), so the page lays out there at once, unseen, and the window grows as itself, revealing the page as it is to be: the view is never larger than that box (one dimension may be shrinking meanwhile), and the window goes no further than where it set out from and where it is going, so the Feel's spring never carries it past (an overshoot stops at the edge, its speed there spent). It comes off the list as it lands, at the box it is already laid out at. A window shrinking is its live page too (`Win.shrinkTo`, `#latchShrink`, 2026-10-07): its view is resized each frame and its page laid out at every size it passes through — until then it was its still, scaled down, a picture dead to the pointer until it landed; a masked or minimized one, one landing to be masked again, or one coming off its mask, have their own ways. One covered on the way (the More card, a tooltip) is its still, and its page is held at the box it grows to all the same; drawn when it lands, it stays on the list until it is live there, coming off a frame after its view is shown. Until 2026-10-07 it came off as it landed, while its view was down: a hidden view's new size never reaches its page, so the page went back to the size it set out at, every still of it was that small page, and the window, shown at last, was laid out anew (the user's report: a window filling the desk looked like a zoomed-in picture, dead to clicks, for seconds). A still is drawn no larger than the page box it pictures (`PaneStill.width`/`height`, `stillSize`): a view that is down keeps drawing at the size it last had while shown, so a still of a window grown under a cover is that box (asked for again as the growth begins, it is the page laid out at the new box, its top-left part), at its own scale, the rest of the window blank until it is live — never blown up to the window. Both sizes are CSS px: `capturePage`'s picture is in the display's pixels (two to a CSS px on a Retina display, its NativeImage at scale factor 1), so main divides by the display's scale (`#captureScale`) for `width`/`height`, crops a held page's picture to its box in the picture's own pixels (`zoomedStill`), and compares a capture with its view's box at that scale (`#captureAtViewSize`). Until 2026-10-08 all three took pixels for CSS px, so on a Retina display a held page's still was its top-left quarter drawn over the whole window — the page zoomed into its corner (the user's report: a window filled the desk, then zoomed into the top left and went dead) — and the cap was twice too loose. The specs run at 1×, where the two agree; desk-grow.spec's "at 2×" runs at `--force-device-scale-factor=2`. And a frame button's tooltip, a cover over its own window, goes as the button is pressed (`FrameTip.dismiss`): Fill the desk used to grow the window as its still while its tooltip stayed up under the resting pointer.
- **A still must be fresh before it stands in.** Each window records when it began wanting one, and only a still requested after that (and painted) may replace the live page. Until then the page stays up. Stills are captured at the start of every gesture for all live windows, so they are ready in time.
- **Live pages stack in the windows' order.** `BrowserLayout.stacked`: main re-adds the shown views bottom to top when the order changes (a raise), so even during the moments above the pages overlap correctly.

The motion is imperative (`components/desk/desk-engine.ts`): rects, springs, the coast and the tilt run per frame outside React, write transforms straight to the elements, and report the live pages' boxes to main (`setLayout`, deduped). React re-renders only when a window is added, raised, becomes drawn or live, or starts or ends a flight. Gestures use the drag layer (`lib/pane-drag.ts`), as pane resizes do, so the pointer is never lost over a page.

**Grabbing a live page** (`@pistachio/shell-contracts/desk`). The shell tells main which views are desk windows and which key grabs (`setDesk`). Main follows the key from every view's key events (`noteDeskKey`), because the mouse events `before-mouse-event` delivers carry no modifiers. A press on a desk page with the key held is taken from the page, the shell hears `onDeskGrab`, and the rest of the press is relayed on the drag-sample channel. Points are anchored to the screen at the press, since the view moves under the pointer. While the key is held, every desk page shows an open hand (a user-origin `!important` cursor rule). A plain press on a desk page makes it the active tab.

**The sidebar is the desk's dock** (`layouts/SidebarLayout.tsx`'s `SidebarColumn`; `SidebarChrome`'s `rail`). (Until 2026-10-09 `toggleDesk` opened with `afterSidebar`: the desk store held the space as `opening`, the column slid to the rail, `sidebarReady` turned `opening` into `groupId` once the slot had settled, and the desk mounted on a stage that already had its width; leaving handed the row back to panes, `finishLeave`. The desk is always up now, `docs/spaces.md` §2.) The column is the desk's: pinned whatever the setting says, at the rail's width (`SIDEBAR_RAIL_W`, 48px) or the sidebar's own, as `useDeskStore`'s `rail` says (persisted with the variants; ⌘S on a desk — `toggleSidebarPinned` — flips it, as does the button at the rail's head). Every change the desk makes to the slot's width (up, gone, rail and whole) runs on the compact sidebar's clock (`useDeskSlide`), since a pinned slot otherwise follows a resize drag frame by frame. Main hears `ShellState.sidebarOnDesk` (it was `sidebarAway`, when the sidebar was put away for the desk), which keeps the compact edge's native watch off and the traffic lights shown in the whole sidebar (`applyWindowButtons`), and `ShellState.sidebarRail` (the rail, in the sidebar layout), which hides them at once — not after a retreat, since the rail's head (`lights` false, 6px down) is in their place from its first frame. (Until 2026-10-02 they stayed on the rail, reaching over the desk's top-left corner, the head padded below them.) The rail is the same chrome narrowed: `SidebarRailContext` tells the few pieces that draw differently (the address, the toolbar, the Context row), and the rest is CSS on `.chrome-sidebar[data-rail]` (every row's children but its first hidden, sections' headers as hairlines, spaces as capsules). A rail draws no background media; a screen share that begins says so as a notice. A menu from the rail's footer (the Profile's menu, `SidebarMenu`) is wider than the rail and hangs out over the desk: at rest the rail's slot clips nothing (`overflow-visible` unless it is sliding), the column is drawn above the desk while a menu is open (`.sidebar-motion-slot[data-rail]:has([role="menu"])`; not always, or a window flying home into a row would pass under it), and the desk's live pages give way to their pictures as for a context menu (`useMenuOverPages` raises the `"context-menu"` overlay; the panel shows once `overlayReady`).

*Since 2026-10-09* (docs/spaces.md §3): the desk is always up and the column's mode is the setting `layout.sidebar` (`lib/sidebar-mode.ts`: `useSidebarMode`, the rail only where the desk is), so the opening handshake above (`opening`, `sidebarReady`, `afterSidebar`) and the desk store's `rail` are gone, and `useDeskSlide` is keyed on the mode: every change of mode, never a reveal, runs the slot's width on the 250ms clock while the desk rescales its windows. The slot carries `data-mode`. Hidden on the desk is an overlay: the slot stays its 10px strip, the column is a cover (`setCover("sidebar")` through `useDeskCover`, `lib/desk/chrome.ts`, which the rail's favorites sheet shares) and slides in by its transform only once `clearCovers` lists it (`data-overlay` raises the slot; its ground is the favorites sheet's), keeping cover and layer until its retreat — or, a mode left while it was out, the slot's slide — has ended. While it is away the engine sends windows home to the window's edge at their row's height (`DeskHost.sidebarAway`). Main hears `sidebarRevealed` as "the column is on screen" (hidden on the desk: once out) and `sidebarRail`; `sidebarOnDesk` is gone (it was always true).

**The sidebar and the desk meet** in `lib/desk/chrome.ts` (`useDeskChrome`): the desk publishes which tabs have windows out (`marks`: out, or in use) and which tab its agent works in (`agentTab`); the sidebar's rows draw them (`DeskRowMark`; `useAgentWorkingIn` reads the desk's agent as well as the console's), say which of the space's rows is under the pointer (`hovered`), and open the cards (`card`, anchored at the button or row that opened it, in the window's coordinates) that the desk draws beside the column, over its windows (`DeskSideCard`). The engine finds the sidebar through its host: `sidebar()`, the column's box (left of the stage, so at negative x in the stage's coordinates), and `homeOf(kind, id)`, the element a window flies home to — a tab's row inside the tab list, else its space's header; a space's header; for a document, the Context row — each only while it is on screen in the list. A window put away flies to that element's icon (`#iconOf`: the square 8px in, as tall as the row, at most 24px), and the element bounces (`data-received`). ⇧⌫ on a row: the row says it is hovered (`hoverDeskRow`), the surface tells the engine (`setDockHover` → `DeskState.dockHover`), main takes ⇧⌫ from whichever view it is struck in, in its relay of every view's keys after the tab switcher's (`takeDeskDockKey`), and tells the shell (`DeskPageInput` `"close"`), which closes the hovered tab through the store's `closeTab` (which asks first if the agent's task is using it).

**Entering and leaving** (until 2026-10-09, while the desk was a mode). The first window started as a window whose *page* was exactly the pane's box, so main's view did not move, and shrank into place once its still was up; leaving, the window in use grew back to the pane's box as its live page, laid out at that box the moment the leave began (`DeskZoomedPage` at zoom 1), on a timed ease (`LEAVE_GROW_MS`, 250ms) rather than the Feel's spring, and the surface went back to panes once it was live there. (Before 2026-09-30 it grew as its still, stretched, and the page then laid out anew: zoomed for most of a second, then a blank flash.) With the desk always up there is no pane to lift off from or grow back into: a **cold start** — launch, a shell reload, a Profile switch — places every saved window at its saved box at once, masked ones masked, minimized ones parked, the window in use on top, with no flights (`DeskEngine.start`, `#laidOut`; `docs/spaces.md` §2).

**Passing to another space** (`DeskEngine.switchGroup`; a change of main's `currentGroupId`, since 2026-10-09). One engine runs the whole passing, so both spaces' windows can be in flight at once and main hears one layout: the surface is keyed by the Profile and the store's `instance`, not by the space, and `groupId` changes under it. The new space's windows set out from its row (`#groupRect`: its header in the sidebar, or the column's middle while it is scrolled out of sight); the surface's layout effect saves the old space under its own id (the host's `shownGroup` moves on only after), marks its windows `#departing`, and brings the new space's out with `#flyingIn(…, from)`. A departing window that is live wants a still like any window in motion, and flies (`#sendAway` to the space's row, `#departFor`) once it is drawn, or after 400ms; so it flies as what it showed. Meanwhile it is not the space's any more: `syncTabs` leaves departing and homebound windows alone, `hasLivePage` looks in every tab, the surface finds the tab for a window outside the space (`strays`), and main's desk state lists the new space's windows first, capped at `MAX_DESK_WINDOWS` (`isDeskState` refuses more). Where a space's windows come out is one routine (`#laidOut`: saved boxes in the desk as it is now, the entry added and put on top, capped at `MAX_DESK_WINDOWS`), used by `start` and `switchGroup`; the entry a passed-to space comes up on is `passedEntry` (`lib/desk/store.ts`). A window of the space come back to that is still departing or flying home is taken back (`#takeBack`: its flight turned round to its saved box), never made anew; and a window flying home counts as leaving in main's desk state, so the incoming space's masks go first. The phase is `"entering"` until everything has landed; then the new space is saved. (Until 2026-10-09 the shell chose the space from the tab in use — `DeskSurface`'s active-tab effect, `deskFor` in `lib/desk/open.ts`, a loose tab's space made on the spot, the chosen tab kept as the entry by `passFor` — and added a new tab to the desk's space itself; main does both now, and the surface follows `currentGroupId`, `docs/spaces.md` §2.) A window of the tab that came in comes out once its space lists it (the tab-sync effect). The space changing under the tab in use without a pass (its window out, its space gone or a new one with no saved arrangement) is `regroup`: the engine takes the new tabs, keeps their windows where they are and sends any other home. Main keeps loose tabs' spaces with the rest (`#tabGroups`, saved with them; `sanitizeTabGroups` keeps `loose` only on a space of one tab) but lists them apart (`tabGroups()` is the drawn ones, which the snapshot, Tidy, the namer and the row's `move` see; `looseTabGroups()`), and grows one into a drawn space in `addToTabGroup`. The now playing (above): the frame's Pop out is `useNowPlaying.pop` then `putAway`; `useBackgroundMedia` (MediaStack) counts a tab in view on a desk by its window being out (`useDeskChrome` marks), or as the tab in use unless it was sent, and keeps a sent tab whatever its sound. The rail draws `RailNowPlaying` in place of the media region. Main raises the previewed tab's view over the desk's pages whenever it restacks them or a preview starts (`#raiseMediaPreview`), and relays the pip view's input (a control, checked as every control is, or a press on the picture or an edge, the shell then raising the drag layer: `setDragCapture`). The OS gives a press's moves and release to the view it began on, whatever is raised over it since, so the drag layer hears none of a press on the pip view: main follows the view's own mouse events while the shell moves the player by it and sends them on the drag layer's channel, as a grabbed desk page's are (`main/desk-pip-press.ts`), and leaves the view where it is in the stack till it is let go (re-adding a view takes it out from under the press it holds). A grab whose release came first is dropped, or the shell would wait on it. The tab preload's meter (`MEDIA_METER_CHANNEL`) runs only while main asks (`BrowserController.setMediaMeters`, re-asked after a navigation): `captureStream()` of the primary element into an `AnalyserNode`, 20 samples a second, the root of the RMS reported rounded and only when it moves; main relays a metered tab's level to the shell (`MediaLevel`). The desk contracts import the media types only: a runtime import would make the media module one both preloads share, and Rollup would split it into a chunk the sandboxed tab preload cannot require (the tab preload then fails to load, and no page reports its media). `#setBeside` (a duplicate, a link opened beside its tab) puts the new tab in a loose space only while that tab is in the current space or a held one (`#onDesk`); `#regroupAfterMove` never takes a loose tab out of its own space. A window of a loose space or a page's space flies to and from its tab's row (a favorite's tile, or on the rail its row under the folder: `data-live-tab-id`; a page's space's other tabs, whose rows go with its desk, to its page's), and the Stack's row stands under that row. The mask editor's whole-page picture is asked for no larger than `MAX_DESK_STILL_WIDTH` on its longer side, its shape kept: a small region shown large would otherwise ask for a page wider than `isDeskState` accepts, and main would drop the whole desk state.

**The sidebar's menus** (`TabList`'s `useTabMenu` with `desk`; `deskTabEntries` in `DeskSidebarControls.tsx`). A menu is the shell's context menu (`useContextMenu`), a shell overlay: main takes the live pages down for it and the desk paints the pictures main took of them. Such a picture is the whole page (`stillShows: "page"`) whether or not the engine had a still of its own; before, the window in use, live since it landed and so with no still of the engine's, showed blank under a menu or the address palette. The shared builders take a `desk` option (`TabMenuOptions.desk`: whether a tab is out, and how it goes to another space, the engine's `moveTabToGroup`), and a tab of the space gets the desk's entries first (`deskTabEntries`, from the engine lent through `lib/desk/open.ts`'s `deskEngine`). A tab can leave the space in use and stay open in more ways than a drop (pinned, made a favorite, taken out of its space, put in a new one): the space keeps the desk, and main has another of its tabs used instead (its tab used last), or, emptied and the person's, the desk shows it empty (`docs/spaces.md` §1). Main opens a duplicate beside its tab, in the tab's space, before it is shown (`#createManagedTab`'s `beside`): it used to be set down after its page had loaded, and a copy of a space's last tab never joined the space, so the copy came up as a tab outside the space and the desk was left (this holds everywhere a tab is duplicated, the sidebar included).

**Where windows go** (`#usable`, `#reach`). The stage is a soft well in the surface (`.desk-stage`, the Whisper well chosen on 2026-10-02 from the canvas of desk-surface styles: the shell's ground at 2.5% darker, an inset shade 3px down and in from its top and leading edges and an inset light from the others, `--desk-well-shade` and `--desk-well-light`; no fill of its own, no dot grid; its corners a window's, `--desk-window-radius`: the Appearance radius, 4px more for the title bar and tab frames, so a window filling the desk fills it to the curve, and the engine rounds a window's clip where it reaches a corner (`#cornerRadii`, read from the stage as it is measured), so no shadow shows past it; 18px until 2026-10-06), set off from the sidebar and the window's edges by the surface's gutter; it is the pane's own box, so the entry and the leave stay seamless. (Until 2026-10-02 it stopped above a band at the card's foot, the Bar's — `BAR_BAND`, its height and two gaps — or the shelf's while windows were parked; the Bar became a notch over the windows instead, *The foot*.) `#reach`, where a window may be moved, coast, rest and resize, is the same box; the two differed while the desk had a dock of its own over its leading edge, and then for a day `#usable` kept a gap all round inside the card, which left a strip of background no window could use. A still asked for too soon after the last one (`STILL_RETRY_MS`) schedules a render for when it may be asked (`#renderIn`), and a window that has just begun to want a still asks at once, so a cover never waits for good on a still nobody asks for.

**The foot** (`setNotch`, `setNotchShape`, `notchOver`, `shelf`; 2026-10-02). Windows have the whole card, and keep their whole pages: what the shell draws at the desk's foot lies over them — the Bar's notch (`docs/desk-agent.md`) in the middle, and the parked windows from the leading corner — and a live page, a native view, would paint over both. So over a live page each is drawn by a utility view of main's stacked above the tab views (main/chrome-view.ts), the shell saying where and what:

- *The notch view* (`NotchApp`, the `"notch"` chrome view): while a live page is under the Bar's nub (the engine's `notchOver`, from the box the nub may fill swelled, in the trailing foot corner, `setNotch`), it is that box, its bottom-right corner the desk's, cut to the nub's outline, its face the nub's own mark (`DeskNubMark`), painted in the shell's ground (`setDeskNotch`). Since 2026-10-08 (the nub) it draws the nub's swell itself: the shell sends how swelled the nub is on its way to (`DeskNotchFrame.swelling`: from, to, since when on the wall clock, over how long), and the view and the shell's own nub read it off the same clock (`swellAt`), so the view's box never changes as the nub swells, only its outline; main raises the view only as it comes up, not on every word (re-added under the pointer, it would lose the pointer and say it had gone). Painted, since a page lies under it: cut to the nub's outline (`nubOutline`, shared with the engine), it lays the ground's layers as the shell lays them around the desk's foot — the desk surface's `background-200` with the glass off; with it on, a stand-in for the window's glass under the chrome's tint and the theme's gradient — and the grain over either, the gradient and the grain over the shell's ground box (`DeskNotchFrame.ground`, its `.chrome-container`) so they run on from the gutter around it. The glass is AppKit's under-window material, which follows what lies behind the window; the stand-in was measured against the gutter on 2026-10-04 (light: the gutter read 202 over a black window and 219 over a white one; dark: the theme's recessed ground already reads within 4). It was first set near the dark end (`oklch(0.862 0 0)`), but over a light wallpaper — the usual desktop — the notch read 14 levels darker than the gutter, so since 2026-10-05 it is a neutral grey at the white end (`oklch(0.925 0 0)`, worked back from that gutter through the tint and the mesh gradient); with something dark behind the window the notch reads lighter than the gutter instead. Before any of this the view was one flat colour, 26 levels lighter than the gutter in light. The pointer on it and a press there go back to the Bar (`onDeskNotchInput`: enter, leave, press — a press first gives the shell the keyboard), which swells the nub as if the pointer were on it (the view drawing the swell) and, on a press, lets out its menu; once the page under that has given way, the view goes, and the shell's own nub is under the pointer. Hidden so, from under the pointer, the view never hears the pointer go, and shown again its page has the pointer there still: it says the pointer came wherever the pointer is — a window brought down under the notch, one filling the desk, grew the Bar, which, never hearing the pointer leave, stayed grown, every window under it its picture, dead to the pointer (the user's report, 2026-10-08). So the Bar takes the view's word only when the OS's pointer (`getCursorPoint`) is on the notch or the Bar, or cannot be read (Playwright); the view says it again as the pointer moves on it (at most every 100ms: a page that thinks the pointer is on its button already hears no coming); and while the Bar holds the pointer to be on it (arming or hovered), it reads the OS's pointer every 150ms (`BAR_POINTER_CHECK_MS`) and lets go once the pointer is off it — `document.elementFromPoint` there not in the Bar, nor in the notch view's box. (desk-bar-pointer.spec stubs main's pointer; `PISTACHIO_E2E_CURSOR=1` lets main read it under Playwright.) With no live page under it, the nub is the hole below, the ground itself.
- *The shelf view* (`ShelfApp`, the `"shelf"` chrome view): while a live page is under the parked windows down in the shelf (the engine's `shelf`: each one's place, left to right; not the raised one, nor one in hand), they are drawn there as the shell draws them at rest — the card, the title bar dimmed as a window not in use's, the picture of the page (`setDeskShelf`, from DeskSurface, cut off at the desk's foot by the view's own edge). Meanwhile they are their stills (the engine forces them drawn, and asks for their stills, which the view shows), and they cover no window under them (the stack's coverage leaves the lowered shelf out, `#render`), so the window under them stays live. The pointer onto one in the view raises it (`onDeskShelfInput` → `hoverMini`, its own source, `shelf`, apart from the shell's `frame`), and raised, it covers the page under it, which gives way; the view goes, the shelf being the shell's to draw again. Gone from under the pointer, the view never hears the pointer leave, nor does the shell unless the pointer crosses the window's frame on its way — off onto the page above, it does not — so the window stayed up for good (the user's report, 2026-10-08: "very glitchy"). While a parked window is raised, the shell reads the OS's pointer every 120ms (`getCursorPoint`, DeskSurface) and lets it down once the pointer is off it (`leaveMini`). Main raises the view only as it comes up — re-added under the pointer on every word from the shell (a parked window's picture changing), it lost the pointer and said it had gone, and the window it had raised dropped and rose again on the next move — and tells it the pointer has gone each time it comes back (a `mouseLeave`), so it hears the pointer come onto the window it last had under it. (desk-shelf-hover.spec; Playwright's Chromium tells a hidden view the pointer left, which the real app's does not, so only the first of these shows there.)

(Until the evening of 2026-10-02 a window under the idle notch or the parked windows was cut short of them, its page stopping at their top across its whole width: a light strip under every page that reached the foot — even one only touching the shelf's last window — which read as a fault.) What comes out of the nub is a cover like any (`bar`, the nub's box with its fan of droplets; `pill`; `conversations`): the windows under it are drawn while it is up — asked for as the menu is (the pill's footprint too, so a droplet's pill opens at once), and the notch view goes once the page under it is a still. The pointer resting on the nub asks nothing of the pages: the swell is the nub's own, and over a live page the view draws it, so a pointer passing the corner freezes no page and the view's ground never gives way to the hole's mid-swell.

The nub is not painted. It is a hole (`setNotchShape`, the nub as it swells and settles — a circle's radius, how far its centre is sunk in from both edges, its fillets' radius — written each frame by `nub-motion.ts`; draining into the corner as the nub lets go of the edges for its menu, and none while it stands free, painted, a button of its own) through the well — the well is the stage's `::before` now, clipped by `--desk-notch-clip` — and through every window under it (`#notchClip`: a `path(evenodd, …)` clip on the window, its shadow kept as far as the desk's edges; not a carried, flying or turned window, which passes over the nub), down to the shell's own ground. With the desktop glass on (the macOS default) that ground is the window's vibrancy, which nothing drawn over a page could match; as a hole, the nub is the ground whatever the theme, continuous with the surface's gutter around the corner. The outline (`nubOutline`, shared with the notch view) runs off the foot at a fillet (11px), round the far side of the circle (18px, its centre 20px in from both edges; swelled, 21px and 23px), into the trailing edge at the other fillet, down it and round the desk's own corner (`--desk-window-radius`, the engine's `#corner`) back along the foot — so a window filling the desk, rounded there as the desk is, has the hole matching its curve. Until 2026-10-08 it was a notch centred on the foot, its flares 10px, its shoulders rounded (`notchOutline`). Nothing of a window at rest falls past the desk's foot (`#write`'s clip): a window reaching the foot cast its shadow into the surface's gutter there, and the notch, cut down to the clean ground, stood out from the darkened edge around it. Its shadow stops at the desk's other edges too (2026-10-04): the shell around the desk — the sidebar, the gutter at the top and trailing side — lies above the desk, so no window's shadow falls on it. Only the shadow is cut there, as far as the edge (`ClipReach`); a window crossing a side edge in hand keeps its whole body, since a live page's native view would paint past any clip. Lifted, flying or turned, a window passes over the edges as it is.

**Minimized windows** (`minimize`, `expand`, `hoverMini` in the engine; `Win.mini`; `DeskZoomedPage` and `DeskHover` in `@pistachio/shell-contracts/desk`; main's "Desk zooms" section).
- *The zoom is Chromium's device emulation, Electron's own* (`webContents.enableDeviceEmulation`: `viewSize` the window's page box over `DESK_MINI_ZOOM`, `scale` the zoom), not the zoom factor, which Chromium keeps per site (⌘+ on one tab zooms every tab of that site), and not the debugger's `Emulation.setDeviceMetricsOverride`, which would hold a debugger session (forced focus and masks share it). Probed in Electron 43 (a standalone app): the page lays out at the emulated size and its media queries follow; input on the view is mapped to the page by Chromium itself (a click at view (100, 50) lands on the page at (200, 100)), unlike a mask's viewport override, so nothing is re-sent; a view cut shorter than the page box changes nothing the page can see (no resize, the same `innerWidth`/`innerHeight`), and a click in it is still mapped; another tab of the same site is untouched. A document loaded afresh in another renderer does not carry the emulation over, so main sets it again on every `did-navigate`. A page with no document yet must not be emulated at all: `enableDeviceEmulation` on a view just made for a waking tab, before its first load commits, crashes the main process (a null renderer view, `EXC_BAD_ACCESS` at `0xf8`). 0.0.28 crashed so whenever a desk reopened after a relaunch on a saved minimized window, its tab asleep: the desk wakes every window's tab, and the waking tab's zoom was applied as its view was made. Main now emulates only pages that have committed a document since their view was made or their renderer went (`#committedPages`, marked first on `did-navigate`); a waking page is zoomed on its first navigation. (Synthetic wheel events do not scroll any page there, so the wheel was not probed.)
- *The shell tells main* each minimized tab's page box and zoom (`DeskState.zoomed`), by where it is headed, reported before the layout as masks are. Main sets the emulation at once, while the window is drawn for its shrink, so the page lays out at its new size unseen. Growing back (`miniMotion "out"`), the window is listed at zoom 1 with the box it grows to, so its page lays out once, there, and comes off the list (and the emulation off) as it lands. A zoomed page in fullscreen has its emulation off until it comes back (as a mask's override). The off-screen viewport main gives a page read before it was ever drawn (`#ensureViewport`) is also device emulation: a zoomed page never takes it, and a release of it sets the zoom again.
- *The shelf* (`#parked`, `#layShelf`, `#shelfRect`): the parked windows left to right from `SHELF_INSET` (18px, clear of the well's corner up to an 18px radius, so a window cut off square at the desk's foot never pokes out past the corner's curve), each `MINI_SIZE.w / 2` after the last (closer when they would run past the desk), at `height − h/4` (`MINI_PEEK`, a quarter; half until 2026-10-02), or raised, `height − h − gap`. They are the top of the stack (`#stackShelf`, kept by `#raise`), the raised one over all, and lie over the windows at the desk's foot (*The foot*: over a live page, main's shelf view draws them): nothing about the desk's room changes as windows park and leave.
- *Peeking live* (unless a live page is under the shelf: then it is its still, drawn by main's shelf view, *The foot*). A parked window's box runs past the desk's edge, and peeks up from the foot of the desk's card, so the surface's gutter below the card stays clear (until 2026-10-01 it peeked from the window's own edge, through the gutter, when the desk had no card). Its element is clipped there (`clip-path`, written with its box; the stage itself is not clipped, which would flatten the Bar's glass), and the view the engine reports is cut short at the edge: main shows the top of the zoomed page. Rising and going down, a parked window stays live (no still is wanted for that motion; its page never changes size); shrinking into the shelf and growing back it is drawn.
- *Hover.* The shell hears the pointer on a window's frame, and on its page while the page is drawn; over a live page it hears nothing, so main follows its mouse hook (`before-mouse-event`) for the zoomed pages and says when the pointer comes onto one and leaves (`onDeskHover`, once each way). The engine keeps both sources for the hovered window and lowers it only once both have let go for `MINI_LOWER_MS`, so crossing from its frame to its page is not leaving.
- *Stills.* A still is the page as its view shows it, drawn at the page's own shape from the top (`height: auto`), never stretched to the window: one taken while it peeked is its top half. Down (drawn), main sizes a zoomed view to its whole page box for its still, and captures it as a mask's (`#captureAtViewSize`, a frame apart until it is at the view's size); the emulation draws the page into the corner of whatever the view's surface is, so a capture from a surface not yet resized is cropped to the page box (`zoomedStill`). Seen before that crop: a covered window's still showed its page a quarter the size, in its corner.
- *Snapping* (`#placeCarried`, `#gestureEnd`, `#overShelf`): a minimized window in hand lights zones and Shift's tiles as any window (a masked one still does not); let go in one, it is unminimized (`#unminimize`, so its page is held at the tile's box as it grows there) and sent to the tile. In the shelf's band (`h/4 + gap` above the desk's foot), no zone is read for it, and a release parks it.
- *Kept*: `SavedDeskWindow.mini` (the box it grows back to, and whether it is parked). A desk reopened brings its minimized windows back minimized, parked in the saved order; the window it opens on lifts off at its own size. Undo layout puts back whether each was minimized (`DeskLayoutSnapshot`); the agent sees `minimized` on each window, and placing one gives it its size back.

**Masks** (`DeskMask` in `@pistachio/shell-contracts/desk`; the engine's `startMask`, `applyMask`, `unmask`, `editMask`; `MaskSelector` and `MaskEditor` in `DeskWindow.tsx`; main's desk-mask section). Chromium can show part of a page without the page knowing: `Emulation.setDeviceMetricsOverride` over the tab's debugger session holds the page's layout at a given size (the box it had when masked), and its `viewport` draws a region of the document into the view's top-left at a page scale. So a masked window's view is just the region's box, its page still lays out at its old width (no responsive reflow), and a resize changes only the scale, which Chromium re-rasterizes (sharp, unlike a stretched bitmap). What had to be learned by trying it in Electron 43 (a standalone probe):
- The viewport's offset is in document px, and Chromium paints only what lies inside the page's own viewport: a page scrolled away from the region shows blank. So the region is kept as a part of the page's BOX, and re-aimed by the page's scroll, which a script in an isolated world reports through `Runtime.addBinding` (the page cannot see the binding), installed for new documents with `Page.addScriptToEvaluateOnNewDocument` (a reload re-reports) and for the current one in a world from `Page.createIsolatedWorld`. Offsets are the shell's px (DIP); the page's scroll is CSS px, times the zoom factor.
- Input is not mapped: a click on the view lands where it would on the unmasked page. So main maps it (`#forwardMaskedMouse`): every mouse event on a masked view that goes on to the page (after the desk's own grab key has had its say) is stopped and re-sent with `sendInputEvent` at the page's point, the region's corner plus the point on the view over the scale. Re-sent events are delivered at points outside the view's (smaller) box, and `before-mouse-event` sees them synchronously, so a flag lets them through. Those events carry no modifiers, so the keys down (followed in `noteDeskKey`) and the buttons held are put back on them: Shift- and ⌘-clicks stay those, and a move with a button held stays a drag. The wheel never passes `before-mouse-event`; the compositor scrolls what is under the pointer as drawn, which is right. A page gone fullscreen has its override taken off until it comes back (`#suspendDeskMask`).
- `capturePage` of a masked view, shown or hidden, is the region at the view's size. Main sizes a masked view that is down to the size its window shows it at (`DeskState.masks[].width/height`), so its still is right, and tags stills with the mask they show (`PaneStill.mask`, `deskMaskKey`). The engine keeps which mask each still shows: a still of the whole page (taken before masking) is cropped to the region with CSS; one of another mask stands for nothing.
- Order matters, or the page shows at the wrong scale, or reflows at the region's size, for a frame: the shell reports the desk (with masks) before the layout; main places a masked view only once the override for its size is in place (`#placeMaskedView`; the window's still, painted under every live page, shows meanwhile); taking a mask off, main takes the view down at its old box and keeps the page at that box until the view is shown there (below); a still waits for any of that (`#deskMaskWork`).
- Taking a mask off (`#clearDeskMask`) does not take the override off. Its window grows back through every size between the region and the page, and a window waiting for a still stays live meanwhile, so main would have placed the plain view at each of those sizes and the page would have laid out at every one (a recorded run: 550×289, 562×303, …, 884×686, media queries flipping). Now the override stays, set to the page's own box with no region (`DeskMaskRelease`), until the view is shown at that box, and comes off a frame later (`#settleDeskRelease`, `#endDeskRelease`); shown at another size, it comes off once that size has held 250 ms (the window really was resized). The engine draws an unmasking window from its first frame (`Win.unmasking`, forced drawn, only a whole-page still counts as fresh) and places its still at the page's place with `--reveal-*` properties written each frame (`#revealBox`: from the region's box at its scale to the page's landing box at its own, as far as the window has grown), so the region stays put. A capture of a masked or releasing page is taken again a frame apart (up to 8 times) until it comes at the view's size (`#captureAtViewSize`): a view resized while down draws at its new size a frame or so later, and a still taken before is the old box's picture at the new scale (the Edit mask glitch, a zoomed crop of the page's top-left, 2026-09-29). Every override main sends for a mask carries `dontSetVisibleSize: true` (`deskMaskMetrics`): otherwise Chromium notes the view's size when an override begins and puts that size back when it is cleared, and a mask set up again after its page left fullscreen came off with the page laid out a screen wide (probed: cleared, a 1200-wide start came back as 1200 though the view was 800; with the flag it stays 800, and the masked picture is unchanged).
- The page must never learn it is masked. The first override sent WITH a `viewport` lays the page out at the viewport's size for a frame (a `resize` to the region, media queries flipping) before `width`/`height` take hold, and a responsive app (Notion) reflows for good: its sidebar, wrapping and scroll change, so the region shows other content. Main turns the override on at the page's own box first, with no viewport (nothing changes), and only then sends the region (`#aimDeskMask`, whenever no override is in place: a new mask, a woken tab, back from fullscreen). Probed: then no `resize`, no media-query change and no `ResizeObserver` report — nor when the region, its scale or the view's size change after. `screen.availHeight` still reads the screen's full height under emulation (no layout depends on it). The e2e test "a mask never lets its page see a resize" records all four from inside the page.
- Editing a mask: the engine reports the window's mask as the WHOLE page box (`x = y = 0`, the page's size) at the size the editor shows it, so main re-aims the same override at all of the page (the same box, so the page sees nothing) and its still is the whole page; `stillShows` takes a still of the whole-page mask for the page. A mask that changes but keeps its page box is re-aimed where it stands (`#syncDeskMasks`), never taken off and set up again, and the override's aim carries the mask's key, so a view is shown, and a still tagged, only for the mask actually in place. The editor's page and bar are a cover (`maskedit`), shown once no live page is under them.
- The session is shared with forced focus (`debugger-hold.ts`): each holds it, and it is detached only when neither does.
- Kept across desks: `SavedDeskWindow.mask` (checked with `isDeskMask` on reading; a bad one leaves the window whole). The engine's `start` gives a window from the sidebar its mask at once, and asks for the space's stills only after the first report has told main of the masks, so they come back as the region. The first window carries the mask it is to get back (`maskWanted`): landed whole, it asks for a still, and `#putMaskBack` masks it once that still is painted (or after 1.5 s without one) and sends it where it was saved. A sleeping tab's page is a new one when it wakes, so main drops the mask state of the old page and sets up the new one (`#syncDeskMasks` from the wake).

**From row to window** (`chrome/shelf-drag.tsx`; the engine's `pullFromSidebar`, `#takeInHand` and `addAt`; `dropTabOnDesk` in `lib/desk/open.ts`). A row's drag is the sidebar's own gesture (the shelf's: among the rows, folders, spaces, the favorites); on a desk its split zones are off. With a row of the desk's space in hand, the pointer 2px past the column's edge hands the press on: the shelf's gesture ends without a drop (giving back its capture) and `pullFromSidebar` starts a pane drag with the button still down. A tab not out gets a new window, `"spawn"`, whose scale starts at the row icon's width and springs up with the lift. A window already out becomes a `"move"`, and a `lag` (its offset from where the hand holds it) springs to zero, so it flies to the hand; magnets wait until it arrives. Either way the hand holds the bar at `pinTop` (half the bar's height), at `clamp(pointer − desk's left, 28, width / 2)` along it. A row of a tab outside the space stays the shelf's gesture over the desk (its ghost drawn by the drag layer, as over a page); let go there, the tab joins the space (`addTab`), the desk waits to hear of it (the snapshot that says so, up to 1.5 s), and its window comes out at the drop (`addAt`: the size windows come out at, its title bar under the pointer, kept on the desk). A release over the desk is read from the sample it was let go at, never handed on: the engine's drag would start with the button already up. The favorites' grid is read before the desk (`overFavorites`, in `dropFor`, the hand-off and the release): on the rail it is in the sheet out over the desk, and a row let go there is a favorite, not a window.

**The leading edge** (`edgeZone` in `lib/desk/geometry.ts`; the engine's `#placeCarried`). It is read as the other edges are: the same band, and past the edge counts as at it. The sidebar's column is still measured (`#measureSide`: windows fly to and from its rows), but nothing over it is a target. Until 2026-10-08 the engine laid a drop rail's two segments out over the column (`sidebarDrops`, `dockDropAt`, `DeskDropRail`): Collapse and Close, and a fling home; removed for an edge like the right one.

**Splits** (`seamsAt`, `seamTravel`, `alongSeam` in `lib/desk/geometry.ts`; `#jointAt` and `#placeResized` in the engine; `Gesture.joint`). A desk has no tree of panes, only free windows, so a split is read from where they are, at the press: the windows that meet across the gutter beside the edge in hand are the desk's gap apart (within `SEAM_SLACK`) and run side by side for some of the way (more than a pixel), and the pointer is along the stretch they share (give or take the gutter's width, so a press at a corner counts). From that pair the seam spreads to every window meeting one already on it across the same gutter, on either side, at the same line. A corner takes hold of both its gutters if each is a seam, and where they cross each seam takes in the windows of the other cornered there (an edge of theirs on its line), and spreads again, until neither grows: so the four corners at a grid's crossing are the same press. The seams and every window on them, as they stood at the press, ride on the gesture; each frame the pointer's travel along each seam's axis is clamped to what leaves every window on it its least size (never less than now, for one smaller already), stuck to the edges of the windows not on it (an edge lined up with theirs, or a gutter from them, within the magnets' 14px), and applied to them all: those before the gutter end that much further on, those after begin there. An edge of a corner with no seam its way is the window's own, resized and stuck as any. Windows on a seam are stopped where they are at the press (a settling one is not on it). All of them are live through the drag (only the edge's own window is raised), each laid out by main at its new box every frame, and saved when it is let go. A stage resize or a change in the desk's foot band carries windows to the new desk with `rescaleRect`, which counts each window with the gutter after it, so a gutter stays the desk's gap; a desk reopened at another size is denormalized from its saved fractions as before, its gutters scaled with it.

**Shift, the snap key.** The engine keeps whether Shift is down from every source that knows: a press's own event, each pointer sample (the drag layer's and the shell's pointer events say; `DragSample.shift`), the shell's own key events, and main, which follows Shift from every view's keys (`noteDeskKey`, the same relay as the grab key) and tells the shell when it changes (`onDeskShift`). The key events are what make a Shift pressed or let go with the pointer standing still count at once. A sample's reading is passed on once (`lib/pane-drag.ts`), never repeated, so a stale one cannot outvote a later key event. While the drag layer holds the pointer, main's double-Shift detector stands down, so trying Shift twice mid-drag never bookmarks the page.

**A window's tab is never asleep.** Every tab the desk reports as a window (`DeskState.tabIds`) is woken by main the moment it is reported (`setDesk` → `#ensureLiveTab`, shown once painted, as any wake is), so a window never asks for a click to wake: a desk reopened on saved windows whose tabs went to sleep meanwhile (or came back asleep after a relaunch), a window the agent brought out quietly. Its placeholder says *Waking…* until then. And none is put to sleep: `suspendTab`, the idle sweep's too, skips any tab in the layout and any desk window, live or drawn (covered, minimized, under an overlay). Before 2026-10-02 only the layout was skipped, and a window whose tab slept showed *Asleep — click to wake*.

State: the variants, and each space's arrangement (as fractions of the desk, bottom to top, each masked window with its `DeskMask`). These are the shell's, in `lib/desk/store.ts` and this device's localStorage (`pistachio.desk.v1`); a saved `tab:` arrangement (a page's own desk, until 2026-10-09) is dropped on reading. Main never stores an arrangement; which space is current is main's (`DurableSpaceSession.currentGroupId`).

## 5. Not done / open questions

- The feel numbers (coast τ, restitution, magnet reach, lift, tilt, spring presets) are first guesses, tuned against screenshots rather than a hand on a trackpad.
- **Live** in hand: the frame (DOM) and the page (native) are separate compositors, so during a fast drag the title bar may run a frame ahead of the page. Not measured.
- A covered window's still is refreshed every 3 s; it is not a live page.
- A row is handed to the desk only past the column's edge, and only a tab's own row or an entry's (a pin's, a favorite's: its page): a split's stays the sidebar's drag over the desk. An entry's page is a window in hand only over its own desk; over another space's it is let go into that space, with no window in hand until then.
- Splits are the web's (`docs/spaces.md` §1): a split saved by an earlier desktop is folded as the session loads, its tabs kept.
- With Shift as the grab key, a desk page loses Shift-click (extend selection, open in new window), and every page grab starts in snap mode. Option or Command avoid both.
- Splits: a gutter is a split only while its windows are the desk's gap apart, give or take 2px. A desk reopened at a size other than the one it was left at scales its saved arrangement, gutters with it (saved as fractions of the desk, `normalizeRect`): from a much smaller or larger window (beyond about a quarter either way) the gutters are no longer the desk's gap and stop being splits until the windows are tiled again or moved against each other. Nothing marks a split before it is dragged (the cursor is the resize cursor either way), there is no way to resize just one of two split windows from the gutter (its far edge, or moving it off the gutter, does), and the gutter does not stick at halves or thirds of the desk.
- Shift snaps moves only. A resize with Shift held is a plain resize (no snapping of edges to fractions of the desk, no kept aspect ratio).
- Nothing over the sidebar takes a carried window, and no throw puts one away: its − button does, and its × closes it.
- Dragged by its title bar, only a window spanning the desk (both ways, or its whole height or width) lets go of its size; a quarter or a free window keeps it. A tall or wide half lets go to a fixed share of the desk, not to a size it had before it was tiled (no size is kept for a half). (Taken by its row, a window too big to carry comes scaled down, `carrySize`.)
- Keyboard: Tile, Cascade and Arrange have shortcuts (⌘⌥T, ⌘⌥C, ⌘⌥L); there are none yet for snapping or cycling windows, nor for a window's own controls (close it with ⌘W as any tab). The address palette does not list Tile or Cascade. A row takes Enter or Space as it always does (its tab in use: out, or to the top). ⇧⌫ closes a tab by its row only under the pointer, not a keyboard-focused row. With the pointer resting on a row of the space (after clicking one, say), ⇧⌫ typed into a page closes that tab rather than deleting.
- Menus: the desk's entries act on the row right-clicked, whatever else is selected.
- Rearranging is the sidebar's own: a window (rather than its row) cannot be dropped on a space's row to move its tab there, and a space's row cannot be dragged onto the desk.
- A drag's native layer relays where the real pointer is: in e2e runs, whose pointer is Playwright's, a real cursor resting over the window pulls a drag to it (a row comes out as a window; the first desk spec's Shift grab of a page never lit its tile, a long-standing flake). The first desk spec and the sidebar-as-dock one call `clearOfCursor` first, which moves the window off the real cursor; the others do not yet.
- App icons: main still reads the icon a page declares (`appIconUrl`, `src/main/app-icon.ts`), but since the dock gave way to the sidebar nothing in the shell draws it; rows show favicons.
- Where a clicked tab goes is read once, when it comes out; nothing re-tiles the desk around it afterwards.
- A mask put back shows the same part of the page's box as before, whatever the page shows there now (a page that changed its layout, or a tab that has gone to another page).
- On a masked page, what the page positions from its own idea of where an element is lands off the region: a `<select>`'s native popup, and a page's own JS wheel handling (a map zooming about the pointer) sees an unmapped point. Clicks, hovers, drags, the wheel's scrolling and the keyboard are right. Scrolling the page scrolls it under the region, as it would in the whole window.
- The region is the same part of the page's box, re-aimed as the page scrolls. A page zoomed (⌘+) while masked is not followed, and neither is a page whose layout moves the content under the region.
- Editing a mask shows the page as a still: it is not live while the region is adjusted.
- Minimized: scrolling a zoomed page with the wheel or a trackpad is not covered by the tests (synthetic wheel events scroll no page in these runs); Chromium maps the emulated view's input itself, clicks included. Only the shelf's top window is live where it peeks: one overlapped by the next, or under the Bar, is its still (refreshed every 3 s like any covered window). The shelf is one row from beside the sidebar, however many are parked (they close up to fit, not wrap), and it may run under the Bar, which covers them there. A window collapsed from minimized comes back out of the sidebar at its own size. There is no keyboard shortcut to minimize, and the agent sees minimized windows but does not minimize them itself. Shrinking into the shelf, the window shows the still it had; its page is already laid out at the zoom by the time it lands, and comes up so.
- Unmasking shows only the region, with the page area around it blank, until a still of the whole page arrives (a capture, ~100 ms); on the first frames the new title bar covers the top few pixels of the region.
- (Until 2026-10-09 a desk was left only by the More card's Leave the desk or Toggle desk, and Toggle desk with no desk up opened the tab in use's desk, a page's own among them; loose and page spaces were made for a tab only when it was chosen on a desk, and off the desk a page's space's other tabs had no row. All of that went with the mode: `docs/spaces.md`.) The rail shows no screen share's card (one that begins says so as a notice). On the rail the window's buttons are hidden: closing or minimizing the app's window is ⌘W/⌘M, or the whole sidebar (⌘S) brings them back. The rail has no preview of a tab beyond its tooltip.

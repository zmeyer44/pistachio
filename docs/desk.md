# The desk

An experiment in a different browser. A tab group can open as a **desk**: its tabs are free windows, dragged, thrown, resized and stacked inside the page area, and the group's tabs sit down the side as icons in a dock, as apps sit in the macOS Dock on the left of the screen. It asks what browsing feels like when a tab is a window you place rather than a button that fills the screen. It ships with switchable **variants**, because the point is to feel the options side by side.

Desktop only (it needs native page views to move). Branch `desk`.

## 1. What a person does

**Open it.** Hover a tab group's row in the sidebar and press the window button (or right-click the group → *Open as desk*; the top layout's group chip has the same menu item). The sidebar goes first: pinned or compact, it slides away as the compact sidebar does, taking the macOS window buttons with it, and the page reflows to fill the row. Then the page in view lifts off into a window without a flicker, and the dock slides in from the left. The group's last arrangement comes back if there is one. The sidebar stays away, with no edge to bring it out, until the desk is left.

**The dock.** A glass shelf down the desk's leading edge, 60px wide, with every tab in the group as an icon:
- *The icon* is the tab as an app: the large icon the site declares for itself (its `apple-touch-icon`, or a declared icon of 96px or more, an SVG counting as any size), full bleed; otherwise its favicon on a tile; the shell's own pages (home, the brief, a note) their own mark; failing all of those, its initial on a tile in a colour of its own. Main reads the app icon from the page's `<link>`s (`src/main/app-icon.ts`, `BrowserTabInfo.appIconUrl`).
- *A dot* beside a tab whose window is out on the desk, as beside a running app; the window in use has a longer one in the group's colour.
- *Hover* an icon: it grows away from the edge, and a preview of the tab (its latest still, title, site, and whether it is on the desk or in the dock) opens beside it, gliding from icon to icon.
- *Click* an icon: a window that is out comes to the top and takes the keyboard. A tab that is not out comes out, flying from its icon, where the desk's layout has room for it (`placeNewWindow`): on an empty desk, a comfortable window in the middle; on a **tiled** desk (every window a tile, lying along two of the desk's edges or a cell of a tiled arrangement, none overlapping), the hole it has left if that is room enough (beside a half, the other half; beside three quarters, the fourth), and with no such hole the window in use splits along its longer side and gives the new one its second half, so the desk stays tiled (one window filling the desk becomes the two halves); among windows set down freely, the size windows come out at, where it covers the least of them.
- *Drag* an icon: it comes away in hand as the icon (the place it came from stays, faintly). Pulled 24px clear of the dock it becomes the tab's window, held by its title bar near the bar's leading end, so the window reaches out across the desk from the hand. A tab not out comes out at the size windows come out at, growing out of the icon under the pointer. A window already out flies from where it was to the hand, its title bar under the pointer, at its own size, or, too big to carry (a full-height half, the whole desk), scaled down to fit in two thirds of the desk's width and four fifths of its height, its shape kept (`carrySize`). From there it is any window in hand: magnets, edge zones, Shift's snap, a throw, the drop rail. Let go while it is still an icon and nothing changes.
- *Below a divider*: tile, cascade, every tab out tiled, **Feel** (the variants, in a menu beside the dock), and leave (×). Each says what it does in a tooltip beside the dock (shadcn's Base UI Tooltip, `components/ui/tooltip.tsx`): the first after 350ms, its neighbours at once. Feel shows none while its menu is open, and none show while the dock is busy or aside.

**Windows.**
- *Move* by the frame (title bar, tab or handle, depending on the frame variant). A press raises the window; a click gives its page the keyboard.
- *Let go of the whole desk*: a window that fills the desk (90% of it or more both ways) shrinks as soon as it is dragged, the way a maximized window lets go of the screen when its title bar is pulled. It goes back to the size it had before it filled the desk, or else to the size windows come out of the dock at (58% × 74% of the desk), and it springs to that size around the point the pointer holds (a title bar stays under the pointer). Small enough to carry to a half or a quarter.
- *Hold Shift to snap* (any window in hand: moved by its frame, grabbed from its page, or dragged out of the dock). The desk reads the pointer in thirds and lights the tile the window will land in: the corners are quarters, the sides halves, the top middle the whole desk, and the middle and bottom middle a comfortable window in the centre (the Snap throw's map, shown before letting go). The tile holds until the pointer is 16px past a line, so it never flickers. Let go and the window springs into the tile, whatever the Throw variant or the pointer's speed; a fling at the dock while aiming does not put it away. Shift can go down or up at any moment of the drag, with the pointer still: the tile lights or goes out at once. While a tile is lit (Shift's, or an edge zone's), a carried window that is drawn thins a little so the tile reads through it.
- *Hold Shift and drag anywhere on a window*, including on its live page. The press never reaches the page. The key is a variant: Shift, Option, Command or off. With Shift (the default) the grab starts in snap mode, since Shift is held; let go of Shift once the window is in hand to place it freely.
- *Stick*: an edge within 14px of the desk's edge, or of a neighbour's edge (beside it with the gap, or flush), snaps onto it and a guide line shows. The dock's edge is one of these edges too, so a window pushed to it still lines up beside the dock.
- *Behind the dock*: the dock floats over the desk, and a window may lie behind it, as a window can slide under the Dock. Moved, thrown or resized, a window may go into the dock's column, as far as the desk's leading edge, and stays where it is let go (carry it by its title bar well along the bar, so the pointer stays clear of the leading edge's zones and the drop rail). Tiles, filling the desk, the arrangements and where a new window comes out still keep clear of the dock. A window behind the dock shows its still under the dock's glass, like a window behind another window (a live page would paint over the dock). The one exception is the window in use. Click into a window behind the dock, or choose its tab from the dock, the sidebar or a shortcut, and the dock slides away, as it does for a carry, so that window's page can be live. The dock comes back when the pointer comes to its place (its column, level with its shelf), when another window is used, or when that window is moved out from behind it; while it is back, the window under it is a still again. A window only let go behind the dock is not chosen by that, so the dock comes back over it.
- *Edge zones*: push the pointer into the desk's left or right edge for that half, the top edge for the whole desk, and the corners for quarters. The zone lights in the group's colour; let go and the window fills it.
- *Throw*: release while moving and what happens depends on the Throw variant (below).
- *Resize* from any edge or corner. Edges stick the same way.
- *Double-click* the frame, or press its ⤢ button, to fill the desk and back.
- *The dock steps aside*: as soon as a window is carried (moved, grabbed from its page, or dragged out of the dock), the dock's shelf slides off the desk's edge, clipped there so it never passes over the sidebar, and it slides back once the window is let go.
- *The leading edge, read in depth*: so that no two targets overlap, the edge a carried window is pushed into offers three things at three depths. The desk's first 30px (a deeper band than the other edges' 18px) are the split-screen snaps: the left half, with the top-left and bottom-left quarters at the band's ends (96px). Past the desk's edge, in the column the dock slid out of or further out over the sidebar, a **drop rail** stands in the dock's place: the dock's own glass, cut in two by a hairline, **Minimize** above, taking most of the height, and **Close** below, the smaller segment (28% of the height, 120–220px), where the Dock keeps its Trash. The rail slides in from behind the desk's edge once the pointer is within 180px of it. Nothing on it has colour until a release would go somewhere: then that segment fills with its colour (the group's for Minimize, red for Close), the hairline goes, and its mark (a filled badge, a little larger) follows the pointer up and down the segment, so the target is always beside the hand. The segment chosen stays lit as the rail slides away on letting go. Over the rail, a drawn window shrinks and fades into it (the lit segment stands for it) and comes back if the pointer leaves.
- *Minimize*: let go on the Minimize segment, fling the window hard at the desk's edge, or press its − button: it goes to its icon as the dock slides back, and the icon gives a little bounce as it takes it.
- *Close*: let go on the Close segment: the window goes into it, then its tab is closed (Reopen closed tab brings it back). A window dragged out of the dock and let go on Close closes that tab too.

**Mask: keep part of a page.** For when only part of a page matters (a chart, a ticker, a player): shrinking the window would not do, because the page is responsive and its content reflows, shrinks or moves.
- *Choose*: the window's crop button (in its frame's controls; a web page only, not the shell's own pages). The page freezes and dims, the cursor becomes a crosshair, and a drag draws the region to keep, as a screenshot's selection is drawn, with its size shown in a corner. Let go and the window becomes that region. A drag under 16px, Escape, or the crop button again calls it off.
- *Masked*: the window is the region, where it was on the page and at the size it was, with the bare frame's handle above it; the rest of the window fades from around it. It stays live and usable: main keeps the page laying out at the box it had, shows only the region, and maps the pointer on it to the page's own point, so a click on a masked video plays or pauses it, hovering shows the player's controls, a drag scrubs, and the keyboard works as it always does. The player's own fullscreen button makes the page itself on the whole screen, and it is masked again when it leaves fullscreen. The handle's controls, shown on hover or while the window is in use, are Unmask and Put away.
- *Resize* from an edge or corner: the region keeps its shape and scales like a picture, not a page. The page never reflows; the region is re-rendered sharp at the new size once it is let go (while it is dragged, its still stretches). A region may be as small as 16px a side.
- *Move* by its handle, or with the grab key held anywhere on it, as any window. A press on the region is the page's.
- *Kept out of the layouts*: filling the desk, the edge zones, Shift's snap and the Snap throw would stretch the picture, so they leave a masked window alone, as do Tile and Cascade; and a tab brought out of the dock never takes half of a masked window.
- *Unmask*: the whole window grows back around the region, at the page's own scale, the region staying where it is. Leaving the desk, the window in use comes back as the whole pane.
- *Kept*: masks are saved with the desk's arrangement (on this device, as the arrangement is). Reopened, a masked window that comes out of the dock comes out masked, where it was and at its size, its region's shape kept whatever the desk's shape is now. A masked window whose tab is the one in view when the desk opens lifts off whole, as any first window does, lands as the window it was cut from, and is cropped back to its region (the rest fading, as when it was first masked) once a still of the page as it then stands is up, then goes where it was left. A masked tab that slept while the desk was away gets its mask back when it wakes.

**Arrange** (buttons at the foot of the dock): tile every window, cascade them, or bring every tab out tiled.

**Leave**: the × at the foot of the dock. The window in use grows back into the whole surface and becomes the pane again; the rest fly home. Then the sidebar slides back in beside the pane, and the window buttons return (a compact sidebar comes back hidden). Choosing a tab outside the group (sidebar, ⌘T) also leaves the desk, straight away. The group being closed, ungrouped or left in another Space ends it too.

## 2. Variants

Under **Feel** at the foot of the dock (a menu opens beside it). Click a row to cycle it; Shift-click goes back. Choices persist on this device.

| Axis | Choices | What changes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Throw | **Glide** · Snap · Free | Glide: momentum that fades (τ 0.3 s); meeting an edge ends the coast in a rebound that settles flush against it. Snap: every release lands in a tile, read from where the throw would carry the pointer, on the desk in thirds (corners → quarters, sides → halves, top middle → full, middle → a centred window). Free: stays where it is dropped (magnets only). |
| Spring | **Snappy** · Bouncy · Smooth | The spring every settle rides: response 0.26 s / damping 0.86, 0.42 / 0.56, 0.48 / 1. |
| In hand | **Lifted** · Live | Lifted: a carried window shows its still. It lifts (scale 1.035, deeper shadow), tilts with the swing about the point it is held by, shrinks when held over the dock, and may travel anywhere. Live: the page stays live and flat in hand, and stays on the desk. |
| Frame | **Title bar** · Tab · Bare | A title bar above the page; a folder tab on its shoulder; or just the page with a handle pill above it. |
| Grab key | **⇧ Shift** · ⌥ Option · ⌘ Command · Off | The key that grabs a window from anywhere on it. Shift is also the snap key (not a variant), so a Shift grab starts snapping; Option or Command grab without snapping, and Shift added mid-drag snaps. |

Reduced motion replaces every spring with a quick critically damped one and drops the tilt.

## 3. How it works

A desk window is two things drawn by two processes: its **frame** is the shell's DOM, and its **page** is the tab's native view, placed by main over the hole the frame leaves. A native view paints over everything the shell draws, so:

- **Covered windows are drawn by the shell.** A window with anything stacked above it shows its *still* where the page would be, so the stacking is the DOM's and always right. Only uncovered windows go live (`uncoveredWindows` in `lib/desk/geometry.ts`). An armed zone counts as a cover, so the pages under it give way. So does the dock's shelf where it stands (`setDockShelf`, its resting box, whatever its slide), and the drop rail while it shows. So does whatever the dock draws over the desk: the icon in hand, and the preview, the Feel menu and the tools' tooltips, which the dock declares with `setCover(key, rect)` and shows only once the engine lists the key in `clearCovers` (no live page left under it). The tooltips share one cover, the band beside the tools, held 200ms past a tooltip closing so that moving from tool to tool the pages there stay stills; a tooltip is rendered at once but transparent until the band is clear. The preview's cover is the whole band beside the dock, held for as long as an icon is hovered, so moving from icon to icon the preview never waits.
- **Transformed windows are drawn too.** A native view cannot scale or rotate, so a lifted, flying or (in Lifted) settling window is its still.
- **A still must be fresh before it stands in.** Each window records when it began wanting one, and only a still requested after that (and painted) may replace the live page. Until then the page stays up. Stills are captured at the start of every gesture for all live windows, so they are ready in time.
- **Live pages stack in the windows' order.** `BrowserLayout.stacked`: main re-adds the shown views bottom to top when the order changes (a raise), so even during the moments above the pages overlap correctly.

The motion is imperative (`components/desk/desk-engine.ts`): rects, springs, the coast and the tilt run per frame outside React, write transforms straight to the elements, and report the live pages' boxes to main (`setLayout`, deduped). React re-renders only when a window is added, raised, becomes drawn or live, or starts or ends a flight. Gestures use the drag layer (`lib/pane-drag.ts`), as pane resizes do, so the pointer is never lost over a page.

**Grabbing a live page** (`@pistachio/shell-contracts/desk`). The shell tells main which views are desk windows and which key grabs (`setDesk`). Main follows the key from every view's key events (`noteDeskKey`), because the mouse events `before-mouse-event` delivers carry no modifiers. A press on a desk page with the key held is taken from the page, the shell hears `onDeskGrab`, and the rest of the press is relayed on the drag-sample channel. Points are anchored to the screen at the press, since the view moves under the pointer. While the key is held, every desk page shows an open hand (a user-origin `!important` cursor rule). A plain press on a desk page makes it the active tab.

**The sidebar is put away while a desk is up** (`layouts/SidebarLayout.tsx`). `toggleDesk` opens with `afterSidebar` in the sidebar layout, so the desk store holds the group as `opening` rather than `groupId`, and nothing mounts yet. The layout treats either as `away`: the column hides, the slot narrows to the compact edge's width (the desk keeps that 10px clearance from the window's edge), and the compact edge and its reveal watch are off. A pinned slot has no width transition of its own (it follows resize drags frame by frame), so `useAwaySlide` gives it the compact clock for the slide away and back. When the slot's width transition ends (at once if it had nothing to narrow, or under reduced motion), `sidebarGone` turns `opening` into `groupId` and the desk mounts on a pane that already fills the row. Leaving needs no ordering: the desk hands the row back to panes (`finishLeave`), `away` goes false, and the sidebar slides back beside them. Main hears it as `ShellState.sidebarAway`, which hides the traffic lights (they sit in the sidebar's toolbar) and keeps the compact edge's native watch off. While away, a screen share that begins says so as a notice, as with the compact sidebar hidden.

**Entering and leaving are seamless on purpose.** The first window starts as a window whose *page* is exactly the pane's box, so main's view does not move. It shrinks into place only once its still is up. Leaving runs the same path backwards, and the surface goes back to panes only after that window is live at the pane's box again.

**The dock and the window in use behind it** (`#yielding`, `DeskView.dockAside`). The engine keeps two boxes: `#usable`, the desk beside the dock, where layouts go, and `#reach`, the whole stage, where a window may be (moves, coasts, rests, resizes, the Live variant's rubber band). The window the person chooses (a click into it, its icon, an outside selection that `activeChanged` sees as not the desk's own raise) is `#asideFor`; while that window is the one in use, has a live page and overlaps the shelf, the dock steps aside. The shelf's cover stays up for the 240ms the shelf takes to slide away, so the page never paints over it on the way. Coming back runs the other way round: the cover goes up, the page gives way to its still, and only then (`dockAside` false) does the shelf slide in. The dock stands while the pointer is at its place (the shell's own pointer events on the stage, `notePointer`) or its Feel menu is open (`holdDock`). Over the live page the shell hears no pointer, so while the dock is aside the shell tells main where its place is (`DeskState.dock`), and main, which sees every mouse move on a desk page (`before-mouse-event`), says once when the pointer comes there (`DeskPageInput` `"dock"`). A still asked for too soon after the last one (`STILL_RETRY_MS`) schedules a render for when it may be asked (`#renderIn`), and a window that has just begun to want a still asks at once, so a cover never waits for good on a still nobody asks for.

**Masks** (`DeskMask` in `@pistachio/shell-contracts/desk`; the engine's `startMask`, `applyMask`, `unmask`; `MaskSelector` in `DeskWindow.tsx`; main's desk-mask section). Chromium can show part of a page without the page knowing: `Emulation.setDeviceMetricsOverride` over the tab's debugger session holds the page's layout at a given size (the box it had when masked), and its `viewport` draws a region of the document into the view's top-left at a page scale. So a masked window's view is just the region's box, its page still lays out at its old width (no responsive reflow), and a resize changes only the scale, which Chromium re-rasterizes (sharp, unlike a stretched bitmap). What had to be learned by trying it in Electron 43 (a standalone probe):
- The viewport's offset is in document px, and Chromium paints only what lies inside the page's own viewport: a page scrolled away from the region shows blank. So the region is kept as a part of the page's BOX, and re-aimed by the page's scroll, which a script in an isolated world reports through `Runtime.addBinding` (the page cannot see the binding), installed for new documents with `Page.addScriptToEvaluateOnNewDocument` (a reload re-reports) and for the current one in a world from `Page.createIsolatedWorld`. Offsets are the shell's px (DIP); the page's scroll is CSS px, times the zoom factor.
- Input is not mapped: a click on the view lands where it would on the unmasked page. So main maps it (`#forwardMaskedMouse`): every mouse event on a masked view that goes on to the page (after the desk's own grab key has had its say) is stopped and re-sent with `sendInputEvent` at the page's point, the region's corner plus the point on the view over the scale. Re-sent events are delivered at points outside the view's (smaller) box, and `before-mouse-event` sees them synchronously, so a flag lets them through. Those events carry no modifiers, so the keys down (followed in `noteDeskKey`) and the buttons held are put back on them: Shift- and ⌘-clicks stay those, and a move with a button held stays a drag. The wheel never passes `before-mouse-event`; the compositor scrolls what is under the pointer as drawn, which is right. A page gone fullscreen has its override taken off until it comes back (`#suspendDeskMask`).
- `capturePage` of a masked view, shown or hidden, is the region at the view's size. Main sizes a masked view that is down to the size its window shows it at (`DeskState.masks[].width/height`), so its still is right, and tags stills with the mask they show (`PaneStill.mask`, `deskMaskKey`). The engine keeps which mask each still shows: a still of the whole page (taken before masking) is cropped to the region with CSS; one of another mask stands for nothing.
- Order matters, or the page shows at the wrong scale, or reflows at the region's size, for a frame: the shell reports the desk (with masks) before the layout; main places a masked view only once the override for its size is in place (`#placeMaskedView`; the window's still, painted under every live page, shows meanwhile); taking a mask off, main takes the view down and gives it back its old box before clearing the override; a still waits for any of that (`#deskMaskWork`).
- The session is shared with forced focus (`debugger-hold.ts`): each holds it, and it is detached only when neither does.
- Kept across desks: `SavedDeskWindow.mask` (checked with `isDeskMask` on reading; a bad one leaves the window whole). The engine's `start` gives a window from the dock its mask at once, and asks for the dock's stills only after the first report has told main of the masks, so they come back as the region. The first window carries the mask it is to get back (`maskWanted`): landed whole, it asks for a still, and `#putMaskBack` masks it once that still is painted (or after 1.5 s without one) and sends it where it was saved. A sleeping tab's page is a new one when it wakes, so main drops the mask state of the old page and sets up the new one (`#syncDeskMasks` from the wake).

**From icon to window** (`pressIcon`, `#beginIconDrag`, `#takeInHand`). A press on an icon is a click until it travels 4px; then the gesture is `"icon"`: the engine moves a ghost of the icon (`attachGhost`) and nothing on the desk changes. The pointer passing 24px beyond the dock's edge turns the gesture into a window in hand. A tab not out gets a new window, `"spawn"` (drawn throughout, and sent back into the dock if let go over it), whose scale starts at the icon's width and springs up with the lift. A window already out becomes a `"move"`, and a `lag` (its offset from where the hand holds it) springs to zero, so it flies to the hand; magnets wait until it arrives. Either way the hand holds the bar at `pinTop` (half the bar's height), at `clamp(pointer − desk's left, 28, width / 2)` along it.

**The drop rail** (`dockDrops`, `dockDropAt` in `lib/desk/geometry.ts`; `DropRail` in `DeskDock.tsx`). The engine lays the two segments out from the stage's height (`view.drops`), and while a window is carried it tells the dock whether the rail should show (`dropsShown`) and which segment a release would go to (`dockDrop`); it writes the pointer's height to the rail as `--pointer-y` for the lit mark to follow, without a render. The shelf and the rail slide behind the desk's edge by an `overflow-x: clip` box, not a `clip-path`: an element under a clip-path has nothing behind it for `backdrop-filter` to blur, and the glass goes flat. The dock hides its shelf whenever the view's gesture is a carry (`move` or `spawn`). A window put away flies to where its icon rests, not where it is while the shelf slides (`#iconRect` subtracts the shelf's current transform), so it meets the icon as the dock comes back. Closing is the host's `close` (the store's `closeTab`), called once the window has gone into the Close segment.

**Shift, the snap key.** The engine keeps whether Shift is down from every source that knows: a press's own event, each pointer sample (the drag layer's and the shell's pointer events say; `DragSample.shift`), the shell's own key events, and main, which follows Shift from every view's keys (`noteDeskKey`, the same relay as the grab key) and tells the shell when it changes while a desk is up (`onDeskShift`). The key events are what make a Shift pressed or let go with the pointer standing still count at once. A sample's reading is passed on once (`lib/pane-drag.ts`), never repeated, so a stale one cannot outvote a later key event. While the drag layer holds the pointer, main's double-Shift detector stands down, so trying Shift twice mid-drag never bookmarks the page.

Main also keeps desk windows from being suspended as idle (`suspendTab` skips any tab in the layout).

State: which group has its desk up, the variants, and each group's arrangement (as fractions of the desk, bottom to top, each masked window with its `DeskMask`). These are the shell's, in `lib/desk/store.ts` and this device's localStorage (`pistachio.desk.v1`). Main never stores an arrangement.

## 5. Not done / open questions

- The feel numbers (coast τ, restitution, magnet reach, lift, tilt, spring presets) are first guesses, tuned against screenshots rather than a hand on a trackpad.
- **Live** in hand: the frame (DOM) and the page (native) are separate compositors, so during a fast drag the title bar may run a frame ahead of the page. Not measured.
- A covered window's still is refreshed every 3 s; it is not a live page.
- A tab dragged from the sidebar onto the desk still offers the old split zones; the desk does not take sidebar drags yet.
- Split groups among the desk's tabs are ignored while the desk is up (they come back on leaving).
- With Shift as the grab key, a desk page loses Shift-click (extend selection, open in new window), and every page grab starts in snap mode. Option or Command avoid both.
- Shift snaps moves only. A resize with Shift held is a plain resize (no snapping of edges to fractions of the desk, no kept aspect ratio).
- The drop rail only takes a carried window; a tab dragged from the sidebar has nowhere on it to go. Flinging a window at the leading edge minimizes it; there is no fling to close.
- Dragged by its title bar, only a window filling the desk lets go of its size; one in a half or a quarter keeps it. (Taken by its icon, a window too big to carry comes scaled down, `carrySize`.)
- Keyboard: no shortcuts for snapping or cycling windows yet. An icon in the dock takes Enter or Space (bring out, or to the top); there is no arrowing between icons.
- The dock does not magnify its neighbours as the Dock can (only the hovered icon grows), and does not reorder: dragging an icon within the dock and letting go changes nothing. With more tabs than fit its height, the icons scroll.
- App icons: only what the page declares in `<link>`s. A web app manifest's icons are not read, nor the conventional `/apple-touch-icon.png` a site serves without declaring it. An `http:` site's icons do not show at all (the shell loads images over https only), as with its favicon elsewhere.
- Where a clicked tab goes is read once, when it comes out; nothing re-tiles the desk around it afterwards.
- Behind the dock, a window not in use is a still, refreshed every 3 s like any covered window. The window in use can never be seen live with the dock over it: the dock steps aside instead. A native view cannot be clipped to show a page with the dock over part of it.
- The pointer brings the dock back only at the dock's place, level with its shelf. Pushing into the leading edge above or below the shelf does not. A window let go behind the dock is the window in use, but it takes the keyboard only once it is chosen (clicked into).
- A mask put back shows the same part of the page's box as before, whatever the page shows there now (a page that changed its layout, or a tab that has gone to another page).
- On a masked page, what the page positions from its own idea of where an element is lands off the region: a `<select>`'s native popup, and a page's own JS wheel handling (a map zooming about the pointer) sees an unmapped point. Clicks, hovers, drags, the wheel's scrolling and the keyboard are right. Scrolling the page scrolls it under the region, as it would in the whole window.
- The region is the same part of the page's box, re-aimed as the page scrolls. A page zoomed (⌘+) while masked is not followed, and neither is a page whose layout moves the content under the region.
- The region is chosen once: no handles to adjust it after. Unmask and mask again.
- Unmasking shows the window's page area blank for a moment, until a still of the whole page arrives.
- With the sidebar away, nothing but the dock's × (or a shortcut that chooses a tab outside the group, such as ⌘T or the tab switcher) leaves the desk. A screen share that was already running shows no indicator while the sidebar is away (only one that begins meanwhile says so). In the top layout nothing is put away.
- Opening waits for the sidebar's slide (180ms) before the desk enters.
- Stepping aside, the dock waits 240ms for its shelf to slide away before the page goes live, so the keyboard reaches a window chosen behind the dock that much later.

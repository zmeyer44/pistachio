# Live share

A space shared live: the person on the Mac makes a link for one space, sends it, and whoever opens it in an ordinary browser sees that space's desk as it is this moment — its windows where they stand, their pages moving, the host's pointer — and is in it with them: their own cursor, a pen to draw over it, their face and voice, and, where the host allows it, their hands on a window. The bar is Tuple's: pairing that feels like sitting at one desk, not like watching a video of one.

Desktop hosts, browsers join. Builds on `docs/desk.md` (windows, the stage, stills), `docs/spaces.md` (a space is a tab group in code), `docs/anonymous-accounts.md` (a signed-out Mac has an account). This is not `docs/shared-browser.md`: that moves one person's browsing between their devices; this puts several people in one desk at once.

Normative: where code and this document disagree, one of them is a bug.

## 1. What a person does

### The host (desktop)

**Share.** *Share live…* on a space's menu (the rail tile, the card's ⋯, the whole sidebar's header menu), or `/share` in the Bar, starts a live share of that space and opens the **Live card** with its link already on the clipboard ("Link copied"). One live share per Mac at a time: sharing a second space asks to stop the first.

**The Live card** (a desk side card, like the More card): the space's name with a live dot and how long it has been live; the link (Copy); *Who can join* — *Ask me* (default) or *Anyone with the link*; *Guests can draw* (on by default); the space's windows, each with *Guests can use* (off by default), and over them, with two or more such windows, *All windows* (on once guests may use every one; turned on, they may use the rest; turned off, none — `allWindowsUse`); the people — each guest with their colour, mic and camera state, a ⋯ to remove them — and, above them, anyone knocking (*Admit* / *Deny*) and anyone asking to use a window (*Allow* / *Deny*); the host's own mic, camera, background and devices; a Chat tab; and *Stop sharing*.

**Kept for the next share.** The card's own settings — who can join, guests can draw, the host's mic, camera, background and devices — are kept as the host sets them (`<userData>/live-share.json`, `main/live/live-prefs.ts`; this Mac's alone, never synced) and a share starts with them. Only the host's choices are kept, not what came of them: a mic that would not come on stays kept on. Control makes every share knocking, so a kept *Anyone with the link* goes to control as the share goes live (put back, and said why, if control refuses). A device is kept with its label: its id may differ next session, so a share starts on the system's default and takes the kept one up — by id, else by label — once the live-host app lists it, unless the host picked one of that kind first; one no longer there leaves the default. Which windows guests may use is not kept (windows are the share's own).

**On the desk while live.** Over the shared space's desk the host sees, drawn above every window (pages included):
- each guest's pointer, an arrow in their colour with their name, moving smoothly;
- what anyone draws, and pings (a ripple where someone double-clicked with the pointer tool, or ⌥-clicked);
- reactions floating up from the face of whoever sent them;
- a thin outline in the guest colour around a window guests may use, with the name of the guest using it at the moment;
- the faces: one bubble per person with a camera on (an initial on their colour otherwise), stacked at the top trailing corner of the desk, a ring while they speak, a slashed mic when muted; draggable to any edge, and hovering one shows its controls;
- the **Live pill** at the top centre of the desk: dot, "Live", the count of guests, and a stop button; pressing it opens the Live card.

**Draw.** The Live card's pen (or `/draw`) turns the desk into a drawing surface for the host: pen, highlighter or laser, a colour, Clear. Escape or the pen again gives the desk back.

**Elsewhere.** Passing to another space does not end the share. The call goes on (faces, voices, chat, the pill), the shared space's windows keep streaming as they were left, and the guests see "Zach is in another space". Guests' hands are off every window until the host comes back. Pointers and ink belong to the shared desk, so they are not drawn over another space.

**End.** *Stop sharing* (card, pill, the space's menu: *Stop sharing live*), closing the shared space, quitting the app, or a Profile switch that takes the space away ends it. Every guest sees "The share has ended". The link is dead from then on; sharing again makes a new one.

### The guest (browser)

**Open the link** (`https://www.pistachio.run/live/<token>`). The lobby says whose space it is ("Zach is sharing *Launch plan*"; the name is the one the shell gives, else read off the Mac's own name — `scutil --get ComputerName`, "Zach’s MacBook Pro" — since the device name made from `hostname()` has lost the apostrophe), asks for a name (remembered), shows a camera preview with a mic level, mic and camera toggles, a background choice (none, blur, a few built-in images, an uploaded picture), device pickers, and *Ask to join* (*Join* when anyone with the link may). Asking waits: "Waiting for Zach to let you in", with Cancel. Denied: "Zach didn't let you in". A dead link: "This share has ended" (or "isn't live").

**In the room** (after `joining`; the flow also ends in `left` and `replaced` — "Use this tab" when the same guest opened the room twice). The page is the host's desk:
- the windows at the host's places, scaled to fit the browser window (the whole desk is always in view; the stage keeps the host's aspect, letterboxed), each a rounded card with a label just above it (favicon, title, the site in mono, a dot on the window in use, a hand on a usable one, who is using it; *Ask to use* and fill on hover) — above, not inside, since the desk's default Drawer frame is all page; its page live; the window in use on the host is marked;
- everyone's pointers including the host's, ink, pings, reactions;
- the faces in a column at the trailing edge (collapsible), the speaker ringed;
- a toolbar at the foot: mic, camera, background (and devices in each one's menu); pointer, pen, highlighter, laser, colour, clear mine; reactions; chat (with an unread badge); people; leave. Keys: M mic, V camera, P pointer, D pen, H highlighter, L laser, Esc pointer, ⌘/ chat.
- banners for what changed: "Zach is in another space", "Zach let you use *GitHub*", "Reconnecting…", "Waiting for Zach" (the host gone from the room a while, §7) — one line at a time (the one that began last), in the top bar, or in a row of its own under it where the bar is too narrow (the stage starts below that row), so a banner never covers a window's label; the line saying the guest asked to use a window goes when the host's answer comes ("Zach let you use *GitHub*", or "Zach said no to *GitHub*").

**Double-click a window's strip** to bring that window up to fill the stage locally (Esc, or the strip again, goes back). Nothing moves on the host.

**Use a window.** On a window the host allows, the pointer is the guest's hand: clicks, drags, scrolling, typing and paste go to the page on the Mac (the window in use on the Mac follows, as if the host had clicked it — one keyboard, shared). A terminal the host allows takes the guest's keys as the host's own — commands, Ctrl+C, Escape, the arrows, a paste (bracketed, so nothing runs before Enter) — and their wheel scrolls its scrollback (§6.6); there ⇧Escape, not Escape, gives the keyboard back. Elsewhere a page or a terminal offers *Ask to use*, which asks the host; the shell's other windows (home, notes, a brief, a document) are only watched.

**Leave** at any time; reload rejoins without knocking again while the guest is still admitted.

## 2. The shape of it

```
 Mac (host)                                                     Browser (guest)
 ┌───────────────────────────────────────────────┐              ┌──────────────────────────┐
 │ shell renderer: desk engine ──setLiveDesk──┐  │              │ www /live/[token]        │
 │   Live card, menus, /share, badges         │  │              │  lobby → room            │
 │ main: LiveShareService ◄───────────────────┘  │   HTTPS      │  LiveStage (videos at    │
 │   control client ─────────────────────────────┼──► control ◄─┼─  window rects), cursors,│
 │   tab views ──getMediaSourceId──┐             │   /v1/live   │  ink, faces, toolbar     │
 │   sendInputEvent ◄─ guest input │             │              └────────────┬─────────────┘
 │ live-host window (child, click-through)       │                           │ WebRTC
 │   LiveHostApp: LiveKit room ────────────────────────► LiveKit SFU ◄──────┘
 │   publishes one video track per window,       │      (tracks + data)
 │   camera, mic; draws cursors/ink/faces/pill   │
 └───────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

- **Media and data go through a LiveKit SFU** (self-hosted `livekit-server`, Apache-2.0; LiveKit Cloud works by configuration alone). The host uploads each stream once; the SFU fans out, with simulcast and adaptive layers, so a guest on a small window never decodes a 1440p page.
- **Each web-page window is its own video track**, captured from its tab's `WebContents` by Chromium's tab capture (`webContents.getMediaSourceId(liveHost)` + `getUserMedia({ chromeMediaSource: "tab" })`, as `DeskLivePicture` already does). Probed in Electron 43 (2026-10-17): capture runs at device pixels, needs no Screen Recording permission, keeps delivering while the view is hidden or even detached from the window, follows the page across a cross-site navigation (renderer swap), emits frames only when the page paints, and can carry that page's audio.
- **The desk itself is data**, not pixels: the shell's engine reports the shared space's windows (rects, order, focus, minimized) and main sends them to the room as `lv.desk` frames; guests rebuild the desk from them and place each track at its window. So guests see only the shared space's windows — never the sidebar, the Bar, another space, a menu or the address palette — and a window covered on the Mac is still whole for them.
- **The host's side of the room runs in the live-host window**: a transparent, click-through child `BrowserWindow` of the main window, exactly covering its content, which renders `LiveHostApp`. It is the only renderer that holds the LiveKit connection; it draws what must lie over native page views (cursors, ink, faces, the pill). Main is the hub between it and the shell, as for every chrome view.
- **Control** mints the link, holds the lobby (knock, admit, deny), mints LiveKit tokens, and removes people. It never sees media or data.

## 3. The room

**Names.** Room `live_<shareId>`. Host identity `host` (one per room; a second host connection replaces the first). Guest identity `g_<requestId>`. Participant `name` is the display name; participant `metadata` is JSON `{ "role": "host" | "guest", "color": "#rrggbb" }`, set by control in the token and never by the participant.

**Grants** (LiveKit `VideoGrant`): host — `roomJoin`, `canPublish`, `canPublishData`, `canSubscribe`, TTL 6 h, refreshed through control. Guest — `roomJoin`, `canPublish` with `canPublishSources: [camera, microphone]`, `canPublishData`, `canSubscribe`, `canUpdateOwnMetadata: false`, TTL 10 min (LiveKit refreshes a connected participant's token itself; a guest who reloads asks control again).

**Tracks.** Host: `win:<windowId>` (source `screen_share`, one per streamed window), `camera`, `microphone`. (No window audio: Chromium's tab-audio capture mutes the page for the host — §14.) Guest: `camera`, `microphone`. Window tracks are published H.264 with simulcast (full and half resolution), no `contentHint` (`"detail"` puts OpenH264 in its screen-content mode: 6–17 fps for a repainting 2560-px page), `degradationPreference = "maintain-resolution"`, at most 30 fps, at most 2560 px on the long side, bitrate scaled to pixels (≈ 2.5 Mbps at 1080p for the top layer). On the Mac they are encoded in software, OpenH264 in the live-host renderer, one layer or two: LiveKit's SFU forwards only the H.264 its answer puts first, Constrained Baseline, and Chromium never gives that profile to macOS's hardware encoder (it gives VideoToolbox Baseline, Main and High). Probed in Electron 43 with livekit-server 1.13 on a repainting 2244×952 window: two layers ran 16–22 fps at 35–50 ms a frame and 60–67% of a core (the half layer about 1 fps); one layer gained nothing (13–23 fps, 34–54 ms, 43–67%); the window sent in High ran on VideoToolbox at 30 fps, 7 ms a frame and 9% of a core, and the SFU dropped every packet of it (a payload type its answer did not put first). A window captured afresh (its page replaced) or resized keeps its publication: `LiveRoom.replaceWindowTrack` swaps the track on the sender and sets the layers' bitrates for the new size in place, so guests never lose its picture (unpublishing and publishing again left it blank for a second or for good). A full reconnect of LiveKit's own (its signal restarted) publishes every track again itself, on new publications of the same LiveKit tracks: the room holds each window by that track, not its publication, so a resize, a recapture or an unpublish acts on the publication out now (one LiveKit has yet to put out again, or failed to, goes out afresh), and a track let go of while LiveKit had it unpublished — a window's, a camera turned off — is taken down again as LiveKit puts it out. Cameras: 720p VP8 simulcast (LiveKit's defaults), with echo cancellation, noise suppression and auto gain on the mic. Guests run `adaptiveStream` at the screen's pixel density (at LiveKit's default a window on a Retina display gets its half layer and reads soft) and the host `dynacast`, so layers nobody watches are paused at the encoder.

**Who sees and hears whom.** The host's tracks are for the guests main admits (§6.1, §6.3: `setReceivers`, LiveKit's subscription permissions), and the host's room takes only their cameras and microphones (nobody else's plays on the Mac). LiveKit's subscription permissions are each publisher's own, so each guest sets its own: only the host and the guests the host lists (`lv.guests`, §4) may subscribe to its camera and microphone — the host alone until its first list comes, and from a reconnect until a fresh one does — and its room takes (subscribes to, plays, shows) the host's tracks and the listed guests' alone, letting go of anyone else's LiveKit subscribed it to as it was published. So someone in the room the host has not let in — a removed guest LiveKit has not dropped yet, or still has while control is down (§6.1), a token minted outside control — gets nobody's camera or microphone, a guest's no more than the host's. It costs a guest just let in one hop of the host's list before the others' cameras reach it (on one machine through a local SFU, a guest drew the other's camera 104–128 ms after the host sent the list, which reached both 4–13 ms after it went; on the guest page under load, 517–869 ms, the subscription's negotiation and first picture included, §12).

**At most 8 window tracks** at once; windows past that (by the stack, top first) are drawn as their frame with "Not streamed". Minimized windows are not streamed (their tracks unpublished, published again when they come back), nor are masked ones: a tab's capture is its whole page, what the mask cuts away included, so a masked window is its frame alone (`track: null`, `paused: true`) until its picture is cut to the region (§14), and it takes none of the eight. A shell-drawn window streams, cut from the shell's own picture (§6.6), and takes one.

**Colours.** `LIVE_COLORS` (eight, in `@pistachio/live-share`): the host is the first, guests take the next free one in join order.

## 4. Protocol

All in `@pistachio/live-share` (`src/protocol.ts`); every message is validated with its zod schema on receipt, and a message that fails is dropped (counted, never thrown). Messages ride LiveKit data packets as UTF-8 JSON with the topic below; anything over 12 KB (a sync) goes as a LiveKit text stream on the same topic. A receiver keeps a text stream only from a sender whose role may send its topic (judged before a byte is kept), at most two of one sender's at a time, each within its topic's limit and all together within one sync and one desk frame at their largest (8.5 MB); any other stream is read off and thrown away (LiveKit queues what nobody reads, and cancelling makes it throw). Coordinates are **stage-normalized** (0..1 of the host's stage width and height) unless named otherwise, so a host resize, which rescales the windows (`rescaleRect`), keeps ink and pointers on what they were over.

| Topic | From → to | Reliable | Body |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `lv.desk` | host → all | yes | `LiveDeskFrame` |
| `lv.cursor` | anyone → all | no | `{ x, y, tool, down? }` or `{ hidden: true }` |
| `lv.ink` | anyone → all | yes | `begin` / `add` / `end` / `erase` / `clear` |
| `lv.ping` | anyone → all | yes | `{ x, y }` |
| `lv.react` | anyone → all | yes | `{ emoji }` (one of `LIVE_REACTIONS`) |
| `lv.chat` | anyone → all | yes | `{ id, text }` (≤ 2000 chars) |
| `lv.hello` | guest → host | yes | `{}` — "send me the room" |
| `lv.sync` | host → one guest | yes | `{ desk, strokes, chat, grants }` |
| `lv.guests` | host → the guests it lets in | yes | `{ guests: identity[] }` — every guest the host lets in, the receiver too (≤ 64) |
| `lv.input` | guest → host | yes | `{ w, e: LiveInputEvent }` |
| `lv.ask` | guest → host | yes | `{ w }` — ask to use window `w` |
| `lv.grant` | host → all | yes | `{ w, user: identity | null }` — who is using window `w` now |
| `lv.refused` | host → one guest | yes | `{ w }` — the host said no to this guest's ask to use window `w` |
| `lv.knock` | control → host | yes | `{ requestId, name }` (sent by the server: no participant) |

Only `host` may send `lv.desk`, `lv.sync`, `lv.guests`, `lv.grant` and `lv.refused`; only the server (no participant) `lv.knock`; `lv.input` and `lv.ask` are acted on only from a guest. A receiver checks the sender before parsing the body. What a guest says to the host — `lv.hello`, `lv.input`, `lv.ask` — goes to the identity `host` alone, whoever the caller names: `LiveRoom.send` takes its `destinationIdentities` from the topic (`LIVE_TOPICS[topic].to`, `destinationsOf`), not from the call, so no other guest's client receives a byte of a guest's keys or pasted text (a password the page's picture masks). "All", from the host, is the guests main admits (§6.3): whatever the host sends goes to them alone, by `destinationIdentities`; from a guest, it is the host and the guests the host lists (below), never everyone in the room. A list of nobody is never sent: to LiveKit an empty `destinationIdentities` is everyone, so `LiveRoom.send` sends nothing then.

**Who a guest is with** (`lv.guests`, kept by `LiveRoom`). The host tells the guests main admits who they are — on every change, to them alone, ahead of anything the change starts (a sync); to a guest as it says hello, ahead of the sync; and to all of them when its own room is back. A guest is with the host and the guests on its last list (itself left out): what it sends to all goes to them alone (`destinationsOf`'s `within`), it lets only them subscribe to its tracks, and whatever anyone else sends it is dropped and counted (a text stream read off, unkept), they are not among its participants, and their tracks are not taken (§3). Until its first list, and from a reconnect — LiveKit resuming, or the room joined again from scratch — until a fresh one, it lets only the host subscribe and sends to the host alone: someone the host let out meanwhile may be on the last list. It still hears the last list's guests meanwhile (receiving is no leak; nothing of its own reaches them). Its strokes and chat said meanwhile are held (at most 256; past that, the oldest goes to the host alone, whose board and chat are the room's) and go once the list comes, in the order said and before anything said after, to the host and the guests on it, so no board or chat lacks them; pointers, pings and reactions are of the moment and go to the host alone. The guest page asks for the room after every reconnect (§7), and the host's answer brings a fresh list. A guest just let in is with the others one hop of the host's list after the host is told (§3). What the others said in that hop went to the host and not to the newcomer, and may reach the host after the newcomer's sync was taken — a stroke begun then would be missing from their board for good — so a guest let in beside others is sent the room once more 2 s after their first sync (§6.3), which a guest merges as any answer to its asking.

```ts
type Rect = { x: number; y: number; w: number; h: number }; // stage CSS px

interface LiveDeskFrame {
  v: 1;
  seq: number;                       // numbered by main, once per share (across the host app's reconnects); a frame, alone or in a sync, older than the one shown is dropped
  stage: { w: number; h: number };   // the host's stage, CSS px
  hostPresent: boolean;              // the host is on this space (and the window is up)
  space: { title: string; color: string };
  windows: LiveWindow[];             // bottom to top
}

interface LiveWindow {
  id: string;                        // the tab id, or "file:<id>" for a document
  kind: "page" | "shell";            // page: its tab's capture; shell: drawn by the shell, cut from its picture (§6.6)
  title: string;                     // ≤ 300 UTF-16 units (as zod counts), cut with "…", never through a surrogate pair
  origin: string | null;             // "github.com" — never the full URL
  favicon: string | null;            // an https: or data:image/ URL ≤ 4 KB, else null
  frame: Rect;                       // the window's frame
  page: Rect;                        // its page box inside the frame (stage px, like frame)
  focused: boolean;                  // the window in use on the host
  minimized: boolean;
  controllable: boolean;             // guests may use it: allowed, the host present, and it streams (never with track null)
  terminal?: boolean;                // a terminal the shell draws (kind "shell"): guests may be let use it (§6.6); absent: not one
  track: string | null;              // "win:<id>" while streamed
  paused: boolean;                   // its picture is the last one, not live (shell kind while the host is away or covered, §6.6; a masked page)
}
// and on the frame: guestsCanDraw?: boolean — absent means on
```

**Ink.** `{ op: "begin", id, tool: "pen" | "highlighter" | "laser", color, size, pts }` (`size` a fraction of the stage width), `add { id, pts }`, `end { id }`, `erase { id }`, `clear { scope: "mine" | "all" }`. `pts` is a flat `[x, y, x, y, …]` array of stage-normalized points, at most 64 pairs a message (a long stroke is many `add`s, sent every 24 ms; past 1000 pairs it continues as a new stroke), on a 1/10000 grid of the stage (half a pixel of a 5K stage; finer than the outline draws). A board keeps every point on that grid whoever sent it — a finer one is rounded to it — so a coordinate is at most 7 characters of a sync. Stroke ids are `<identity>:<n>`, so a stroke's owner is in its id; `erase` and `clear: "mine"` touch only the sender's strokes, `clear: "all"` is the host's alone. Pen and highlighter stay until cleared; a laser stroke fades over 1.2 s from its last point. A stroke still open with no point for 3 s is drawn as finished (its `end` may never reach a board: a late joiner's sync can carry a stroke whose end came first and was refused); its owner's next points still land. While the frame says `guestsCanDraw: false`, every board — the host's and each guest's — refuses a guest's `begin` and `add` (ending, erasing and clearing their own still land). Strokes are drawn with `perfect-freehand`. The host holds the board (at most 400 strokes; the oldest go) and sends it in `lv.sync`, cut to what a guest reads of one (8 MiB of JSON, `buildSync`): past it the oldest strokes go first, then the oldest chat, then the desk (the next `lv.desk` brings it). A sync over it would be refused, and every later one with it as the board and the chat only grow, leaving whoever joins or comes back with neither. A full board on the grid (~5.7 MB) and the chat at its longest fit together; only an unusually large desk beside them costs the oldest strokes. A guest merges every sync as of when it began asking for one (joined, back from a reconnect, the host back): the host's snapshot is from then or later, and can land seconds after — or after an answer to a later hello, or once the connection came back — so what the guest's board heard since is newer. A stroke that began reaching it since stays; of a stroke in both, the copy with more points; a stroke erased or cleared there since (its owner's erase or clear, the host's clear) stays gone though the snapshot holds it — a board keeps what was deleted, strokes it never had included (a guest just come has none of the sync's), and an erased id for good, since ids are never drawn again; anything older the sync does not hold goes (a clear missed while away), a stroke an earlier sync brought too.

**Input.** `LiveInputEvent` is a page-normalized pointer or key event: `mouseMove | mouseDown | mouseUp { x, y, buttons, button?, clickCount, modifiers }`, `wheel { x, y, dx, dy, modifiers }` (dx, dy CSS px, clamped ±2000), `keyDown | keyUp { key, code, modifiers, repeat? }` (a key down with Alt also `mac?`: the guest's keyboard is a Mac's, whose Option types, where a PC's Alt is Meta — for a terminal, §6.6), `char { text, modifiers? }` (one grapheme, or `"\r"` for Enter — sent exactly when the DOM would fire `keypress`, so typing has one path and nothing is synthesized; `modifiers` is Shift alone, when held: Chromium breaks a line on Shift+Enter's char where Enter's starts a paragraph, and a keypress handler reads `shiftKey`. Never ⌥ — on a char it eats ⌥↵'s line break, makes a form's field or button submit as a download of the host's, and reads the key as `""` — nor ⌘ or Ctrl, which type nothing on a char; probed in Electron 43; a char that names any of them is invalid), `insertText { text }` (paste or IME commit, ≤ 2000 chars; a longer one is several, never split inside a character or a CRLF). `x, y` are 0..1 of the window's `page` box. The package's `toElectronInputEvents(event, viewSize)` turns one into Electron `InputEvent`s one for one (view DIPs; device emulation is Chromium's to map; wheel deltas negated — Electron's positive `deltaY` scrolls up, probed in Electron 43; multi-unit graphemes and `insertText` become `webContents.insertText`) — pure, so main imports it without Electron in the package. Shortcuts sent this way do nothing on macOS (⌘A selects nothing): select-all, undo and redo may be mapped to the `webContents` methods; copy and cut never are (they would write the host's clipboard). Blink still runs the old IBM clipboard chords for `sendInputEvent`'s key downs on macOS (probed, Electron 43: Shift+Insert typed the host's clipboard into the page, Ctrl+Insert and Shift+Delete wrote it), so `toElectronInputEvents` drops Insert with any modifiers, Delete with Shift, and C, X or V with ⌘ or Ctrl, whatever the guest calls the key; plain Delete still deletes. A key's name is looked up in the mapping's own tables alone: a guest's `"__proto__"` or `"toString"` names no key (it would find what every object inherits, and `sendInputEvent` throws, in main, on a `keyCode` that is not a string — probed, Electron 43). The guest page leaves Shift+Insert, like ⌘V, to its own browser's paste, which sends the guest's text as `insertText`.

**Chat.** A sync's entry is `{ id, from, name, color, text, at }`. A guest's message goes straight to the host and the guests it is with (above), never relayed; the host keeps the last 200 messages of the share — the host's own and the guests' as main took them (not stored anywhere else, gone with the share) — and sends them in `lv.sync`; a guest sees the room's history from before they joined, merged with what reached it live. Wherever chat is kept or merged (the guest page, main, the Live card) a message is its sender and its id together, never its id alone: ids are each sender's own, and only the sender is vouched for (by the room, or by the host in a sync), so a guest who sends another's id first hides nothing of theirs. A guest shows at most 5 messages a second from any one sender (10 at once); the rest are dropped.

**Pings and reactions.** Each is a node and a timer of its own where it is drawn, and guests hear each other's directly (above), so every end meters what it hears, the host and each guest alike: at most 4 of any one participant's pings a second (8 at once) and as many of their reactions (`MARK_RATE`; the host's window counts the two together), metered before anything is looked up, the rest dropped. One's own are drawn unmetered. A layer has at most 48 up at once, the oldest taken down early (`MarkPile`), however many people send them. The other feeds a guest hears from its peers need no meter of their own: a pointer is one per participant, its latest sample, drawn once a frame; ink lands on a bounded board (above), drawn at most once a frame with each stroke's outline kept; chat is metered (above).

## 5. Control

All under `/v1/live`, in `services/control/src/live/` (routes registered on `v1` by `registerLiveRoutes`, schema in `src/db/schema.ts` + `src/db/migrate.ts` + `test/schema-parity.test.ts`), configured by `livekitFromEnv` (§11). Unconfigured, every route answers 503 `live_unavailable`.

**Host routes** (device bearer; on the anonymous allow-list too — a signed-out Mac can host):

| Route | |
| --- | --- |
| `POST /v1/live/shares` `{ title, hostName? }` | Opens the share's room first (LiveKit `createRoom`: at most `LIVE_MAX_GUESTS` + 1 participants, the host included; closed by LiveKit itself once nobody has been in it for 10 min — before the host joins, or after the last one leaves); a room that cannot be opened fails the start with 502 and makes nothing, this device's previous share going on. Then creates the share (ends this device's previous live one, whose room is closed). → `{ share, link, livekit: { url, token, room, identity: "host" } }`. At most 2 live shares per user (a start refused for it closes the room it opened). |
| `GET /v1/live/shares/:id` | The share, its pending knocks, and the guests it let in (`admitted`: requests still `admitted` — not those removed or gone since), which the host checks its room against (§6.1). |
| `PATCH /v1/live/shares/:id` `{ admission?, title? }` | `admission: "ask" \| "open"`; `title` as the space is renamed (the host sends a new name once it has stood 2 s). The host sends one at a time, and checks the share's answer to its reads against its choices (§6.1). |
| `POST /v1/live/shares/:id/heartbeat` `{ refresh? }` | Every 60 s while live; a share with no heartbeat for 10 min is ended by the sweep. → a fresh host token (6 h) when the last one minted is within an hour of expiry (`host_token_expires_at`), or when the host asks (`refresh: true`, §6.1: its room must join again): control's record alone cannot say what the host holds — an answer that brought a token and was lost leaves control holding the host's token new for hours while the one the host has runs out. Asked, at most 10 a share in 10 min (in-memory); past that the record decides, and the heartbeat still answers. Each also opens the room again after answering (`createRoom` answers with the room when it is open), so a room LiveKit lost — a restart, or closed while empty — is back within a minute: with `room.auto_create` off (§11) the host could not rejoin it otherwise. A share that ended while that was asked has its room closed again — a try of its own, claimed at once: the heartbeat asks only for a live share, but the host's stop can land while the open is on its way (nothing asked first could rule that out), its close then reaching LiveKit before the open does. |
| `POST /v1/live/shares/:id/requests/:requestId` `{ decision: "admit" \| "deny" }` | Decides a knock. |
| `POST /v1/live/shares/:id/participants/:identity/remove` | Removes a guest (LiveKit `removeParticipant`) and marks their request `removed`, so they cannot mint again. LiveKit does not revoke the token they hold (and refreshes a participant's token as it joins), so the host keeps them out itself (§6.1); asked again for someone already removed or gone, it removes them from the room again. |
| `DELETE /v1/live/shares/:id` | Ends it: status `ended`, LiveKit `deleteRoom` (everyone is disconnected). A room LiveKit does not close then stays to close, and the sweep closes it (Bounds); ending again asks nothing of a room already closed. A share is its account's — another account's token is answered 404 — so the Mac sends this before its account leaves it (signed out, another signed in to, the Mac revoking itself, §6.1), under the account's own token; `/account/link` takes an anonymous account's shares into the account signed in to (`docs/anonymous-accounts.md`). A Mac revoked elsewhere can send nothing: its share ends with the sweep once it has been silent 10 min (Bounds). |

**Guest routes** (public, no account; CORS as every route):

| Route | |
| --- | --- |
| `GET /v1/live/join/:token` | `{ title, hostName, admission, status: "live" \| "ended" }`; 404 `live_not_found` for an unknown token. |
| `POST /v1/live/join/:token/requests` `{ name }` | → `{ requestId, secret, status }`; with `admission: "open"` (or a re-join, below) status is `admitted` and the body carries `livekit: { url, token, identity, color }`. With `ask`, control sends the host `lv.knock` through LiveKit's server API. |
| `GET /v1/live/join/:token/requests/:requestId` (`authorization: Bearer <secret>`) | `{ status: "pending" \| "admitted" \| "denied" \| "removed" \| "left" \| "ended" }` (+ a fresh `livekit` token on every poll while admitted, which also counts as the guest being about — below). Polled every 1.5 s while pending; also how a reload rejoins (sessionStorage keeps `requestId` + `secret`). |
| `DELETE /v1/live/join/:token/requests/:requestId` (bearer secret) | Cancel / leave. |

**Records.** `live_shares (id, user_id, device_id, token_hash unique, room, title, host_name, admission, status, created_at, updated_at, host_seen_at, host_token_expires_at, ended_at, room_close_due_at, room_close_attempts)` and `live_requests (id, share_id, name, secret_hash, color, status, created_at, decided_at, absent_since)`. An ended share's room is to close — `room_close_due_at`, when control next asks LiveKit — until LiveKit has deleted it or says it has none. The link token is 32 random bytes, base64url (43 chars); only its SHA-256 is stored, as is the request secret's. Names are trimmed, 1–40 characters, control characters stripped.

**Bounds.** 8 admitted guests per share (`LIVE_MAX_GUESTS`); 30 pending knocks; 10 knocks per minute per IP and 60 per share, 30 shares created per account per hour, 10 host tokens asked for per share in 10 min (in-memory, `PasswordAttemptLimiter`-style); the sweep (every minute, and in the hourly maintenance pass) ends shares silent for 10 min, closes the rooms of ended shares not yet closed, frees the places of guests gone without leaving (below), and deletes ended shares and their requests after 7 days. Every way a share ends — stopped, replaced by its Mac's next share, silent — marks its room to close, and it counts as closed only once LiveKit deletes it or says it has none: guests never ask control anything while in the room, so a room nobody closed would keep them in it, seeing and hearing each other, without learning the share ended. A close that fails is tried again 1, 2, 4… minutes on, at most an hour apart (`room_close_attempts`), each room claimed before LiveKit is asked, so it is asked once however many sweeps and stops run at once, until its share is deleted with its 7 days (logged as given up). A try's answer settles its room only while it is the latest claimed (`room_close_attempts` still its number): a stop's close that LiveKit took before a heartbeat's open re-made the room, answering only after the heartbeat's own try for that room, leaves the room due if that try failed.

**Guests gone without leaving.** A guest who closes the tab instead of pressing Leave stays `admitted`, holding one of the eight places, and the host cannot remove them — they are in no roster of the host's. Control is the authority on who holds a place and LiveKit on who is in the room, so control asks LiveKit itself, main or no main: each sweep lists the participants of every live share's room that has a guest admitted (`listParticipants`; a room not open holds nobody). An admitted guest missing from it is marked absent (`absent_since`) from that look; one still missing at a look 2 min (`GUEST_ABSENCE_MS`) after, who took no token between, is `left`, and their place is free; one in the room, or taking a token (the request poll while admitted: a reload, the room reconnecting), is about again (`absent_since` cleared). So a reload — a token within seconds — always rejoins while admitted, and an abandoned visit is let go 2–3 minutes after it went. Every change is conditional on the row as it stands, so a token taken as the sweep looks keeps the guest, and a poll that finds the request let go since its read gets no token. A room LiveKit cannot be asked about keeps everyone it has. A guest let go who comes back with the token they hold is shown out by the host as any guest control no longer lists (§6.1); reloading, their page finds `left` and starts at the lobby.

**Errors.** 404 `live_not_found` (unknown, malformed, or another account's); 409 `live_ended`, `live_full` (`reason: "guests" | "lobby"`), `live_not_pending` (deciding a knock already decided, or cancelled while the host decided: an admit or deny takes a knock only while it is still pending, so one cancelled meanwhile stays cancelled), `live_too_many_shares`; 429 `live_rate_limited`; 502 `live_unavailable` with `reason: "remove_failed"` when LiveKit refuses a removal (retrying retries it), `reason: "room_failed"` when it cannot open a new share's room (nothing was made); 503 `live_unavailable` when unconfigured; 401 for a guest call without its bearer secret.

## 6. The desktop host

### 6.1 Main: `apps/desktop/src/main/live/`

- **`LiveShareService`** (`live-service.ts`): the one owner of the share. Holds `LiveShareState` (shell-contracts `live-share.ts`), publishes it on its own channel (`pistachio:live-changed`, coalesced like every snapshot), and handles `LiveCommand`s from the shell. What the live-host app says did not take (`mediaFailed`) shows as it is, with why: a mic or camera off, a background as the one the camera shows instead (the one it had, which the report names) — so the Live card never names a background the camera does not show. A background failure names the pick that failed (`tried`), and is let go when the host has picked another since: that one is on its way, and answers for itself. Talks to control through `live-control.ts` (methods on the account's control client; works with an anonymous account's token). Heartbeats every 60 s; a fresh token one brings goes to the host app (`token`). The host app asking for a token (`tokenExpiring`: its room must join again) is answered by a heartbeat that asks control for one (`refresh`, §5) — had the answer that brought the last been lost, control would give none for hours while the one held ran out, and the host could not rejoin — and with the token held when none comes (control unreachable, or asked too often). Renames the share (`PATCH title`) once the space's new name has stood 2 s (a Home space's name is its page's title, which may tick), the name as control keeps it (control characters and bidi marks stripped, one line, cut to 200 characters with no space left at the end), so control's word on it can be compared. What it sends control of the host's choices — who may join, the name — goes one `PATCH` at a time, each with what changed since the last went out: two on their way at once may be applied out of order, leaving control letting anyone in while the card says *Ask me*. Chosen again meanwhile, only the newest of each follows. An admission control did not take puts the card back on what control holds, whatever was picked since, with the error; a rename that failed is only logged. Each read of the share (the lobby, the heartbeat) that set out with no update on its way and saw none go since compares control's admission and title with the host's, and where they differ (an update taken for failed that landed after all, a rename that never did) sends the host's again — never the other way: only this Mac changes its share, and the card is what the host goes by. Makes one share at a time: a start waits for a create still in flight (one stopped while control made it), since control ends a Mac's last share as it makes the next. A knock the host admits or denies leaves the list at once, and comes back with the error if control did not decide it (full, unreachable). A change to guests' speaking or connection and nothing else goes to the shell on a channel of its own (`pistachio:live-roster-changed`, the guests alone), not the whole state with its chat, and not to the host app, which has the room's word on both. Ends the share on: the shared space dissolving or closing, its Profile switched away, the main window closing, `before-quit` (best-effort `DELETE`), control answering `ended`, and this Mac's account about to leave it (`accountChanging`, which the account's `beforeAccountChange` awaits: a sign-out; a sign-in or sign-up that puts another account's token in place of the one held; an anonymous account a sign-in could not link, its own token put back on the client for this; this Mac revoking itself) — `account-changed`, said in a notice. The `DELETE` goes out under the account's own token (a request takes the token it is sent with), waited for 1.5 s at most, and a share control is still making ends as it lands within that: past the change, control would answer another account's token 404, the share would read as ended elsewhere and never be told to end, and its room would keep its guests until the sweep found it silent. A sign-in that links the anonymous account keeps the share (control moves it with the device, §5; until the link lands the client holds a bootstrap token, whose 403s are errors, not an end), and an upgrade keeps the account. A Mac control stopped honouring (revoked elsewhere: `onSignedOut("revoked")`) can tell control nothing: the share stops here, and the sweep ends it within 10 min (§5).
- **The live-host window** (`live-host-window.ts`): created when a share starts, destroyed when it ends. `new BrowserWindow({ parent: main, transparent: true, frame: false, hasShadow: false, focusable: false, resizable: false, movable: false, skipTaskbar: true, show: false, webPreferences: { partition: "live-host", preload: "live-host.cjs", sandbox: true, contextIsolation: true, backgroundThrottling: false } })`, loading its own page, `pistachio-app://live-host/live-host.html` (a protocol handler on the `live-host` partition serving the renderer folder, nothing outside it): a page of its own so its CSP alone can add `'wasm-unsafe-eval'` for the background effects' MediaPipe, and a secure origin (a `file:` page is not one); its bounds follow the main window's content bounds (move, resize, fullscreen); `setIgnoreMouseEvents(true, { forward: true })` except while `LiveHostApp` reports the pointer on something of its own (`interactive { active }`: a face, the pill — event-driven from forwarded moves, nothing polls) or while the host draws (then the whole window takes the pointer and becomes focusable so Escape reaches it; focus returns to the main window after). It reads what strangers send (guests' packets, names and chat), so it is sandboxed and its preload is its own (`preload/live-host.ts`): `liveHostConfig`, `onLiveHostEvent`, `sendLiveHostReport`, `liveHostSource` and `liveHostAppearance` (the theme's), each answered by main for this window alone — nothing of the shell's API. The renderer makes that a whole bridge for the pieces it mounts (`renderer/src/live-host-bridge.ts`: every other native member refuses). Its session, made with the first share and never at launch, grants camera and microphone to this one `webContents` and nothing else; `web-contents-created` gives it `disable_non_proxied_udp` as it gives every page, and the window sets its own back to `default` as it is made (the proxied policy would force WebRTC onto TCP relays). It never navigates.
- **Capture**: for each streamed page window, main answers the host app's `liveHostSource(tabId)` with `tab.webContents.getMediaSourceId(liveHost.webContents)`, granting a capture of the page's picture alone (no sound, §3) to that requester, one grant per requester (the shell's picture of a covered window and the share may capture one tab at once). While a guest is in the room, and for 30 s after the last leaves, the shared space's streamed tabs are held awake (no suspension; woken if asleep, three at a time, each waited on 8 s at most, so a page that never finishes loading holds up none of the others — `live-hold.ts`); a tab whose `webContents` is replaced (woken, crashed) is announced so the host app captures it again. The shared space is named with the hold, guests or not: Tidy leaves it alone, as it does the space in front. A page whose view is not shown lays out at the size its view last had — 0×0 for one woken while covered, and its capture never starts — and bounds given to a hidden view never reach its page (probed, Electron 43): so `holdLiveTabs` takes each held page's size, and a held page not at it is drawn at it for a moment directly beneath the view in use, inside its box, until the page reports the size (a round trip), then hidden again and captured afresh. With no view up to hide it under (a shell cover is up, or the window in use is smaller than the page), it is tried after the next layout that has one. A hold comes with every frame of a streamed window's resize, so a page known to be at its size (drawn at it, or read at it, since; forgotten when its renderer goes or its first document comes) is not read again; the size is read from the main frame, which answers while the page still loads (the contents' `executeJavaScript` waits for the load to end), within 1 s.
- **Desk frames** (`live-desk.ts`): the shell reports the shared space's geometry (`setLiveDesk`, §6.4); main adds what only it knows — title (and the space's, each clipped to the protocol's 300 UTF-16 units — the schema's count, not characters, since one unit over drops the whole frame), origin, favicon, `controllable` (allowed, the host present, and streamed: a guest's hands reach only a window whose picture they have, and the input gate refuses any other — so the guest never offers a hand it would refuse), `hostPresent` (`currentGroupId === shared`, the window visible and not minimized, the app not hidden, the Mac neither locked nor asleep — `live-presence.ts`, which looks again on each window, power and app-activation change, and once more a moment after the app comes or goes: ⌘H hides it with no window event) — dedupes, and forwards a `LiveDeskFrame` to the host app at most 30 times a second — at once when a frame holds a picture the last one let run. The windows that stream go to the shell as the state's `streamed` whenever they change (§6.4). A shell-drawn window streams as a page does (one of the eight), and is held (`paused`) while the host is away, while the shell's report says something of its own lies over the stage where no box holds it, and while something whose box is known lies over the window (§6.6); each frame goes to the host app with the shell's mark of the report it was built from (`LiveShellMark`: the report's number, where it put the stage, the window's size), and a report that changes nothing guests see still sends that mark, the same frame and `seq` with it, at the same pace (§6.6); main answers `liveHostShellSource()`, the capture of the shell's own page those windows are cut from, only then (§6.6). It is no tab page main captures, keeps awake or lets a guest use: its tab is neither held nor `controllable`, and a guest's input or ask for it is refused.
- **Guest input** (`live-input.ts`): `lv.input` from the host app is applied with `webContents.sendInputEvent` (a terminal's goes to the shell instead, §6.6) after checking that the sender is admitted, the window is controllable and streamed, not minimized or masked, and the host is present; at most 240 events a second per guest, the rest dropped. A `mouseDown` from a guest makes the window the one in use (as a host's press does) and focuses its page only while the app is frontmost, so a guest's click never pulls the host out of another app (typing reaches the page either way). The guest using a window is the last guest to press in it (`lv.grant`); it goes back to nobody only when the window is disallowed or that guest leaves. A press main lets through (the left button, a key) is owed its release: the guest's own release of it goes whatever changed since (it can only end that press), and plain (with ⌘ pressed meanwhile a key's would read as one of the app's chords, and with ⌘ or Shift the button's would open its click's link in a tab or window); and when their hands come off the window — the host takes it back, it is minimized, masked or no longer streams, the host goes away, the guest leaves or is removed, the share ends — main sends the page the release of whatever they held on it at once (the button where their pointer last was, then each key; the guest's own release after is refused), so no page is left dragging or with a key down; a key pressed while the guest holds 16 down in the window is refused before the page has it, since main would not owe its release. A release of nothing main holds for that guest in that window — its press refused, dropped or let go already, or the release sent again (§7) — reaches no page, and spends nothing of the guest's 240 a second: Chromium clicks nothing for a mouseup without its press (probed, Electron 43), but the page still hears it, and pages act on one (a key-up of Enter that searches, a mouseup that shows a selection's toolbar). Main lets go of nothing for a guest's connection alone: what it hears of one is the SFU's judgment of its quality, which can read lost while their input still comes, and would end a drag they are still in; a guest who is gone LiveKit drops from the room, and main lets go as they leave the roster, and one who comes back lets go again of what they let go of meanwhile (§7). Whatever the guest's page sends, main refuses a button but the left (a right press opens main's context menu on the host's screen, a middle one opens tabs), a press or release of the left one with ⌘, Ctrl or Shift held (Chromium makes a Ctrl press the host's context menu, and a click with ⌘ or Shift on a link a tab or window of the host's: probed in Electron 43, each is asked of main only after `sendInputEvent` has returned, so the `#liveInput` flag below cannot tell it from the host's own; refused rather than sent plain, which would take the page to the link instead, so a guest's Shift-click extends no selection; ⌥ goes, opening nothing), a wheel with ⌘ or Ctrl (it zooms, and Chromium keeps a site's zoom for the host), and a key that is one of the app's — a binding of the person's shortcuts, or ⌘C, ⌘X, ⌘V, ⇧⌘V, ⌘Q, ⌘H, ⌘M. Guest events are flagged while sent (`#liveInput`), so none of main's own key bindings, the tab switcher (nor its pointer disarm, which would cancel the host's ⌥⌘ hold) or the desk's grab hear them.
- **Who is in the room**: the host app reports the room's roster; the share's guests are those of it that control lists as let in (`admitted`), asked by a lobby read that set out after they came into the room — started at once whenever someone new does, tried again 2 s after a read that failed while anyone waits on it — less anyone the host removed since (marked at once, before control answers) or denied. Every read after it judges them again (a read judges only those who came before it set out): one that no longer lists them has them out, as below — control let them go (they left, or the sweep found them gone) while LiveKit keeps them, its removal having failed, or they are back under the same identity before the room said they left. While knocking is how people get in, the lobby is read every 15 s; with anyone free to join, with each minute's heartbeat while anyone is let in — so such a guest keeps the room at most 15 s, or a minute. Until that first read answers, someone new is nobody's guest: not in `participants`, not in `admitted`, nothing of theirs heard (measured in the real app with control in process: let in 9–25 ms after the room named them, against under 1 ms when the room's word alone let them in; the window's first picture, 1.6–2.1 s after the join, did not move). A guest who leaves the room is asked about afresh when they are back. Anyone the read does not list (they left, were removed, or never were) is out for the share as those the host removed or denied are: any of them in the room is removed again through control at once (LiveKit refreshes a participant's token as it joins, and removing them does not revoke it), and again on every lobby read while the roster still lists them (one back under the same identity before the room said they left is no new participant to it), and nothing of theirs is heard: no input, asks or chat. A removal control does not confirm — the host's, or main's of anyone shown out — is asked again 1, 2, 4… s on, at most 30 s apart, until it does (someone LiveKit no longer has counts as removed), whatever the roster says meanwhile: someone LiveKit still has is still in the room — they see who is in it and hold one of its places, though nothing of the host's or a guest's reaches them (§4 `lv.guests`) — and with anyone free to join the lobby is read only once a minute. It stops when the share ends, and for someone control has no request of (a token it never made), whom it cannot remove. Until the host's own removal is confirmed, the Live card says it has not gone through yet. Main tells the host app who may receive the room (`admitted`: on every change, and in `liveHostConfig`); the host app lets only them subscribe to its tracks, sends its data only to them, and tells them who they are, so each guest does the same with its own (§6.3, §4).
- **The host's pointer** (`live-cursor.ts`): while at least one guest is in the room and the host is present, main polls `screen.getCursorScreenPoint()` at 60 Hz, maps it into stage-normalized coordinates, and sends it to the host app (which publishes it as `lv.cursor`, ≤ 30 Hz, and hides it outside the stage).

### 6.2 Contracts: `packages/shell-contracts/src/live-share.ts`

`LiveShareState` (status `idle | starting | live | ending | error`, the shared `groupId`, `shareId`, `link`, `admission`, `guestsCanDraw`, `controllable` tab ids, `streamed` tab ids (the windows whose pictures go out, §3), `participants`, `knocks` (only requests control lists as pending), `asks`, `chat`, `unread`, `local` media state, `hostAway`, `startedAt`, `using` (window → guest), `drawing`, `connection`, `ended` (why the last share ended — `account-changed` when this Mac's account left it), `error`). `start` while another space is shared stops that share first; `start` on the space already shared copies its link again. A 404 or `live_ended` from control ends the share as `ended-elsewhere`., `LiveCommand` (start, stop, admit, deny, remove, setAdmission, setGuestsCanDraw, setControllable, answerAsk, setMic, setCamera, setBackground, setDevice, setDrawing, clearInk, chat, react, markRead, copyLink), `LiveDeskReport` (what the shell reports), and the main ↔ live-host messages (a `mediaFailed` for a background names the pick that failed and the background the camera shows instead). The live-host window's own preload exposes `LiveHostApi` and nothing else (the room's five members — `liveHostShellSource` the fifth, §6.6 — and `liveHostAppearance`), its channels spelled out and held equal to `IPC` by the compiler (a sandboxed preload can load no chunk it would share with the shell's); main → live-host `admitted { identities }` names who may receive the room; `desk { frame, shell }` and `liveHostConfig().shell` carry the shell's mark the frame was built under (`LiveShellMark`, §6.6), and `LiveDeskReport` its number (`mark`, 16 bits, wrapping) and the window's size (`viewport`).

### 6.3 The live-host app: `packages/shell-ui/src/LiveHostApp.tsx`

Mounted by `apps/desktop/src/renderer/src/main.tsx` for the `live-host` hash. It:
- joins the room (`@pistachio/live-share/room`) with the host token main gives it, reconnects on its own, and asks main for a new token when LiveKit says it expired; a room joined afresh (LiveKit gave the last one up) starts its camera and mic from nothing, and a step still under way in the room left — a background loading, a mic's prompt — counts for nothing in the new one when it lands;
- is the room of the guests main admits (`admitted`, `components/live-host/audience.ts`) and of nobody else in it: only they may subscribe to its tracks, and its room takes only their cameras and microphones, from the moment each room joins — the list as it is then goes to `LiveRoom.connect` (`receivers`), since LiveKit subscribes a room to what is in it before `connect` returns, so a removed guest still in the room is never played even for that moment; without one, a host's room lets in nobody — and again on every change (`setReceivers`); everything it sends goes to them alone (`destinationIdentities`; with nobody admitted nothing goes, since to LiveKit an empty list is everyone), and only their pointers, ink, pings, reactions and faces are drawn; and it tells them who they are (`lv.guests`, §4: on every change, to them alone, ahead of the sync the change starts; to a guest as it says hello; to all when its room is back), so each guest keeps its own camera, mic and word among them and the host;
- while any guest is admitted, and for 30 s after the last leaves, captures each window of the latest desk frame that should stream (`liveHostSource`) and publishes it as `win:<id>`, unpublishing what left (one whose capture had ended too, its fresh one still to come: the room holds it until told, and would publish its stopped track again joining afresh); a window's capture is resized in place (`applyConstraints`, smaller or larger, up to the page's own size, which no capture exceeds — probed in Electron 43) whenever its page box changes size, and taken afresh when its page is replaced — tried again after a failed try until a fresh one is in, though the old track is still the page's size — the track handed to `replaceWindowTrack` either way, so the window keeps its publication and guests never see it go; a picture the room does not take (a publish that timed out, a sender gone with the connection) is asked of it again 1, 3, 8, then every 20 s, and at once when the room is back after a drop — every window's picture then, so one a full rejoin (the room's, or LiveKit's own) could not publish again goes out too — until it does (a new capture asks afresh; the window leaving, its track ending or a new room ends the asking), so guests are never left a blank or frozen window once the room can take it; the shell-drawn windows' tracks the same (§6.6);
- for the shell-drawn windows of the frame, captures the shell's own page once and cuts each window's picture out of it, each frame by the word of the report it was painted under — read off the frame itself — and only while the latest frame lets the window run too (§6.6, `components/live-host/shell-crops.ts`);
- publishes the host's camera and mic per `LiveShareState.local`, with the background effect (`@pistachio/live-share/effects`, which loads `@livekit/track-processors` only when a background is chosen; MediaPipe's wasm and the vendored segmenter model, `packages/live-share/assets`, are served by our own origins at `/live/mediapipe` — www's `public/` and the desktop's renderer folder, both copied at build — never a CDN), the camera going out only once its background is on it, or staying off (the room's rule, §7). The camera's steps (background, on and off, its device) and the mic's and speaker's run in lanes of their own (`components/live-host/media-steps.ts`), so a permission prompt for one holds up nothing of the other; and the camera coming on (its prompt, its capture, its background loading) holds up nothing after it: a background chosen meanwhile, or the camera turned off, goes to the room at once — the room puts the background chosen last on a camera it is starting, and stops one turned off before it goes out — so the host's camera never goes out behind a background other than the latest, or after it was turned off. A camera device chosen while one comes on is switched to once it is in. An off waits on nothing: it goes to the room the moment main says so, ahead of whatever the camera's lane has under way — a background loading on a camera that is out (the segmenter's first load, a second or two), a device switch — and the room stops and unpublishes the camera at once, letting go of a change that lands after. The mic's off likewise goes at once, ahead of whatever the sound lane has under way — the mic coming on (its permission prompt, its first publish), a device switch: the room mutes it, and a mic still coming is let go (stopped as its capture lands, or muted before its publish goes out, §7), so it never goes out live once the card says Mic off; one that fails after is not told. Turned on, the camera and the mic are asked for on the device chosen, the system's by name (`"default"`), as is a device switched to: left out, LiveKit would capture on, or keep, the one it used last, and a device chosen while one is off is not switched to until it is on (`mediaSteps`). A background that does not take is told to main with the one the camera shows instead (`room.background()`: the one it had, put back on it), which main makes the host's (`local.background`); that pick alone is not tried again until main names another (a pick the host made since still goes on). A camera or mic the room took out, unable to put it out again after joining from scratch (`mediaFailed`, §7), is told to main as one that did not come on, and not asked for again until main's word has it off;
- relays: desk frames → `lv.desk`; the host pointer → `lv.cursor`; host ink/pings/reactions/chat (from main) → the room; an ask the host said no to → `lv.refused` to that guest alone (an ask main took away while its guest is still in and the window still not theirs: main takes one away when it is answered, allowed or not, or when its guest leaves); and from the room, guest input/asks/knocks/chat/roster → main — the roster again whenever a guest comes back under the same identity with a new LiveKit sid (back before the room said they left, a removed guest's refreshed token is no new participant to LiveKit's client, only a new sid as they become active), so main shows them out again;
- answers an admitted guest's `lv.hello` with `lv.guests` and `lv.sync` — a guest says hello once it listens (one only just in the room does not yet, so a join alone is not answered), and again every 2.5 s until a sync comes; a hello heard before main admits them is answered as it does; at most one sync per guest every 2 s (the list goes on every hello); a guest let in beside others is sent the room once more 2 s after their first sync (`CATCH_UP_MS`): what the others said before hearing the list naming them reached the host but not them (§4);
- draws, over the stage (main gives it the stage's rect in the window): pointers, ink, pings, reactions, the controllable outlines, the faces, the Live pill; and reports interactive regions for the click-through. The outlines (and the names on them) are a window's own, and go under the browser's own surfaces over the desk as if the shell drew them — this window lies over the whole browser window, the shell's notice view and whatever the shell draws over the desk included. Main tells the app where those lie (`chrome`, window px, in the config and as it changes): the report's `covers` (§6.4: each box the report knows, padded, and a side card's whole column, since its top glides unmeasured), placed by the report's stage, and the notice stack's cards (`main/notice-layer.ts`: where the notice view says it draws them, `setNoticeFootprint`, placed by the view's box and taken only while the view is the size it was measured in; and from the moment a card arrives, or the view is resized, until the view says so again, the whole view — main places and shows the view before the view hears of the card, so no frame of a card is drawn under an outline); or null while the report says something no box holds lies over the desk (`shellCovered`: the palette's scrim, a menu, the Bar's own, a portal, a passing, the shell reloading). The outlines' layer (`WindowMarks.tsx`) is clipped to what those boxes leave of the window — a `clip-path` of the rest, the boxes grown out to whole px (`chrome-cut.ts`), set by ref as main's word changes and never per frame — or not drawn at all for null. The notice view says where its cards are drawn as they move (`stackFootprint`): where they stand, as wide as the widest and as tall as the stack, and while they move (370 ms from a change: the open transition and a frame) wherever they were and are going too, as far toward the stack's edge as a card comes from and a peek away from it. Pointers, ink, pings, reactions, the faces and the pill are drawn over everything, the browser's own included. A guest the host mutes from their face is muted for the host alone, for the rest of the share: LiveKit keeps a volume on the participant it was set on, and a guest back from a reload — everyone, in a room joined afresh — is a new one to it, heard at full volume, so the host app keeps each volume by identity until the share ends and sets it on the guests main lets in whenever the room's participants or main's list change. It is not let go when a guest leaves the list: one reloading is off it a moment (out of the room, they are asked about afresh when back, §6.1), and one removed is let in again, if ever, under a new identity (each is a request's); nobody off the list is played at all. The face shows what it keeps. The window is transparent over the browser's, so nothing in it animates for good (the pill's dot rings three times as the room goes live and as someone comes or goes, then is still) and nothing uses `backdrop-filter` (there is nothing of its own behind it to blur): the pill and the faces' labels are a near-solid translucent fill.

### 6.4 The shell (`packages/shell-ui`)

- **The report**: while a share is live, the desk engine's view of the shared space — its stage size and its rect in the window, and each window's id, kind, frame and page rects, order, focus, minimized and masked state — is sent with `setLiveDesk`, at most every 33 ms while anything moves — from the render that drew it once it is due, two renders apart and three by turns (§6.6) — and once when it settles; when the shared space is not current, its saved arrangement (`SavedDesk`, scaled to the last stage) is sent once. Per-frame geometry never enters a React store or the snapshot. It says too what of the shell's lies over which window (§6.6, `lib/live/desk-report.ts`), and where: each box it knows goes in the report as far as `covered` reads it (`covers`, stage px; past 32, the stage counts as covered), which the live-host app's outlines go under (§6.3) — a box moving is news at the report's pace. A cover whose box is known covers the windows its box, padded 8 px, lies over (each window's `covered`) — over their page boxes now and where the report sent last put them, since a frame painted now is cut there until the next report goes: a frame's or the Bar's tooltip band (`tip:<tab>`, `bar-tip`, `nub-tip`), the Bar's answer (`answer`: its box set in the task that moves it, and taken down as it is unmounted), the rail's floating player, and — as the stage's column from its leading edge to the card's trailing one, the stage's whole height, since its top glides with nothing measured again — a side card (the Live card's too, the More card's, the Stack's) and the hidden sidebar brought out (`coverReach`). Anything else lies over every window (`shellCovered`): every other cover (the Bar's own nub, pill and menus — goo, drawn past their boxes —, the answer's snap ghost and lit zones, which fade out after their covers go, a file drop, the mask editor, the layout preview, the media card, and any cover not named), a passing to or from another space (windows of another space on the stage, the phase not yet open), the store's overlays (the address palette, a context menu, the tab switcher, a dialog, Settings, the error toast, onboarding, a screenshot's selector, a Glance — each dims, blurs or fills the whole window), and anything in `<body>` beside the app's root (a portal: where it is drawn is its own business) but a tooltip (Base UI's popup) lying wholly inside a tooltip band, or wholly off the stage, as it is laid out now — and, the shared space not in front, always. When unsure, every window. Each is heard in the same task that puts it up — the engine reports from the render that set the cover, the store's subscribers hear the `set` that raised the overlay, a mutation observer the portal (`<body>`'s children, and anything changing inside each portal: a tooltip placed or moved) — so before it is painted, and a report that covers more than the last, or whose stage moved in the window or whose window changed size, goes at once, whatever the pace. A window drawn other than as its box — grown and turned as it is carried with the *Lifted* feel — is `covered` too, so that report goes at once. Each window's page is the whole pixels inside it as it is shown: rounded inward, and only as far down as the window is shown (a drawer that pushes it down cuts it at its own foot). Every report sent is numbered (`mark`), and the number painted in the shell's corner in the same task (§6.6); a render that draws a window elsewhere while its report waits paints the last number stale in its own task, and one that moves a window the last report called steady (where the report before it put it too) sends its report at once (§6.6); a reload goes on counting where the page left off (sessionStorage).
- **The Live card** (`components/live/`), entries (space menus, `/share` and `/draw` in `slash-commands.ts`, *Guests can use this window* in a window's ⋯), the rail tile's and the sidebar row's live badge, notices for knocks and asks (`showNotice` with *Admit* / *Allow*), and chat.
- **The state** is main's (`onLiveShare`); a change to guests' speaking or connection alone comes as the guests (`onLiveRoster`), taken as the state's `participants`.
- **Who can be let use a window**: a tab's own page, unmasked, or a terminal (§6.6). Guests watch the shell's other windows (home, notes, a brief, a document) but no guest's hands reach them, and a masked one is not streamed (§3), so neither the card nor the window's ⋯ offers *Guests can use* for it; its row in the card says "Guests watch it; only you can use it" or "Only on your screen for now". A page that does not stream now (minimized, or under the eight that do — main's `streamed`, §3) is no guest's to use until it does: its row says "Not streamed now: guests see its frame alone" and its switch still hands it over for then; its ⋯ offers *Guests can use* only to take it back.
- **Nothing animates at rest**: the live mark and the card's live dot pulse three times as the share goes live and as someone comes or goes, then are still (not at all with reduced motion).

### 6.5 Hidden and covered

A covered window on the Mac is its still there, but its tab is still captured: guests see every window live (one woken while covered is first laid out at its size, §6.1). A shell-drawn window is cut from the shell's picture as it shows it, so one under another window has that window in its picture (guests draw that window over it too); and while the shared space is not current, or anything of the shell's lies over the desk, it is held (§6.6). A minimized or masked window is not streamed (§3). When the shared space is not current its views are hidden (not destroyed) and keep streaming.

### 6.6 Shell-drawn windows

Home, notes, brief, terminal and document windows are drawn by the shell renderer in its own page — a terminal's xterm.js canvas too (probed: the window's element in the shell's DOM holds it, its tab's view kept down at 0×0) — so capturing their tab gives a blank page. They are `kind: "shell"`, and stream like pages, among the eight (§3), minimized ones not: the live-host app captures the shell's own `webContents` once and cuts each such window's page box out of it, frame by frame (`components/live-host/shell-crops.ts`; the browser's pieces in `shell-capture.ts`). Guests' hands reach a terminal alone of them (below; the rest, §14).

- **The capture.** Main answers `liveHostShellSource()` with the browser window's `webContents.getMediaSourceId(liveHost.webContents)`, for the live-host window alone, and only while a share is live, the host is on the shared space (so the shell shows its desk) and a shell-drawn window of it streams. The app asks while a guest is admitted (and for 30 s after the last leaves, as for pages), the host is on the space and such a window streams, and lets it go otherwise — in another space too. It is taken at the window's content size in device pixels (the live-host window's own viewport, which lies exactly over it, each side rounded to even — Chromium stretches the page that hair to fill it, probed: a 1441 × 901 window at 1× filled a 1442 × 902 frame, its corner's pixels its own; at most 8192 on the longer side, past any Mac's window, since the mark needs the page at its own device pixels), resized in place as the window is. Probed in Electron 43: the capture starts from the live-host window with no grant (the shell's session sets no permission handler); its frames are I420, of exactly the size asked, the content fitted into them (letterboxed when their shapes differ); and it brings a frame only when the shell repaints — or when a reader more starts on the track: Chromium asks the source for the page as it is for a new sink, and every reader gets that frame.
- **The cut.** For each window and each frame, `new VideoFrame(frame, { visibleRect, displayWidth, displayHeight })` — no pixels copied — is written to the window's own `MediaStreamTrackGenerator`, published as `win:<id>` through `replaceWindowTrack` once a frame is on it (the room reads its size for the bitrate from the generator's settings, which say the last frame's), and asked of the room again until it takes it, as a captured window's is (§6.3). The rect is the window's page box (stage CSS px: the whole pixels inside its page as it is shown — rounded inward, and as far down as the window is shown, a drawer that pushed it down cutting it at its own foot, §6.4) placed by `stageInWindow`, kept inside the stage and the content box, scaled by the frame's own size against the content's (never an assumed 2×), and rounded inward to even pixels: I420 wants a crop to start on an even one (probed: an odd one is refused), and inward leaves out every pixel beside the box. A crop longer than 2560 px goes out at 2560 (`displayWidth`: WebRTC scales it on the way to the encoder, probed). While a window is being resized its picture goes out at the size it had, scaled, until the new size has stood 300 ms (each new size is a new encoder). Every frame read is closed; a generator closes what is written to it. A picture track has nothing for a guest who starts watching, or whose decoder asks for a whole picture, but what is written to it, and a still shell writes nothing: so a window whose picture runs is given a frame at least once a second, the capture asked for the page as it is (a short-lived reader on a clone of its track). Probed through a loopback in the live-host window, a crop is encoded on VideoToolbox; through the SFU it is encoded as every window is (§3).
- **Held** (`paused: true`; nothing cut, the last picture written again once a second). Main holds a shell-drawn window's picture while the host is away, while the shell's report says something of its own lies over the stage that no box holds (`shellCovered`, §6.4: the palette, a menu, the Bar's own, a portal, a passing), and while something whose box is known lies over the window itself (its `covered`, §6.4: the Live card beside it, its frame's tooltip, the Bar's answer, the floating player) or the window is drawn other than as its box (also its `covered`: carried with the *Lifted* feel, grown and turned with the throw, until it has settled back flat) — so the Live card open beside a terminal holds the window under the card, and the terminal runs on; and from the moment the shell's page starts loading (a reload, back from a crash) or its desk report goes (`setLiveDesk(null)`) until it reports again. A page's picture is its tab's capture whatever the shell draws over it, so it is held for none of these. A window under another window of the space is cut as it is. A held window's track has nothing for a guest who starts watching it, or whose decoder asks for a whole picture, but what is written to it — a track of frames is no capture an encoder can ask for the page as it is (Codex reproduced it with the H.264 publisher: a subscriber who came while it was held drew nothing until it ran again) — so its last crop is written to it again every second (`CROP_REFRESH_MS`) for as long as it is held, and nothing new is cut. That crop is kept as another handle on it (`clone`, no pixels copied) while the window runs, each new crop replacing it; once the window is held its pixels are copied out of the capture's frame into memory of their own (`copyFrame`: the visible rect alone, in the frame's format and colours) and the handle let go — a frame of the capture is the whole page, and one of the few the capture can have out at once, so a window held for long keeps none (one whose pixels cannot be copied goes out as it was cut, said once a hold). One kept picture a window, closed as a newer crop replaces it (one of a new size too), as the window leaves the frame and as the share ends; a window held since it began streaming has none, and sends nothing. Measured in the real app: held, the terminal sends 1 frame a second (none before); a guest who joined under the palette was shown it 0.4–2.9 s after joining — the line, and the terminal's own white (252; 161 on the Mac under the palette's veil) — where without the replay they drew nothing in 10 s.
- **The mark** (`lib/live/marker.ts`). The word of what lies where reaches the app through main, a frame of the capture straight from the GPU: by itself, a word late by any stall would let a frame painted under a menu be cut by a report from before the menu. So the shell paints each report's number into its own pixels in the same task as it sends the report — the task that noticed what covers what, before it is painted — and the app reads the number off every frame and cuts it by that report's word alone: where that report put the stage and each window, and which windows it let run (main sends the word with its frame, `LiveShellMark`; the app keeps the last 16 by number). A frame whose word has not come yet waits for it (the newest such alone — but a fresh one of a report over a stale one after it — a second at most); one whose word main passed over (two reports in one 33 ms frame) is let go; one with no mark that reads true is never cut. *Fresh or stale.* A report waits its turn while the desk moves (§6.4), and the shell draws the moving window elsewhere every frame meanwhile: a frame painted then, cut by the last report's word, would be cut where the window was — the desk beside it in the picture, the window cut short. So the render that first draws a window somewhere the last report did not put it, its own report kept back, paints that report's number stale in the same task (its CRC turned, every bit), and the app cuts a frame whose mark is stale only for the windows that report and the one numbered just before it put in the same place (`steady`; none, if main passed the one before over): the shell sends a report at once from the render that moves a window the last report called steady. A window on the move — carried, resized, gliding, laid out afresh — is cut only from a frame painted in the render its report went from (its mark fresh); the windows standing still run on through it. Reports go from the render that drew them once due (never from a timer between renders, where the next render would draw past them), two renders apart and three by turns at 60 Hz (four and five at 120): the capture takes about every other frame of the desk, steadily, and reports a steady two renders apart could fall on the frames it leaves every time. Every window's page counts, not only those the shell calls its own: main cuts any window it does not know as a tab's page from the shell's picture too. The paint and what it says land in the same compositor frame: probed in the real app, a red box and a cell of the mark changed together in one task at random intervals, and every one of 277 frames at 1× and 274 at 2× showed both or neither. The mark is ten cells of a CSS px in the window's top-left corner, the part macOS rounds away (probed, macOS 26: the window server draws nothing of the first ten points of the top row nor of the quarter circle under it; the cells lie in what a corner of radius 10 pt clips too), each an opaque grey of eight levels 20 apart — the band's foot and top first, so a reader measures the levels off the frame itself, then the number's 16 bits and an 8-bit CRC, three bits a cell — over everything of the shell's (a portal too), taking no pointer. Probed through the real capture at 1× and 2×, in light and dark: every cell came through to the same luma in every frame (BT.709's limited range, within 1 of 16 + 219·grey/255), so a reading is every pixel of every cell squarely on a level, or none; the copy of the corner's pixels a frame takes 0.1–0.2 ms. A frame is read only when it is of the page's own device pixels (its sides a whole number of the window's, within 1 %): a scaled capture has no pixels for a cell. No crop ever takes in the mark (it lies off the stage, and a crop that would is not cut). In full screen the corner is square, and the cells are a speck of greys near the theme's corner there.
- **Nothing but the box, nothing of another space** (§10). With the mark no frame is kept back on the chance its word is late, nor waited out after a window runs again or the stage moves: a frame painted under a cover bears the number of the report that said so, however late that word comes, and a frame of an old layout bears an old report's, which it is cut by. Measured in the real app: the guest heard ⌘L's pause 6–20 ms after the key, and drew no new frame of the terminal under the palette, a context menu, its own frame's tooltip, or another space; with the Live card open beside it the terminal sent 41–45 frames in 1.5 s, and 31–33 while the host typed in the card's chat; and with main's handling of the shell's reports held back 200 ms (the guest heard the pause 206–207 ms after ⌘L), the app read 4 frames painted under the palette while the word was on its way and cut none, and every frame the guest showed since ⌘L was the terminal's own white (252; 161 on the Mac, under the palette's veil). The terminal carried across a desk painted green, the pointer moved every frame: before the stale mark, 16 of 20 of its crops carried as it is had a column of green at an edge, and 20 of 50 carried lifted (grown and turned; up to 691 green samples an edge); since, none of its crops nor any frame the guest showed had any. Carried lifted its picture is held; carried as it is, the app read 30 frames of the shell a second and cut the terminal from 9–12.5 a second of them (the guest showed 7.5–11.6 a second while it moved), home's picture ran on through it, and the guest saw the window move with each desk frame (22–25 a second).
- **A guest's hands on a terminal.** A terminal is the one window of the shell's the host can let guests use (the card's switch, the window's ⋯, an ask answered — `guestsCanBeLetUse`); its frame says `terminal: true`, so the guest page takes the guest's pointer and keyboard on it as on a page, and offers *Ask to use* while it is not theirs. There Ctrl's C, X and V are the terminal's keys, not the clipboard chords the page would swallow (Ctrl+C stops what runs) — but Ctrl+V off a Mac, where it pastes the guest's text still — Insert alone is its key too (a page's would be Blink's overtype) — and Escape is the program's (Vim's modes), ⇧Escape giving the keyboard back instead (`attachWindowInput`'s `terminal`). Main's gate checks each event as a page's (§6.1, the same refusals — no ⌘ key of the app's, no click with ⌘, Ctrl or Shift, no zooming wheel — the same releases owed), a press making it the window in use and its guest the one using it; but a terminal has no page to send events to (its tab's view is down at 0×0), so what the gate lets through goes to the shell whole (`liveBrowser`'s `sendTerminal`: its keys, text and wheel, `LiveTerminalInput` on `IPC.liveTerminalInput`), and the terminal says it to its shell as the host's own keys (`TerminalSession.guestInput`, `lib/terminal/guest-keys.ts`). Each key is encoded by xterm.js's own keyboard service — the cursor keys' mode, the kitty keyboard protocol a program turned on, Ctrl's control characters, Option typing its character on a Mac — fed a key made from the guest's (the fields of a KeyboardEvent it reads, its legacy keyCode as Chromium's is: a letter's by the letter it types, wherever it is — AZERTY's Ctrl+A is ^A though A is on Q's key, its Ctrl+M Enter though M is on the semicolon's — anything else's by where it is), never an event sent through the shell's page: the shell's key handlers there (a menu's Escape, the desk's Shift, the Bar's) are the host's, and a capture-phase one would hear it before the terminal. Option's character, AltGr's (a Windows guest's browser says Ctrl+Alt: a key that then types a character other than a letter, a digit or a space types it, as xterm.js takes it on Windows, whichever protocol is on; a letter's Ctrl+Alt stays a chord), a capital and anything else xterm.js takes from the key's character, comes from the guest's own `char`; and Alt that made no character of its own (a Linux or Windows guest's Alt+B, Ctrl+Alt+X) is Meta, as xterm.js makes it off a Mac — on this Mac, where it has no bytes for it (Option types, not Meta), ESC and the key's bytes without Alt. Which Alt a key's is the guest page says: a key down with Alt carries `mac` (§4, optional — a page from before it, or a host that drops it, is as before), and a Mac's Option types whatever it makes, ASCII too (German Option+L's @, British Option+3's #), where a PC's Alt is Meta whatever it makes; a page that does not say is taken at its character (past ASCII, Option's) — kept by guest (`LiveTerminalInput.from`): two typing into one terminal at once, one's keys land between the other's key down and its char. The terminal's own keys go as they do for the host (⌥← ⌥→ a word, Enter starting a shell that ended), but no ⌘ key: ⌘ is the app's, and the app is the host's (find, ⌘K's clear, the type size are not a guest's). A paste (`insertText`, the guest's own text) goes as xterm.js pastes the host's — line breaks as Enter, bracketed for a shell that asks with every ESC in it shown as ␛, so no text pasted ends the bracket early and the shell runs none of it before Enter — and the wheel scrolls the scrollback (a line for each line's height; a program's mouse reports are not sent). The pointer's presses, moves and releases are nothing to the terminal: there is no selecting or clicking inside it from a guest. While its picture is held (the palette or a menu over the desk, the Live card over it, a carry lifted) the gate takes nothing for it (`paused`) and lets go of what a guest held there — they could not see what they typed run — though it stays theirs (`controllable` unchanged, so no banner comes and goes with every menu). Main's own record of the desk (`#windows`) counts a terminal that streams as a window guests' hands may reach, and still no tab page of the share's: never captured as a tab, held awake or laid out (`streams`).

## 7. The guest page: `apps/www/app/live/[token]/`

A full-bleed client page of its own (no session boundary, no account), on `@pistachio/live-share`'s room and React pieces and its stylesheet, looking like the Pistachio desk (Geist, the shell theme's tokens, dark first, glass on the toolbar). States: `loading → lobby → knocking → room → (ended | denied | removed | error)`. The room is the host and the guests the host lists (`LiveRoom`, §4 `lv.guests`): the faces, the people and the pointers are theirs, the guest's camera, microphone, pointer, ink, pings, reactions and chat go to them alone, and nothing of anyone else in the room is shown, played or heard. The page asks for the room (`lv.hello`) as it joins and after every reconnect, and the host's answer brings the list. A guest let in beside another sees them one hop of the host's list later — measured against a fake host that lets them in by hand: each page showed the other's face 43–108 ms and their camera 517–869 ms after the host sent the list (one machine, load 15–19, two runs) — and a chat typed before its list came (in the room, not yet let in) reaches the host and the others once it does. The stage is drawn from the latest `LiveDeskFrame`: one absolutely-positioned element per window (frame strip + a `<video>` attached to its track at the `page` box), transforms only, no React re-render on geometry; cursors and ink on canvas/SVG layers above, sized to the stage. Input on a controllable window is captured from the window's element (pointer events, wheel with `passive: false`, keys while it has the guest's focus, a hidden textarea for IME and paste) and sent as `lv.input` (`attachWindowInput`, kept in `@pistachio/live-share/react` to be tested). Main holds a guest's press until it hears its release (§6.1), so what the guest pressed is released from the page even when the release lands elsewhere: each key whose press went out, when the textarea loses the keyboard (the chat, another window, Escape); each key and the button, when the browser window loses focus, the tab is hidden, or the window's input is taken down. And what it let go of is let go again each time the host lists the guests (`lv.guests`): it does so as its own room comes back, to a guest back from a drop of theirs as they say hello, and on a change of who is in. A reliable message sent as either side's connection went can be lost (probed with livekit-server 1.13: some lost across a failed resume, a full reconnect and a migration, on the sender's side and the receiver's; none across a signal reconnect), and with it a release, which would leave the press down on the host's page; so the last release of each key that is up now and of the button goes again — one each, however long the page has been up, and none of what is down again since — and main ignores one of nothing it holds (§6.1). A window brought up to fill the view fades the others out and then takes them out of the layout (`display: none`), so the room's adaptive stream sees their video off screen and the SFU stops sending it (transparent, they stayed "visible" to LiveKit and were sent and decoded behind the filled one). The share the link names is fetched as soon as the page's first chunk runs, alongside the app's own chunk; the background effects' code and files load only when a background is chosen, never before the lobby works. A reload keeps the request it rejoins with: LiveKit lets the room go as the old page goes (its page-leave disconnect, on `beforeunload`, `pagehide` or `freeze`), and the room ends that as lost, never as left — `left` is its own `disconnect()` alone (`LiveRoom`), and the page forgets the request only once the guest leaves or is out (denied, removed, let go, the share ended). (Read as left, the old page forgot it while the new one was on its way, and a slow network or server brought the guest back to the lobby to knock again.) Rejoining after a reload, the room can end while the camera comes back (a permission prompt, the segmenter): the room keeps why it ended (`LiveRoom.endReason`) and the page reads it once the camera is back, and a join the page no longer wants is disconnected (`joinRoom`). The lobby's camera or mic can still be waiting on its permission prompt when the guest joins or leaves: one that comes after joining is the room's and goes out then (behind the background, on the device chosen in the room meanwhile), unless the guest turned that device on or off in the room meanwhile; one that comes after the lobby was left, or after a later choice for it, is stopped as it comes (`LivePreview`). The lobby stays up while the room joins — the microphone's first publish can take a while — with its switches live: from the moment the room has the lobby's tracks, the lobby's switches, devices and background are the room's (`LivePreview.handOver` hands them over with the tracks) and it shows what the room has on, so a microphone turned off then goes out muted, never live, a camera turned off not at all, and the background and devices chosen last are what go out. Background choices on one camera take turns and the last one shows, one made while the segmenter loads included (`applyBackground`). A camera goes out behind the background chosen, or not at all (`LiveRoom`, so the host's camera too): however it comes into the room — turned on there, the lobby's, one whose prompt was answered late — the background, the one chosen last whatever is chosen while it loads, is put on it before it is published, and a background is on only once its processor has put out a processed picture (`applyBackground`; none in 60 s, `FIRST_PICTURE_TIMEOUT_MS`, and it did not load) — never the camera's frame as it came, which the library puts out first from a new processor, after LiveKit has swapped a camera already out onto its track, so each processor starts past it (a camera already out holds its last picture until the processed one comes); and one whose background cannot be put on (it did not load, effects are unsupported) is stopped unpublished, the camera off and the person told as for a camera that did not start (`setCamera` rejects; the lobby's, with no one to tell, is reported). In the lobby too a camera turned on with a background chosen that cannot be put on stays off, as the room would keep it (`LivePreview.setCamera` rejects). A background changed on a camera that is on and cannot be put on gives way to the one the camera had: the effects put that one back on it (a switch that fails can leave the processor between the two — `backgroundOn` says what a camera shows), it is the room's or the lobby's choice again (`background()`), and the menu says it again, with why (`chooseBackground`: a line in the room, the lobby's note); a camera nobody can say that of any more is taken out of the room (stopped, in the lobby). So the camera keeps going out behind what it was going out behind, and the menu never names a background the camera does not show. A change that fails after a later one came is let go: the later one answers. The lobby's camera does not hold up the join. Off, the camera is unpublished and its capture stopped, never muted (LiveKit unmutes a camera as it is: raw while its background loaded again); turned off while it comes, it never goes out. An off waits on nothing loading for the camera: turned off while a background change loads on it, it leaves the room at once, and the change landing after is let go — it brings nothing back and tells nobody. A room LiveKit gave up on joins again from scratch with a fresh token (`LiveRoom`, the host's too), and LiveKit lets go of every publication of the room's meanwhile, so its own switches find nothing to turn off or move: what is chosen while the room is away is made on the tracks the room keeps, and they go out again as last chosen, never as they were before it went — a mic turned off meanwhile goes out muted (off, as LiveKit turns one off), never live; a camera turned off, not at all; each on the device chosen last (LiveKit's switch, with nothing published, only notes it for the next capture); the camera behind the background chosen last. Each goes out again on its own — the camera, the microphone, each window — and one that does not go holds up none of the others. A camera or microphone that does not go is tried again 1, 3 and 8 s on while it is still the one kept (the room away again meanwhile, it goes out as the room joins), and still not out after that is taken out of the room, as one whose publish fails as it comes on: stopped, kept no more, and the person told (`mediaFailed`: the host's Live card says it is off, and why; the guest's switch says why, a click from trying again, and a line says "Your camera went off: it didn’t come back after the connection dropped.") — never left kept and not out, off to its switch with its light on, an on finding it there and sending nothing, the next rejoin putting it out unasked. One that stopped while it was not out — its capture ended (a device unplugged) or LiveKit stopped it as a publish timed out — is never sent, dark or silent while it read as on: it is taken out so at once. A microphone that was off (muted) is taken out without a word: the person is missing nothing. A window that does not go out again is the host app's to ask for, as it does once the room is back, and again until it goes (§6.3). The connection lost again while the room puts its tracks out again is the latest loss's to answer: the room reads reconnecting until that rejoin is in, and nothing the earlier one had not put out yet goes out before the latest has said who may have it. Meanwhile, and while a camera or microphone is tried again, the person's own switches read as they left them — what the room keeps on (`participants()`'s own entry), not what LiveKit has out this moment — and off once one is taken out. A microphone whose publish fails with the room here — turned on in the room, the lobby's as the guest joins, a late one, one turned on between those tries — is off, as whoever asked for it is told: muted, so nothing sends it out live after, neither a try nor the next rejoin. A mic turned on meanwhile with none kept is captured by the room and goes out with the rest, after the room has said who may have it (LiveKit's own would publish one of its own the moment the connection is back, ahead of that), and not at all if it is turned off again before it comes. The same holds for a kept mic until it is out again: turned off as it goes out (the lobby's, a late one), it is muted. And not only while away: a mic turned on with none kept is always the room's own capture, never LiveKit's switch, whose off waits for that capture and its publish and then mutes what went out live (past ten seconds on a permission prompt it stops waiting, and leaves it live) — so a mic turned off while it comes on is stopped as its capture lands, or goes out muted if it is being published, never live; and a mic out but muted, turned on again on another device, is switched while still muted, so it comes on on that one. A camera or microphone chosen while one is still coming in — on its permission prompt, a camera behind its background — is the one it goes out on: the room puts the capture on that device before it publishes it (LiveKit's switch, with nothing published, only notes it for the next capture), and a camera asked on again on another device while it comes is that camera, on that device. A device named `"default"` is the system's, asked for as LiveKit asks when none is named, not exactly: Chromium has no camera of that id (probed), and LiveKit stops a camera before it captures the next, so an exact one would leave it out dark. A camera let go (turned off, the room or the lobby left) is stopped for good (`stopCamera`): a background processor still loading for it is destroyed as it lands, its segmenter and WebGL context with it, and none is made for it after. The camera's switch and the microphone's, in the room and the lobby, read as on while the device comes on (its prompt, a camera's background loading; `switchReadsOn`), and turning one off then — a click, or its key in the room — goes at once (`MediaSwitches`): the room or the lobby stops it before it is out (a microphone turned off as the room publishes it goes out muted, never live), and the on it overtook is neither remembered as the guest's choice nor tried again on another device. A switch that could not come on — blocked, missing, busy — is never disabled for it: off, it carries a mark and says why in its tooltip (the lobby's note too), and a click tries again, which is how a setting changed or a device plugged in is found. The why is also forgotten once it stops holding, with no click (`MediaSwitches.watch`, from `useMediaSwitch`): a device plugged in (`devicechange`) with one of its kind then listed, the newest list answering, forgets "not found"; the camera's or the microphone's permission no longer denied (the Permissions API's `change`, where the browser has one for them) forgets "blocked"; and in the room a camera or microphone chosen from its menu that took forgets that kind's (`forget`; in the lobby a choice is itself an on, which answers). The room keeps whether the browser refused to play its sound (`LiveRoom.audioBlocked`): LiveKit says so once, often as the room joins (after a reload, with no gesture yet), before the page listens, and the page reads it as it mounts (`useAudioBlocked`) — "Sound is off in this tab", with *Turn on*. The guest's choices — the camera and the microphone on or off, and the device of each and of the speaker — are remembered across visits once each took, as the fields it changed laid over the choices as they stand when it lands (`GuestMediaMemory`), never over a copy taken when it was made: a camera that comes on after the microphone was turned off leaves the microphone off after a reload. What is behind the person is remembered with them, the moment it is chosen rather than once it took (a reload while it loads brings the camera back behind it or not at all, never behind the one it replaced): none, blur, a built-in scene by its name. The guest's own picture is not kept — a page closed lets go of it — only that it was one. The page starts with the background remembered, in the lobby and when it joins with no lobby (a reload while admitted, or again after the room was lost), and a camera comes back only behind it: the background is the room's before the camera is turned on, so the room puts it on before the camera goes out (`restoreMedia`). One the page cannot put back — the guest's own picture, a scene no longer offered, any background where the browser cannot run one — holds the camera off, with nothing behind the person in the menus, until the guest answers it: a background chosen, or the camera turned on as it now is (`GuestMediaMemory.held`). One that does not load keeps the camera off as it does any camera's. Each way the guest is told — the lobby's note, or a line as the room comes up ("Your camera is off: your own background picture isn’t kept once the page closes.", `describeCameraHold`). The speaker remembered plays the room from its first sound, joined from the lobby or after a reload (`LiveRoom.connect`'s `audioOutput`; one gone, or a browser that cannot choose, plays on the default, which the menu then marks); after a reload the microphone and camera come back on the devices remembered, or on the default for one that is gone, as the lobby starts them (`restoreMedia`, `GuestMediaMemory.resume`, `MediaSwitches.setOnOrDefault`) — only for one gone (no such device), not one refused, busy or failing otherwise: the default would fail the same way, or be a camera the guest did not choose. The default that came on in place of one gone is remembered in its place, unless another was chosen meanwhile, so the menu names the device in use and turning it off and on again asks for that one, not for the one gone. A sync's grants (who uses which window) are merged as its ink is, as of when the guest began asking: a grant heard since stays over the word of every answer to that asking (`GuestDesk`). A guest whose host has been gone from the room 15 s asks control where its request stands — the call a reload makes — and again every 30 s until the host is back, under a line "Waiting for Zach" (`HostAbsence`): a room can outlive its share for a while (LiveKit did not answer control's delete and a later sweep closes it, or the host's app went without a word and control ends the share once it falls silent, §5), and an answer that the share ended, or that this guest was removed or let go, ends the page as LiveKit's own word would. A host gone a moment, their app joining the room again, costs nothing.

**Link preview.** Pasted in a chat, an email or an invite, the link unfurls with what the lobby says, read off control on the server as the lobby reads it (`GET /v1/live/join/:token`, 3 s at most; `lib/live/preview.ts`): `generateMetadata` in `page.tsx` gives the title and description, and `opengraph-image.tsx` the card (1200×630, the page's dark scheme and Geist, the lobby's little desk drawn large). Live: "Zach is sharing “Launch plan”", the card *Live now* with the host's pointer, the reader's own ("You") by a ring they drew, and the host's face. Ended: "“Launch plan” is no longer live", the desk dimmed and empty. A link control does not know, or did not answer for, says only "Live share" and claims nothing of it. The card is drawn again after a minute (`cache-control: max-age=60`, not next/og's year), so a link unfurled after its share ended says so; what a chat kept of an earlier unfurl is the chat's. The page stays `noindex` and `no-referrer`.

## 8. Shared React pieces: `packages/live-share/src/react/`

Used by the guest page and the live-host app alike: `CursorLayer`, `InkLayer` (render + the drawing gesture), `PingLayer`, `ReactionLayer`, `FaceStack` / `FaceTile`, `MediaButton`s, `DeviceMenu`, `BackgroundMenu`, `ChatPanel`, and hooks over a `LiveRoom` (`useParticipants`, `useLocalMedia`, `useTopic`). ## 9. Performance

- **Per-frame paths stay out of React and out of the snapshot**: cursors are interpolated and drawn imperatively (one rAF loop per layer, transforms only); desk geometry is applied to window elements by ref; ink in progress draws on its own canvas.
- **Nothing animates at rest**: the cursor layer's loop stops the moment every pointer is still (a timer wakes it when one begins its rest fade 5 s later); the ink layer draws when the board or the gesture changes, and every frame only while a laser fades; the board says when a laser has faded out or an open stroke has gone idle (a timer), not at the next op. The guest's top-bar live dot rings three times as the room goes live (and when the connection comes back), with transform and opacity only, then is still; so does the host's Live pill dot (as the room goes live and as someone comes or goes), and the shell's live mark and card dot pulse three times, then are still. The live-host window, transparent over the whole browser window, has no `backdrop-filter` (it blurs nothing there and costs a pass).
- **Ink canvases have pixels only while there is ink or a pen in hand**: an empty board costs no backing store (two stage-sized canvases are ~40 MB on a Retina display).
- **Cursors**: sent at most every 16 ms (and only when moved), lossy; drawn at display rate by interpolating toward the latest sample (time constant 8 ms: settled in ~24 ms), hidden after 5 s still, removed after 30 s or when the participant leaves. The throttle and the glide add ~12 ms (p95 17 ms) to the host's pointer before any network, simulated with the package's own classes (`test/cursor.test.ts`); at 33 ms and an 80 ms glide they added ~47 ms. End-to-end pointer latency on one machine through a local SFU stays under 100 ms (§12 measures it).
- **Pings and reactions**: a node and a timer each while it plays (1.6 s, 2.6 s); at most 4 a second (8 at once) of anyone's, and at most 48 up in a layer, the oldest taken down early (§4).
- **Syncs**: within `lv.sync`'s 8 MiB; a full board is ~5.7 MB on the ink's grid; a sync is measured once and cut, if ever, by measuring only what goes (§4).
- **Desk frames**: only on change, ≤ 30 Hz in motion, typically < 2 KB.
- **Video**: one encode per window regardless of guests (SFU), in software on the Mac (OpenH264, no content hint: the SFU takes H.264 as Constrained Baseline, which Chromium never encodes on macOS's hardware, §3); simulcast + adaptive stream at the guest's pixel density; static pages cost ~0 fps; minimized windows unpublished; windows behind a filled one not sent; a recaptured or resized window keeps its publication (no blank, no re-subscribe); ≤ 8 window tracks.
- **Host CPU**: the cursor poll runs only with guests present and the host present; the shared windows are held awake (woken, laid out) and captured only while a guest is in the room and for 30 s after the last leaves (a capture keeps its page rendering and read back, watched or not); a guest joining is sent the room once (on their hello), not twice; the shell's own page is captured only while a shell-drawn window streams, a guest is in and the host is on the space (§6.6), and every repaint of the shell is then a frame of each such window (the capture says nothing of where it repainted), a still one a frame a second, each read for its mark first (a copy of a few pixels, 0.1–0.2 ms); the live-host window costs nothing when no share is live (it does not exist, nor does its session).

## 10. Security and privacy

- Only the shared space's windows leave the Mac, each as its own page picture; the shell, the sidebar, other spaces, menus, the palette, notices and other apps never do. Shell-drawn windows (§6.6) send only their box, cut inside the stage, and only while the host is on the space and nothing of the shell's lies over the window — something whose box is known over it, anything else over the desk (held, a window sends its last picture from before again, nothing new); the word that something does is heard before it is painted, and each frame bears the number of the report it was painted under, painted in the same task as that report is sent, and is cut by that report's word alone, however late it comes; a frame painted once a window has moved and its report not yet gone bears that number stale, painted in the task that moved it, and is cut for no window that moved — so no frame is cut where a window no longer is, nor at a box a window is not drawn in (carried lifted, it is held).
- A knocking guest has no LiveKit token and receives nothing until admitted. Someone in the room is sent nothing and let subscribe to nothing until control, asked after they came in, lists them as let in, nor once a read that set out after they came no longer lists them — within 15 s while knocking, a minute with anyone free to join (§6.1). A removed guest cannot mint again; a removal LiveKit or control did not take is asked again until it is; back with the token they hold (LiveKit does not revoke it), they are removed again at once and are meanwhile no guest of the host's — no input, no chat, nothing the host app sends them or lets them subscribe to (§6.1). Nor anything of the guests': LiveKit's subscription permissions are each publisher's own, so each guest lets only the host and the guests the host lists (`lv.guests`) subscribe to its camera and microphone, sends its word to them alone, and hears, shows and plays nobody else — the host alone until its first list, and from a reconnect until a fresh one (§3, §4). A removed guest LiveKit still has (control down while LiveKit is up), or a token minted outside control, gets nobody's camera, microphone, pointer, ink or chat; it takes honest guests (a guest's own client decides whom it lets in, as the host's does) and the host's room up for the list to change. Ending deletes the room, asked again until LiveKit says it is gone (§5). Control opens every room itself, and a production LiveKit creates none on a join (§11), so a token held past the end opens nothing.
- The link is an unguessable capability (256 bits), stored hashed, dead when the share ends. Request secrets likewise.
- A link's preview (§7) shows what the lobby shows anyone holding the link — the host's name, the space's, whether it is live — and nothing else of the share; a chat that unfurls it keeps its own copy.
- Guest grants allow publishing a camera and a microphone and data; nothing else. Participant metadata (role, colour) is set by control.
- Every message is schema-checked, sender-checked and rate-limited; a guest's input goes to the host alone, never through another guest's client (§4), and reaches only a window the host has allowed, only while the host is on the space, never the shell or another window — but for the release of a press main let through, which can only end that press, and which main sends itself the moment the guest's hands come off (§6.1). `sendInputEvent` never reaches the app menu, so a guest's ⌘Q, ⌘W or ⌘V does nothing to the app or the host's clipboard (paste is the guest's own text, as `insertText`); the clipboard chords Blink does run for it (Shift+Insert, Ctrl+Insert, Shift+Delete) never leave `toElectronInputEvents` (§4 Input).
- A text stream is held only from a sender whose role may send its topic, two at a time per sender, within a budget for all streams; the rest is read off and dropped (§4), so a guest cannot fill a renderer's memory with "syncs".
- What a participant sends cannot pile up where it lands, or spoil what others are sent: every receiver meters the pings and reactions it hears, not the host alone (guests hear each other directly), and draws at most 48 at once; ink is kept on its grid and a sync is cut to what a guest reads, the oldest strokes first, so no one's ink or chat makes the room's syncs refused and leaves later guests without a board or chat (§4).
- A camera with a background chosen is never sent without it: it is published only once the background is on it — on, once the processor has put out a processed picture, and never a frame before: `@livekit/track-processors` puts a new processor's first frame out as it came, after LiveKit has swapped a published camera onto its track, so every processor starts past that frame (`isFirstFrame`) and one that cannot is refused (`effects.ts`) — and stays off when it cannot be — after a reload too, where the background is remembered with the camera (the guest's own picture only as having been one: the picture is never stored) and one the page cannot put back holds the camera off, the guest told (§7); a change that cannot be put on leaves it behind the one it had, which the menu then names again — the host's Live card too (§6.1) — and the person is told (§7). Turning a camera off waits on nothing loading for it: it is stopped and unpublished at once, on the Mac and in a guest's page, one still coming on included (§6.3, §7). A mic or camera turned off while the room joins again from scratch stays off when it is back — the mic goes out again muted, the camera not at all — and one turned on then goes out only once the room has said again who may have it (§7).
- With *Guests can draw* off, no board shows a guest's new ink, and a guest's page shows at most 5 chat messages a second from anyone (§4).
- The live-host window's camera/mic grant and WebRTC policy apply to it alone; page sessions are unchanged. It is sandboxed, with a preload of its own: nothing of the shell's API (tabs, the snapshot, navigation) reaches the renderer that parses guests' messages, and main answers its channels for it alone. A tab capture handed to it is of the page's picture, never its sound.
- A masked window is never streamed (its capture would be its whole page, §3). A guest's hands are refused any button but the left, a click with ⌘, Ctrl or Shift (a context menu, a tab or a window of the host's), a zooming wheel, and the app's own keys (§6.1). While the Mac is locked or asleep, or the app hidden, the host is away: guests' hands are off every window.
- Anyone in the room sees every window of the space; the Live card says so ("Guests see every window in this space").
- Not end-to-end encrypted in this version: the SFU (ours, or LiveKit Cloud) relays media and data it can read. §14.

## 11. Configuration and development

Control: `LIVEKIT_URL` (the `wss://` URL clients connect to), `LIVEKIT_API_URL` (optional; the server API, default the `LIVEKIT_URL` with `http(s)`), `LIVEKIT_API_KEY`, `LIVEKIT_API_SECRET`, `LIVE_MAX_GUESTS` (default 8). The dev server defaults them to a local `livekit-server --dev` (`ws://127.0.0.1:7880`, `devkey` / `secret`). Each name is in `turbo.json` `globalEnv`, `.env.example`, and `docs/cloud-sync-design.md` §11's list. Links are built on `PISTACHIO_WEB_URL` (www).

Local: `brew install livekit`, then `pnpm dev:livekit` (`scripts/livekit-dev.mjs`: runs `livekit-server --dev --bind 127.0.0.1`, or says how to install it). Production (decided 2026-10-19): LiveKit Cloud, a project on the free Build tier (5,000 participant-minutes, 50 GB downstream and 100 concurrent connections a month), its URL and key pair set on Railway's `control` service; nothing else is configured for it, as clients get the URL from control. Its billing is per GB sent, so past about 1–2 TB a month a self-hosted `livekit-server` (with UDP and a TURN/TLS domain; Hetzner or Fly) costs a fraction; moving is the same four variables, and a self-hosted server can also offer H.264 High for the Mac's hardware encoder (§3). A self-hosted server runs with rooms created by control alone:

```yaml
room:
  auto_create: false
```

Control opens each share's room as it starts and again at every heartbeat, and deletes it as the share ends (§5), so nothing depends on a join creating one; with this off, a token held past the end (a guest's lasts 10 minutes, §3) finds no room to open again (LiveKit answers 404 "requested room does not exist"; probed with livekit-server 1.13). `livekit-server --dev` keeps creating rooms on a join; nothing in development needs it to stop. Where rooms are created on a join (the dev server, LiveKit Cloud unless its project says otherwise), such a token can open an empty room again until it runs out.

# The double sidebar

(The code calls a space a tab group, and a Profile a Space: `docs/spaces.md`.)

On the desktop the sidebar is two things side by side: a narrow **rail** at the window's edge, holding Home and every space that has more than one window, and beside it a raised **card** with the chosen one in detail. Home reads like an ordinary browser sidebar: favorites, pinned pages, the day's tabs, each of them a full-desk tab. A space reads like a small control room for its desk: its name, its layouts, its tabs, its Context. Chosen on 2026-10-13 from four live variants (the Double Sidebar Lab, https://claude.ai/artifact/6egUyBv1Vw2D8FLqywUaqP), variant B, *Folder tab*: the rail and the card are one object, and the chosen rail item sits on a tab that grows out of the card's edge, as a folder's tab does.

Desktop only: it needs the desk. The web app and the cloud browser keep the single sidebar (`SidebarChrome`), unchanged. The gate is `deskAvailable()`, as for the rest of the desk.

## 1. What a person sees

**The rail.** 56px (`SIDEBAR_RAIL_W`), on the window's own ground, the traffic lights above it. Top to bottom: **Home** (a house), a hairline, then — a tile's beat below, as the tiles are from each other — every **rail space** as a 38px tile in its colour holding its first four tabs' icons in a 2×2 (the space mark the sidebar already had, `FaviconCluster`, a size up) with its tab count on the corner when it holds more than those four; at the foot **New space** (a +) and the **Profile** avatar (its menu, `SidebarMenu`). A space being named shimmers. The pointer resting on a tile names the space and how many windows it holds. The rail scrolls, without a scrollbar, when the spaces outgrow it.

The rail's spaces: every drawn space (`snapshot.tabGroups`: two or more tabs, or the person's own of one or none, an empty space showing its ring), and every page's group with two or more tabs (a favorite's or a pin's page with tabs opened beside it: `snapshot.anchorGroups`), drawn spaces in the row's order, then page spaces in their entries' order (favorites, then pins). A day tab alone (a loose space) and a favorite's or pin's page alone are Home's.

**By the keys** (Slack's, 2026-10-14): ⌘1 is Home, ⌘2 the first space under it, and so on to ⌘9 the eighth, counted as the rail lists them (`chooseRailSpace` in `space-rail/actions.ts`, the rail's own order: `railSpacesOf` with `railEntryOrder`); a key past the last space does nothing. Each is chosen as its tile's click chooses it, from the shell or from a page holding the keyboard (main hands the key on). They are Settings › Keyboard's **Spaces** group, the desktop's alone (the web draws no rail), and can be given other keys there; listed after every other action, so a ⌘-digit someone had given another action before stays with it.

**The card.** A raised sheet (`background-100`, radius 12, a hairline edge and one soft shadow) set 8px in from the window's top and bottom, the desk's well 8px to its right, the twin of the well on the other side. Its outline is one shape: a **tab** grows out of its leading edge, joined by concave fillets, to hold the chosen rail item, and a **shoulder** steps around the traffic lights, which sit in a pocket of the ground. Choosing another rail item slides the tab to it (230ms on the house ease, `cubic-bezier(0.2, 0.8, 0.3, 1)`) while the card's contents cross-fade (190ms). The card's width is the sidebar's width setting (200–380px, 224 to start, its trailing edge dragged as before).

On the card's shoulder, right of the traffic lights: back, forward, reload (the toolbar's navigation), and at its trailing end the desk's ⋯ (the More card: Tile, Cascade, Arrange for me, Feel) and the sidebar button (to the rail). Under them, the full width of the card, the **address** field (the page in use; ⌘L). Then the chosen view, and at the card's foot the footer's pills (sync, downloads, update) when they have something to say.

**Home** is chosen whenever the space in use is one of Home's: a loose tab, or a favorite's or pin's page alone. It shows:
- the **favorites**, three to a row (`FavoritesGrid`); a favorite whose page has tabs beside it carries their count; chosen, a favorite opens in Home — its page there, or a fresh one: a page of it in a space stays that space's, a page's space becoming a space of its own (`docs/spaces.md` §1; until 2026-10-14 it chose its rail space);
- **Pinned** (pins and folders, as before);
- the **New tab** row (New folder and New space on its hover, as before);
- **Today** (the old Live tabs, with Tidy): the day's loose tabs as rows: icon, title, and on hover **+** (*Open a window beside it*) and ×. The one in use is the raised row, its Context row under it as before.

**A space** is chosen by its tile (`selectSpace`). Its view: a wash of its colour at the card's top; its **name** (15px semibold, in its colour; a double-click renames it), its window count, its ⋯ (the space's own menu: rename, colour, new tab in space, release the tabs, close space — a page's space, which goes by its page's name and colour, has its own: new tab in space, *Make it a space of its own* (its page lets go of its favorite or pin, which stays, closed, and it is an ordinary space, named and coloured), release the tabs, close the tabs beside it); its **layouts** (whenever its desk has windows, documents included); a hairline; its **tabs** as rows (the one in use a card tinted with its colour, × on hover, the desk's window marks); **New tab**; and at the card's foot its **Context** row (the Stack).

**Layouts** (`LayoutControl`): one segmented track of the layouts its windows allow, as line drawings: one window, *Fill*; two, *Side by side*, *Stacked*, *Main and side*, *Cascade*; three or four, *Tile*, *Main and side*, *Columns*, *Cascade*; five, *Tile*, *Main and side*, *Cascade*; more, *Tile*, *Cascade*; then, apart, **Arrange for me** (the sparkle: the layout model, ⌘⌥L). The layout the desk is in holds the track's thumb; windows set down by hand hold none. Resting on a layout draws where each window would go as ghosts on the desk and names it under the track ("Main and side: the window in use takes 62%, the rest stack beside it"); a click lays the windows out, a beat apart, and for five seconds ⌃Z puts them back, as after Arrange for me. With the pointer resting on a layout, **Tab** moves its windows round its places (⇧Tab back) — two windows trade places, Main and side gives each the main place in turn — the ghosts showing where each now goes and the click laying them out so; on the layout the desk is in, nothing is drawn until Tab turns the windows where they stand. Main takes Tab from a page while the pointer rests there (`DeskState.cycleKey`); with the keyboard on the track, Tab still moves between its segments (since 2026-10-14). Tile, Cascade and Arrange keep their shortcuts (⌘⌥T, ⌘⌥C, ⌘⌥L), and Tile and Cascade (the More card's too) are these same layouts, ⌃Z and all.

Which window goes where: the window in use takes the main place (Main and side's, Tile's first: the left half, of three) — none of them in use, the largest takes Main and side's — and the rest go to the places nearest them; Side by side, Columns and Stacked keep the windows' order across or down, so none crosses another; Cascade keeps their stacking, the window in use brought to the front. A layout the desk is in already moves nothing (Tile on a tiled desk does not swap the window in use into the first tile), and the desk stays in it when it is resized (⌘S, the window, the chat's room) until a window is moved, opened or closed. Main and side is offered only while its column holds the rest at a window's least height (`focusRoom`), at any number of windows, so a short desk loses it sooner than five; Columns only while each column is a window's least width, Stacked while each row is its least height. Arrange for me is on the track from two windows. Masked and minimized windows stay where they are, as Tile always left them, and are not counted. The caption at rest says the layout the desk is in, "Arranged by hand" with none, "Fill the desk: one window" with one; on a segment, that layout and its shortcut. The ghosts are a dashed outline in the space's colour with a faint wash of it and the window's icon (a document's file glyph); the windows recede under them.

**Home is a browser's tabs.** Each Home tab is the whole desk, and going between them goes from one full page to the next. A new tab made in Home — ⌘T, the card's New tab, the address bar's new tab, a link a page opens in a new tab, *Duplicate tab*, Reopen closed tab, a Glance filled — is a Home tab of its own beside the others in Today, never a second window on the one in use (since 2026-10-14; until then each of them grew the Home tab in use into a space of two). In main: `#joinGroup` and `#setBeside` never put a tab into a Home tab's space (a loose one, or a page's alone: `#isHomeSpace`) unless it is named — `newTab` with its id — or is a second window on its page: a Glance taken in as a tile beside it (`promoteGlance("tile")`).

**A Home tab becomes a space** when the person breaks it away from that browser:
- its row's + (*Open a window beside it*: `newTab` into its space, named), or its row or a window carried onto the desk beside it, or a Glance taken in as a tile beside it — a second window;
- **breaking away** (2026-10-14): its window taken off the whole desk — carried by its frame and let go anywhere short of filling it, resized, let go of with ⤢, masked — or something put in its Context (a file dropped on the desk, the Stack or the Bar; a note; another space's brought in). The desk engine says so (`DeskHost.brokeAway`, after the gesture: where the window is headed is not the desk's usable box) and DeskSurface asks main's `ownSpace`; main's Stack commands ask it themselves. It is the same space made drawn in place — its id, so the desk does not pass and the window stays where it was let go — the person's (`manual`, so it stays a space at one window), coloured, named by the namer; a page's lets go of its favorite or pin, linked to it (`docs/spaces.md` §1). With no room for another space it stays Home's.

Its row lifts and flies to its new tile on the rail, the card's tab slides after it, the card turns to the space: "Naming…", then its name, then its colour washes in. Grown by a second window, its desk splits: the page keeps the left half, the new window takes the right.

**And back.** A space grown so is made *for* the person (`origin: "auto"`), not theirs: closed down to one window, it goes back to Home as that tab, its tile flying into Today. Naming it, colouring it, filing something in its Context, holding a conversation in it, or bringing a tab to it (a drag, Add to) makes it theirs, and it stays on the rail at one window or none, until it is closed. Opening windows in a space never makes it the person's (decided 2026-10-13; until then a new tab did, and every grown space stayed drawn): so ⌘T in a space Tidy made leaves it Tidy's too (`docs/tab-tidy.md`). A grown space is not Tidy's, though (`TabGroupInfo.grown`): Tidy never archives it whole nor unmakes it, its windows being the person's to close. Gone back to Home, it lets go of its name and colour, so grown again it is named afresh, not under the name it had.

**The three ways to stand** (⌘S, Settings › General, `layout.sidebar`, as before):
- *Whole*: the rail and the card.
- *Rail*: the rail alone; the chosen tile on a backing of the card's colour (the tab without its card). Its head holds the button that brings the card back and the desk's ⋯; the traffic lights are hidden, as on the old rail.
- *Hidden*: the rail and the card come out together over the desk when the pointer reaches the window's edge (the overlay of `docs/spaces.md` §3).

**Dragging.** Rows keep their drags: within Today and within a space, onto the favorites and Pinned, out onto the desk. The rail takes drops: a row let go on a space's tile joins that space (at its end, a page's space's too); on Home, it leaves its space and is a Home tab; on New space — or anywhere else on the rail that is no item of it, its **ground**: between the tiles, below them, round New space (since 2026-10-26; until then a row let go there went nowhere) — it makes a space of its own, New space lit for it wherever it is held. A favorite or a pin let go on a space's tile brings its page into that space, and on New space (or the ground) makes a space of its page (opened, if it was closed; named as any space made there, not chosen); Home takes neither, being theirs already. Either way the page stays its favorite's or pin's (`docs/spaces.md` §1): the favorite's tile or the pin's row shows it open there, a dot in that space's colour ("Open in Research" on its tooltip); closing that tab ends it, and so does choosing the favorite or pin, which opens it in Home, a page of its own, the tab staying the space's (earlier on 2026-10-14 choosing it went there, the desk passing to that space). While anything is over the rail it is drawn as what it would be there, a space's tile — the rail's own 38px, its icon on the tint of the space it would go into, the rail's neutral over Home, New space or nothing; the very face a desk window wears there — following the pointer above the rail, grown out of its row or tile and shrinking back into it over the card; it waits in its slot meanwhile, dimmed, and the item it would go to lights. Let go on that item, the tile goes into it and the item bounces; a space made on New space grows in on the rail where it stands. A tile scrolled out of the rail's sight is not there: the ground is. (Until 2026-10-14 the row waited in its slot with nothing following the pointer, a favorite's tile followed it under the rail, and favorites and pins took no New space.) A tile carried along the rail — over another tile's middle — lights that tile, shrinking to go into it: let go, the two spaces are one, every tab of the carried one joining the other at its end, its Context with them; the other keeps its name and colour, and a page's space takes one too. Carried between tiles, the tiles part to open the gap it would land in, and let go it moves its space there; far from the tiles, or off the rail, it goes back (until 2026-10-14 it moved beside the nearest tile however far, and nothing merged). A desk window carried over the column takes the same drops, drawn as what it would be dropped as — over the card showing its space one of its rows (let go, it collapses into its row), over the rail a space's tile (let go on a tile, its tab joins that space; on New space or the rail's ground, a space of its own; on Home, a Home tab; on its own space's tile, it collapses) — the item it would go to lit as for a row (2026-10-14; `docs/desk.md` §1 *Over the sidebar*).

## 2. How it works

- **Shell** (`packages/shell-ui/src`): on the desktop `SidebarColumn` draws `SpaceColumn` (`components/space-rail/`) in place of `SidebarChrome`, as wide as the rail (`SIDEBAR_RAIL_W`, 56), the card (the sidebar's width) and the gap to the desk (`SIDEBAR_CARD_INSET`, 8): `SpaceRail`, `SpaceCard` (the outline is one SVG path redrawn per frame while the tab slides; the views key on `"home"` or the space's id), `HomeView` and `SpaceView`. The rules are pure in `rail-model.ts` (`railSpacesOf`, `columnViewOf`, `cardOutline`; tests `test/double-sidebar.test.ts`). The tab follows its tile as the rail scrolls, riding the rail's edge while the tile is out of sight. The card's views cross-fade over a still copy of the view going (never two live lists). A space's name is edited in one place, the card's head (`useSpaceRename`): a tile's double-click, its menu's Rename, ⌘N's or New space's new space, a row's *New space with this tab*, *Group selected tabs* all choose the space and open it there — waited for by the column (`NEW_SPACE_EVENT`), never by the view that asked, which goes as the card turns. Everything in the card's views and footer, and the rail's list, is out of the window's drag region; the card's shoulder round its buttons and the rail's head and foot round theirs still move the window. The Profile's menu is the manifest's own `profile` region (the rail's foot; the web's footer draws it first, as before); on the rail alone it hangs out over the desk, the pages under it giving way to their stills, and beside the card it fits the column and raises nothing, so a page over the desk (Settings, the library) stays up while it is open. Tooltips on the rail are the native `title`, which draws above the desk's live pages. `TabList` takes a `view`: `"home"` draws Pinned, the divider and Today with the day's loose rows only (group units are the rail's); `"space"` draws the current space's tabs as its member rows (a page's space, its page first). Unset, it is the single list the web draws. The old single sidebar's rail — `SidebarChrome`'s `rail`, `RailFavorites` (its favorites folder), and the narrow branches of the list, the favorites, the address and the Context row — was dead code once the double sidebar was the desktop's, and was removed on 2026-10-13 (done): `SidebarChrome` is the web's, always whole; `useSidebarRail` now says only that the rail stands alone (SpaceRail provides it round the Profile's menu).
- **Which view**: `"home"` when the current space (`snapshot.currentGroupId`) is loose or a page's group of one, else the space's id. Choosing Home on the rail chooses the Home tab used last (or, with none, opens a new tab in Home). A Home tab grown into a space — a new space on the rail holding a tab that was a Home tab (`grewInto`), not ⌘N's or New space's — turns the card a beat late (230ms), as its row's ghost lands on its new tile (`SpaceFlights`, a class component: the old row's box and copy are taken in `getSnapshotBeforeUpdate`).
- **Shelf drag**: the provider spans the rail and the card; rail tiles, Home and New space are a drop zone of their own (`RailDrop`: `addTab` at the space's end, `removeTab`, `create`; a favorite or pin on a tile comes down into it, on New space `bringDown`'s `newGroupId`; the rail on none of its items — `railTargetAt` finding nothing — is New space's, since 2026-10-26), read before the desk; a reorder in Today counts the day's row units the rail's spaces stand among (`fullUnitIndex`: just after the drawn unit before it); a drop in a space's view is a place among its rows and never leaves it (`spaceDropAt`). Over the rail the provider draws the item as `RailToken` (one element in the column's own layer, above the rail and the card; its face shares DeskWindow's `.desk-window-face` CSS), placed and reshaped per frame by the gesture (`paintToken`, `landToken`) and coloured by React from the drop; a space made by a drop on New space skips its flight up (`grownByDrop`, `flights.tsx`). A tile carried along the rail (`useRailMove`) measures the tiles once, at rest, and asks `railCarryAt` (`rail-model.ts`, pure: a merge over a tile's middle `RAIL_MERGE_SHARE`, a move to a slot between the drawn tiles within `RAIL_MOVE_REACH`, else nothing; tiles out of the list's sight take nothing) what a release would do; the gap is `railCarryShifts`, the move `move` (`railCarryTarget`, `railMoveIndex`), the merge `merge` (allowed by `canMergeSpaces`), asked once the tile has shrunk onto its target; the tiles keep their places until main's new order is in, then stand there at once (`data-snap`). A space gone by a merge does not fly back to Home (`SpaceFlights`). A page's space is not carried. Which entries are open on a page in a space is `entryLinksOf` (`rail-model.ts`; `useEntryLinks`), drawn by `FavoritesGrid` and `TabList`'s `PinRow`.
- **The desk's homes** (`DeskSurface`'s `sidebarHome`): a tab's row while it is drawn (or its favorite's tile), else its space's rail tile, or Home's button for a Home space; a document's, the Stack's row, else the tile; windows fly to and from there.
- **Layouts** (the engine): `DeskLayoutKind` (`fill`, `halves`, `stack`, `focus`, `tile`, `columns`, `cascade`), `layOut(kind)`, the layout the desk is in (`DeskView.layout`, recognised from the windows' boxes), `previewLayout(kind)` (ghosts drawn under a `layout-preview` cover), and ⌃Z for five seconds after. The geometry is `lib/desk/layouts.ts` (pure: `layoutsFor`, `layoutsOn`, `slotRects`, `layoutRects`, `layoutOf`, `layoutNow`, on `tileRects`, `cascadeRects`, `stackRects`, `columnRects` and the smart layout's `focusLayout`/`tiledLayout`); a layout is recognised when the windows' boxes — where they are going, `target ?? rect` — are its places as a set, each within 2px. Geometry alone loses a layout on a resized desk (a cascade's 32px step and centred box, Tile's grid and Main and side's rounded main place are not in proportion with it), so the engine also remembers, per space, the named layout it laid out and the box it gave each window (`#laidAs`, also the smart layout's when it is a named one); the desk is in it while the same windows stand in those boxes carried over to the desk as it is now (`holdsLayout`: `rescaleRect`, as the desk carries them, plus a gutter's share of the change for a desk carried in proportion), so nothing needs clearing — a window moved, opened or closed simply no longer matches — and a space passed from and back, or ⌃Z (the snapshot carries it), is in it again. `layoutsFor(count, room, bounds?)` gates Columns and Stacked by the least window size when given the desk (`columnsFit`, `rowsFit`; `layoutsOn(count, bounds)` says it all from the desk), and `layoutRects` refuses them past it. ⌃Z's snapshot carries each filled window's box to let go of the desk to (`restore`), which a layout drops. `DeskView.layoutCount` is how many windows a layout lays out, `DeskView.preview` the ghosts. ⌃Z is the smart layout's runner's (`SmartArranger` hears `onLaidOut`), with no notice; the agent's own tile or cascade keeps its Undo layout instead. `arrange("tile" | "cascade")` and the shortcuts are `layOut`. The ghosts are `LayoutGhosts` (in `LayoutControl.tsx`), drawn by `DeskSurface` once the cover is clear (`data-layout-preview` on the stage dims the windows). Tests: `packages/shell-ui/test/desk-layouts.test.ts`.
- **Main**: a window opened in a space (`#joinGroup`, `#setBeside`) no longer makes it the person's, and a loose space grown so is `auto` and `grown` (`addToTabGroup`; kept by `sanitizeTabGroups`, the session file and the sync mirror; cleared by `asPersons` — rename, recolour, a tab brought — and by going loose); Tidy leaves a grown space whole (`isTidysGroup`: not in `idleAutoGroupIds`, not unmade by Undo's `dissolveTabGroups`). The reconcile's step 3, and `withoutTabs`, turn an auto space of one back into a loose one with `looseInPlace`, which lets go of its name (`DEFAULT_TAB_GROUP_TITLE`, no `naming`; a name asked for before it went is not taken by its regrown self: `#namingAsks`). A tab opened straight into Home: `createTab(url, { group: "loose" })` (`ShellCreateTabOptions`, the preload, the `tabCreate` IPC, the store's `createTab(url?, options?)`; the default stays `"current"`). And the rail's width (`SIDEBAR_RAIL_W`).

## 3. Open

- The rail's order is the row's; a tile dragged moves the space among the row's units.
- A merge has no undo yet, and no way by keyboard or menu (a drag only). The rail does not scroll under a tile carried to its edge.

# Spaces

A **space** is where browsing happens: the unit that tabs, files and an AI conversation belong to. A space may be empty — a place to drop files into, to start a chat in, or to open tabs in — and it may be what a tab group was until 2026-10-09: several tabs kept together, named and coloured. Every listed tab is in exactly one space. On the desktop the space is shown as its **desk** (`docs/desk.md`): its tabs as free windows, its context as the Stack, its agent in the Bar. Since 2026-10-09 the desk is not a mode but the browser itself: there is no pane surface on the desktop any more, nothing to leave, nothing to toggle. The web app and the cloud browser (`docs/web-browser-design.md`) keep the pane surface; the desk is native-only, and nothing here changes them.

Vocabulary, because the code's names predate it: the code calls a space a **tab group** (`TabGroupInfo`, `tabGroupCommand`, `looseGroups`, `#reconcileTabGroups`), and calls a **Profile** — the per-partition browsing context with its own cookie jar, egress policy and cloud key (`contracts/spaces.ts`, `spaceId`, `activeSpaceId`) — a *Space*. Internal identifiers keep their names (the Profile's are on the wire, in sync records and in partition names); what a person reads says space and Profile. A comment that could be read either way says "space (tab group)" or "Profile (Space)".

## 1. What a person does

**The desk is always up.** The app opens on the current space's desk, its windows where they were left (the arrangement kept on this device as before, `pistachio.desk.v1`), masked and minimized ones as they were. A tab chosen anywhere — a row in the sidebar, the tab switcher, the address palette's open tabs, a favorite's page — passes the desk to that tab's space, as choosing a tab off the desk always did; the windows of the space left fly into its row, the chosen space's come out of theirs (`DeskEngine.switchGroup`, unchanged). There is no Leave the desk and no Toggle desk (⌥⌘\ is free); the More card keeps Tile, Cascade, Arrange and Feel.

**Every listed tab is in a space.** A day tab in no named space is a space of one, drawn as the tab alone, grey, with everything a space has (the Bar asks about the tab, the Stack stands under its row, files dropped on the desk are its) — the loose space, which was made only when a tab was chosen on a desk and is now made for every listed tab the moment it is listed (`TabGroupInfo.loose`; main's reconcile makes one for any listed tab in none). A favorite's, preset's or pinned page's tab is its page's space (`TabGroupInfo.anchorId`), made as the page opens, not when it is chosen. There is no page's own desk any more (`tab:` ids are gone): nothing is listed that no space can hold. Hidden tabs (the agent's research, `hiddenFor`; a read-aloud player; anything `unlisted`) are in no space.

**An empty space.** A space keeps standing when its last tab closes if it is the person's: made by hand (New space), renamed or recoloured, or holding anything in its Stack (a file, a fact, a snippet) or a conversation. The desk then shows an empty space: the well with nothing on it but the Bar's nub in its corner, and in the middle, quietly, the space's name and what can be done — New tab (⌘T), drop files here. The keyboard is the shell's (`activeTabId` is null; ⌘W, ⌘R, ⌘L's page half and find do nothing; ⌘L still opens the palette, whose choice opens in this space). (A loose or page's space kept so becomes a drawn one, and needs room: a Profile holds fifty drawn spaces, `MAX_TAB_GROUPS_PER_SPACE`, the session file's bound too; at the bound the emptied space goes, its Stack staying adoptable under its id — `keepWithRoom`, `overCapEmptySpaces` in `main/spaces.ts`.) A space with nothing of the person's in it — an auto space Tidy made, a loose space — goes with its last tab, and the desk passes to the space of the tab used last; with no tab left in the Profile at all, the desk shows a fresh empty space, so there is always one current. The current space is never dissolved from under the desk: main chooses its successor in the same step. A fresh install, with no session to restore, opens on a space holding one home tab, as the first window; after that main never makes a tab on its own.

**New space.** A third slot beside New tab and New folder at the foot of the day's tabs (the rail's + offers it on its menu): an empty space, named "New space" with its name field open, current at once. **Close space** (the space's menu) files it in the archive as one entry — its tabs, and the space itself, so an empty space with a Stack can be filed and restored with its Stack and conversation (the archive entry carries the space's id, and restoring reuses it). **Ungroup** (the space's menu; "Release the tabs" in the menu's words) sets its tabs loose, each its own space; the space itself stays, empty, if it holds a Stack or a conversation, and goes otherwise. A page's space whose page closes or is brought down keeps its other tabs as a space like any other, titled after the entry it was.

**Where an empty space stands** in the sidebar: where its last tab was — it does not jump to the end (`TabGroupInfo.beforeUnit`, the row unit it stands before; cleared once it holds a tab again, which goes into the tab order there). Dragged among the rows it moves as a group does, and a row dropped beside it lands exactly where the row showed it: the sidebar's drops count the day's row units with empty spaces among them (`reorderTab(tabId, index, "units")`, main placing by `placeAmongUnits`). Its row is its name and colour with no icons; on the rail, a ring of its colour (the Stack's pile over it when it holds something), no count. Its header is a drop target: a row let go on it joins it, and the window comes out on its desk if it is current.

**New tabs land in the current space.** ⌘T, the sidebar's New tab, a palette choice, Reopen closed tab, a link opened from the home page: all in the current space, out on the desk as the window in use (a home-page window for ⌘T; the General › New tabs setting does not apply on the desktop and is not shown there). A link a page opens beside itself (`window.open`, a ⌘-click) joins its page's space, as before. A tab the agent shows (`tab_show`) joins the space it was asked from. A background tab from any other path (an organization preset's first open, an import) is a loose space and never takes the desk.

**Tidy** (`docs/tab-tidy.md`) still archives idle auto spaces and groups related loose tabs, but never touches the current space, nor a space that is the person's: it does not archive a loose tab whose space holds a Stack or a conversation, and its Undo does not dissolve a space the person has since given something.

**Splits are gone on the desktop.** A desk lays windows out by hand or by its layout model; side by side is a tile (⌘⌥T, or Add as a tile on a Glance). The tab rows' split buttons, "Open as split view", ⌘\, the sidebar drag's split zones and a Glance's split action are the web's alone (`splitAvailable()`, false wherever the desk is). A split saved by an earlier desktop is folded as the session loads: its members already in one space stay there; two or more loose day tabs become a space of their own; otherwise the split is simply dropped. Nothing is lost but the split itself.

**The sidebar** has three ways to stand — whole, a rail, or hidden until the pointer comes to the window's edge — in one setting; see §3.

## 2. How it works

**Main owns which space is current** (`BrowserController`, `#currentGroupBySpace`, one per Profile; `currentGroupId()`; the snapshot's `currentGroupId`, on the tabs channel; persisted as `DurableSpaceSession.currentGroupId`, checked against the Profile's spaces when read). The invariant: *a tab in use is in the current space* — `activeTabId !== null ⇒ currentGroupId === groupOf(activeTabId)`; the current space is set on its own only when nothing is in use, that is, when it is empty. `#activateTab` is the one place a tab becomes the one in use, so it is where the current space follows (a tab passing to another Profile passes that Profile's current space). A space is chosen with no tab through `tabGroupCommand({ type: "select", groupId, tabId? })`: main makes it current and activates `tabId` if it is the space's, else the space's tab used last, else nothing (`activeTabId` null, an empty layout). The shell passes `tabId` as the window that was on top when the space was left (`passedEntry`; the arrangement is the shell's). "New space" is `create` with `tabIds: []` and `select: true`.

**No home tab on its own.** Every place main used to make a home tab when nothing was in use — `initialize`, `#settleClose`, `switchSpace`, `forkSpace`, `applyDurableSession` — now keeps or makes an empty current space instead. `#settleClose` prefers the closing tab's own space: its most recently used surviving tab; none, and the space is kept, the desk stays on it, empty; none, and the space goes, the tab used last in the Profile and its space. A fresh install (no session) is the one exception: one space with one home tab.

**`createTab` says which space** (main's `group: "current" | "loose" | groupId`, default loose): the shell's `createTab` IPC (its new-tab paths, a palette choice, the home page's links) and Reopen closed tab say current; a popup, and a page's context menu's *Open in new tab*, say beside; everything main opens on its own (a welcome page, an integration's consent, a scheduled task's page) is loose. A background tab never becomes the tab in use just because none is (the old rule at `#createManagedTab` is gone): only a tab made with `activate`, or one joining the current space, does.

**Reconcile** (`#reconcileTabGroups`, before every publish), in order: members gone or in another Profile leave their space; a page's space without its page lets go of it (kept and titled after the entry if it is the person's); an auto space of one that is not the person's becomes loose in place, keeping its id; every listed tab in no space gets a loose one, and an entry's open page its page's space; the current space is settled (a successor chosen before the current one could dissolve); empty spaces that are not the person's and not current dissolve; the row order (`groupedTabOrder`, with empty spaces at their `beforeUnit`). "The person's" is `isPersonsGroup` (`contracts/tab-groups.ts`, pure): `origin === "manual"` and neither loose nor a page's, or a Stack with items, or a bound conversation whose thread still exists, or held by a running desk turn (`holdGroup`). Main asks the Stack and the conversation store through one hook (`BrowserControllerHooks.groupHolds`), and re-reconciles when a Stack changes. A space has its own `spaceId` (the Profile's), set from the session file's nesting key on load and from the tab's Profile on creation, so an empty one still has a Profile (`#tabGroupSpaceId`, which counted members' Profiles, is gone, with the seventeen silent no-ops it caused on an empty group: add tab, new tab, move, close, show hidden tab).

**Persisted and synced.** A Profile with spaces is saved even with no tabs; an empty space is saved with its `beforeUnit`; `currentGroupId` is saved. No `TAB_SESSION_VERSION` bump (any other version wipes the session). Spaces do not sync live (they never did); they travel in the device's restore point, which now carries empty ones. The sync-protocol mirror of the session gains the fields it was missing (`loose`, `anchorId`, `beforeUnit`, `currentGroupId`). An older build dropping empty spaces on read is accepted.

**The archive** entry of a space carries its id (`ArchivedGroupEntry.groupId`) and may hold no tabs; restoring makes the space with the same id when it is free, so its Stack and conversation come back with it (Tidy's Undo the same).

**The desk follows main.** `DeskSurface` is mounted whenever the surface is native (`ContentArea`'s `BrowserSurface`), keyed by a boot instance that changes only on a Profile switch; it builds its engine once the snapshot, `currentGroupId` and the space are known, and a change of `currentGroupId` is the passing (`switchGroup`, or `regroup` in place when the tab in use stayed and its space changed under it). The shell no longer decides a space from the active tab (`deskFor`, the active-tab effect, `switchTo`, `opening`/`sidebarReady`, `leave`/`finishLeave`, the `"leaving"` phase and the desk store's own `groupId` are gone; `currentSpace()`/`useCurrentSpace()` in `lib/desk/open.ts` read the snapshot, `selectSpace()` sends `select`, `newSpace()` the empty `create`). A **cold start** (boot, a shell reload, a Profile switch) places every saved window at its saved box at once — masked ones masked, minimized ones parked, the window in use on top — with no lift-off (there is no pane under it) and no flights from rows; a space never on a desk comes up as its tab used last, filling the desk when it is its one tab. The engine does not report a desk until it is ready, and main ignores a malformed desk report rather than treating it as "no desk" (which dropped every mask and zoom).

**Waking.** The desk reports which windows it wants live (`DeskState.live`: the window in use and every uncovered, unminimized window; a cascade of eight wakes two); main wakes those, the window in use first and the rest one at a time as each paints, and leaves the others asleep as placeholders (favicon and title, no "Waking…") until they are raised or uncovered. A report without `live` wakes every window, as before. No desk window is ever put to sleep by the idle sweep.

**The agent's session follows main too.** `RunController` hears the current space from the browser (`hooks.onCurrentGroupChange` → `runs.followGroup`): the shell's `deskConversation` enter/leave are gone, as is the "conversation to go back to" (there is no off-the-desk). A turn keeps the space it started in (`holdGroup`), and when it ends the session re-follows the current space. A thread started from the Bar is bound to the space whose Bar it was. `#onDesk(tab)` means "in the current space, or in a held one".

**Splits unreachable in main** (`#formSplit` and its callers gone, `setSplit`/`splitWith`/`removeFromSplit`/`splitGlance` no-ops, the snapshot always `splitMode: "single"`, `splitGroups: []`); the contract keeps the shape for the web host. `foldSplitGroups` (pure) runs on the session as it is restored or applied.

**The update prompt** may show on the desk (it is a page over the surface like Settings; the `!deskUp` gate is gone).

## 3. The sidebar

One setting, `layout.sidebar: "whole" | "rail" | "hidden"` (`SidebarMode`, default whole; a file or record that still says `pinned` reads as whole, `compact` as hidden — `sanitizeSettings`, no record version bump), in place of pinned/compact and the desk store's own `rail` (dropped, not migrated: its stored `true` was the default, not a choice). **⌘S** (`toggleSidebarPinned` keeps its id, which is a key in everyone's saved shortcuts; its label is *Cycle sidebar*) goes whole → rail → hidden → whole (`nextSidebarMode`); on the web, where the rail is not offered (its favorites sheet, head buttons and now-playing all need a desk), whole ⇄ hidden, and a stored `rail` is drawn as whole (`effectiveSidebarMode`) and never rewritten. Each column has a button to the next place: the whole sidebar's toolbar collapses it to a rail, the rail's head brings the whole sidebar back, and the hidden column's toolbar keeps it open (whole); Settings › General offers the three. Renaming on the rail still brings the whole sidebar back, as a setting now.

- **Whole** and **rail** are as the desk had them (`docs/desk.md` §1 "The rail"): the column in the layout, the desk beside it. A change of mode runs the slot's width on the 250ms clock (`useDeskSlide`, keyed on the mode) and the desk rescales its windows as the stage changes, gutters kept (`rescaleRect`), as ⌘S did on a desk before.
- **Hidden** on the desk is an **overlay**, never a reflow (2026-10-09): the slot stays its 10px strip (`SIDEBAR_EDGE_W`) and the stage keeps the whole row less that inset, so `engine.measure()` never runs for a reveal and no page is laid out anew. The column is mounted as ever and translated out; brought out, it slides over the desk's leading edge (`data-overlay` raises the slot above the isolated `.desk-surface`), on a ground of its own (the favorites sheet's recipe: the theme's gradient over a frost, with a trailing shadow — with the desktop glass on, the chrome's own ground is transparent and the windows would show through). It is a **cover** over the desk (`setCover("sidebar", rect)` from the column itself, through a `useDeskCover` hook pulled out of `RailFavorites` and shared with it): its rect is the column's settled box in stage coordinates plus its shadow, so it leads every frame of the slide by construction; the windows under it give way to their stills, and the column slides in only once `clearCovers` lists it (intent, `sidebarRevealed`, apart from shown). Going, it keeps its cover and its layer until its transform has ended. A window taken in hand puts it away. The price, accepted: each reveal waits a capture round trip, and a window filling the desk is wholly its still while the column is out.
  - *What brings it out*: a pointer move in the slot's strip, and main's OS-pointer watch for the strip's last pixels under a live page (`syncSidebarEntryWatch`: hidden, not revealed, no drag capture, not veiled — re-synced from `setDragCapture`, so a window carried to the leading edge's snap zone passes over the strip without a reveal). On the desk the trigger is **5px** (`SIDEBAR_DESK_TRIGGER_W`, `pointerHitsSidebarTrigger`'s width parameter), half the strip: the 14px trigger lay entirely over the west resize edge of a window flush with the desk's leading edge.
  - *What makes it leave*: main's verdict as before (`pointerHoldsSidebar`, `onSidebarPointerLeft`, the 120ms check). What holds it, beyond a resize or a row drag: a desk card opened from it (the More card, the Stack's), a row's context menu, a row being renamed — each reaches past the column.
  - *The engine while the column is away* (`DeskHost.sidebarAway()`): a window put away flies to the window's left edge at its row's height (`#iconOf` keeps the row's y and puts x on the edge), and comes out from there; the column is never brought out for a flight (it would lie above the desk, and the window would pass under it). `sidebarHome` (`DeskSurface`) still finds the rows of a hidden pane — translated off the window's left edge, they keep their boxes — and the engine uses only their height then, never their x. A row pulled out of the overlaid column hands off at the column's own edge as before, and the column retreats once the row is in hand.
  - *Traffic lights*: the compact rule, on the shown state — hidden while the column is away, over its toolbar once it is in, gone 180ms after it starts to go; always on in native fullscreen; the rail hides them at once (`sidebarRail`). `ShellState.sidebarOnDesk` is gone (always true); `sidebarRevealed` means the column is on screen (whole and rail always, hidden while shown).
- On the web, hidden keeps reflowing the page as compact did.

## 5. Not done / open questions

- Spaces still do not sync live between Macs; an empty space reaches another Mac only in a restore point.
- The row order main keeps for empty spaces' anchors (`#rowOrder`) is in memory: after a relaunch, an empty space whose `beforeUnit` names a unit that is gone stands at the end.
- At launch the console opens the conversation bound to the current space, or none: a conversation open at quit that was another space's is not brought back.
- A conversation waiting on the person (a question, an approval, a takeover) is put away as it stands when they look at another space, and comes back with its question when they return (`RunController.#putAway`; until 2026-10-09 leaving the desk cancelled it). An answer sent by iMessage while it is put away is taken all the same: its space comes up (main makes it current — the person answered it), its conversation opens there, and the answer runs at its own desk; if another conversation is acting at that moment, the answer waits for its turn to end (`RunController.answerIMessageQuestion`).
- The shell cannot ask `createTab` for a loose space: its IPC always lands in the current space (main's own openings are loose).
- A tab chosen within a few milliseconds of ⌘T can be overridden by the new tab's own later activation (the `newTab` command selects after it creates); the person never hits it, `desk-entries.spec` waits for the new tab to be in use first.
- The "Fill the desk, pressed with its tooltip up" check (`desk-grow.spec`) failed late in three of four full suite runs on 2026-10-09 and passed alone every time: the window stayed drawn for 18 frames after the press. A frame-timing flake as far as anyone can tell; worth watching with the wake policy.
- The internal identifiers still say tab group and Space (Profile).
- The sidebar's day tabs are still one list of loose spaces and named ones; a per-Profile order of spaces (the row *is* the list of spaces) would replace `#tabOrder` and `beforeUnit`.
- Arrangements are still the shell's, in this device's localStorage, forty at most; with every space a desk, main's session is their natural home.

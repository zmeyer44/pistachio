# The desk's agent

A tab group's desk is a task, and the agent can work at it with the person. The desk is the agent's context and its bounds: it sees every window and the group's tabs not out, keeps the task's own files and facts, and works in the group's tabs, where the person can watch it. Concepts and the choice between them: the "Desk Companion" study (claude.ai artifact, 30 Sep 2026). Chosen: the **Co-worker**, in the **Bar**, with the context as a **Stack**, the last row under the group's tabs in the sidebar (it was in the desk's own dock until the sidebar became the dock, 1 Oct 2026); the agent may rearrange windows; one conversation per group, any conversation can be continued in a group; the group's context syncs.

Desktop only (the desk is). Builds on `docs/desk.md` (the desk itself), `docs/console-routing.md` (turns) and `docs/notes.md` (the sync pattern the context follows).

## 1. What a person does

**The Bar.** A notch at the foot of the desk, centred on the desk beside the sidebar: the shell's own ground rising out of the surface's edge, as a laptop's notch hangs from the top of its screen, turned over — square where it leaves the edge, rounded at its shoulders (14px idle, 22px grown), flared into the edge (10px). It is not painted: it is a hole through the well and the windows under it, so it is the ground whatever the theme (docs/desk.md, *The foot*). It holds the message field (its placeholder names the group), attach, the conversations button, the answer chevron, the microphone and send (stop while the agent works; the field then steers), each button with a tooltip (drawn under a cover, `bar-tip`). It fades in as the desk opens, and goes when the desk leaves. Idle — nothing typed, staged or warned of, no dictation, the agent not at work, the answer, the conversations and the @ list closed, no files on their way, and neither the keyboard nor the pointer in it — it is a small notch instead (32px tall), as wide as what it says: the group's mark, *Ask about* the group, and ⌘I. The pointer resting on it (80ms, so one passing over does not), a click, ⌘I, a drag of files or any of the rest grows it into the Bar, a notch still, and it goes back to its idle size as soon as none holds. The change is transitions.dev's plus → menu morph: the notch widens and rises (350ms with a little overshoot; back, 250ms and calm) while the idle face fades and blurs out and the row, at the Bar's full width throughout, is uncovered as the notch widens around it. The windows fill the desk under it, whole: over a live page the idle notch is drawn by main's notch view, above the page (docs/desk.md, *The foot*), and the pointer on it is the Bar's. Grown, it is a cover (`bar`, its whole grown footprint and flares) — the pointer coming to it asks the pages under it to give way at once, it grows once they have (or after 300ms), and it stays a cover until it is back down (280ms). Until 2026-10-02 it was a glass pill floating a gap above the desk's foot, and the desk kept a band for it (`BAR_BAND`) that windows never went into. ⌘I puts the keyboard in it (on a desk, ⌘I is the Bar's, not the sidebar chat's — also from inside a page, which main relays).

**Dictation.** The microphone button listens: the field gives way to a waveform of what it hears, scrolling in from the trailing end, and a clock, with discard (×) at the leading end and Done (✓) where send was. Done, or Enter, transcribes what was said with the host's speech-to-text (main's `transcribeSpeech`, the walkthrough's) and puts it in the field where the caret was, spaced from the words either side, to be read back and sent; it is never sent by itself. Escape or × drops the recording, even one being transcribed. A tap under 0.8 s is not sent; a recording stops itself at two minutes and is transcribed. A refused microphone, a failed transcription or silence says so in the Bar's banner, and the typed text stays. The logic is `lib/dictation.ts` (`Dictation`, free of React, and `useDictation`); the waveform is `components/desk/DictationWave.tsx`.

**The answer.** A card of the same glass, as wide as the Bar and resting on it however tall the Bar grows (they share one column), shows the latest exchange: what was asked, the agent's steps as it takes them, its reply as it streams, and anything it asks (a question, an approval, a takeover). It opens when a turn starts (the person's message, or the agent asking something) and stays until collapsed (its chevron, or Escape); the Bar's chevron brings it back. "Whole conversation" in its header shows every turn, scrolled to the end. While the card is up, the windows under it are stills (it is a cover). No "I'm on it" intro and no "browsing in another tab" chip at a desk: the agent is seen on the windows.

**The answer, detached** (2026-10-07; `components/desk/answer-motion.ts`, `lib/desk/answer-float.ts`). The answer's header is what it is taken by. Pressed, the card lifts (a hair larger, its shadow deeper, a sheen); pulled, it follows the pointer 1:1, still looking docked, and past 42px it tears off into a window: its frame morphs to a window's size (440 wide, or the size it was last left at) and corners (18 → 14px) under the hand, the content laid out once at the new size and clipped to the frame as it goes, a still copy of how it was fading out over it; its header gains a grip and a dock button in place of the chevron. Let go before 42px, it springs back into its place. Floating, it is a window: a press elsewhere (a live page's too) and Escape leave it be. It is moved by its header, and thrown as the desk's windows are (Glide, a rebound that settles flush), settling into the nearest of six anchors (each side's top, middle and foot) when thrown toward one or let go near one, and flush with an edge it is let go near; it rests clear of the Bar. Its edges and corners resize it (the ring straddles its border, mostly outside it; no smaller than 320×220), the opposite edge holding still. The Bar's chevron becomes a floating-window mark with a green dot, and pressing it calls the card out (a ring flashes round it) rather than hiding it; the Bar itself goes back to its idle notch, as with no card up. Brought back over the Bar, a ghost of the docked slot comes up as it approaches (never as it leaves: a card just torn off must leave the zone once before it counts), brightening once the card's foot is down at the slot (or the pointer on the Bar), the card shrinking a little and leaning toward it; let go there — or thrown down into it, or its dock button, or a double press of its header — and it morphs back into the slot, the ghost dissolving into it. Held over the Bar it goes no lower than its slot (it sits on the Bar, never slides under it). Escape mid-gesture puts it back where the gesture began (a tear goes back onto the Bar). Whether it floats, where (a share of the lane) and its size are kept on this device (`pistachio.desk.answer.v1`), so the next answer opens where the last was left. Its motion is imperative: one animation frame loop writes its transforms and layout size (React draws its content, never its place); the drag layer holds the pointer over live pages (`startPaneDrag`); its cover follows it as drawn, reaching 48px ahead while it moves so the pages it heads over give way first, and it stays shown while they do. The windows under a floating card are stills while it lies over them, as under the docked one — but floating, it is a window among the desk's (`DeskEngine.stackCover`): a window the person brings up (a press, the dock, the sidebar) comes in front of it, its page live again, and the card shows where none is over it; a press on the card, a turn starting (the person's message, the agent asking), or the Bar's button brings it back in front of them all. A press on a window also takes the keyboard from the Bar, whose grown cover would otherwise keep that page a still. Reduced motion: no lift, overshoot or cross-fade — it moves and changes size at once.

**The agent at the desk.**
- *Where it is*: the window whose tab it is working in (`agentDrivenTabId`) wears the agent's comet ring on its frame and a chip saying what it is doing (Reading, Clicking, Typing, Opening, Arranging, Noting, Thinking between steps) — in the bar frame's title row, or above a tab or handle frame. The tab's row in the sidebar wears the ring too (on its icon). The page itself glows while the agent drives it (the glow main already injects).
- *Windows it opens* join the group and come out onto the desk beside the window in use, under it in the stack, never taking the keyboard or the selection.
- *Arranging*: it may place, tile, cascade, put away and bring out windows. After a turn that moved windows, the answer card offers **Undo layout**, which puts every window back where it was before the turn.
- *Notes on windows*: it may pin a short note (80 characters) to a window's frame ("Lands 11:05, before check-in"), beside the chip. Notes stay until the next turn starts or the person dismisses them (×). A note needs the window out on the desk.
- *Leaving the group*: moving a tab out of the group is asked first.

**Conversations.** Each group has its own. Opening the desk opens the group's conversation (or a new, empty one); passing the desk to another group opens that group's; leaving the desk goes back to the conversation that was open before. The conversations button lists every conversation, each marked with where it started (This desk, another group by name, Another group when that group is not here, Sidebar, Reminder): choosing one continues it here, and from then on it is this group's (the group it came from keeps it too). New conversation, the list's first row, starts an empty one for the group. A press anywhere else (a live page's too, which main relays), or Escape, puts the list away. While the agent is acting, the console is its: nothing can be chosen, and a desk that comes up then leaves that conversation open.

**The Stack.** The group's context: the *Context* row under the group's tabs in the sidebar, a pile of sheets with its count (empty, one dashed sheet and *Drop files*; on the rail, the pile alone with the count on it). Drop files from Finder on it (pictures, PDFs, text, markdown, CSV, calendar, JSON, Word and Excel files), or text and links dragged out of a page; it bounces when something comes in. Click it to open its card beside the sidebar, over the desk: the files as tiles, the facts, snippets and links as lines, **Add files…**, a field to type a fact, and × on each. A file opens on the desk as a document window, out of the Stack, and goes back into it when collapsed (`docs/desk-documents.md`); a dot marks one whose window is out, and ↗ opens it in its own app (a private temporary copy). Files dropped on the desk itself join the Stack too, and open where they were let go; in the Bar, `@` mentions one, which then rides with the message. What the agent saved is marked (✦, "Pistachio ·"). Contexts of groups not in this Space's list — another Mac's, synced, or another Space's — are offered under **From another Mac or Space**, to bring into this group (a copy).

## 2. What the agent gets

**Scope.** While a desk is up, a turn in its conversation runs on a scoped browser: `tabs_list` lists only the group's tabs and the agent's own hidden ones, and every page tool refuses any other tab with an error that says to ask the person and open its address with `tab_open`. `tab_open` opens a hidden tab, as everywhere on the desktop (`BrowserController.openHiddenTab`): out of the group, off the desk, out of the person's sight, so the agent looks things up while the desk stays as the person has it. A page the agent's own click or key opens is hidden too (`holdPopupsHidden`). Only `tab_show` puts a hidden tab on the desk — when the person asks to open or see a page, or to put it on the desk: it joins the group (`showHiddenTabInGroup`) and comes out quietly, beside the window in use. The scope is the controller's, not the prompt's: `DeskScope` wraps `DesktopBrowserBackend`. (A page's own `window.open` or `target="_blank"` from one of the desk's tabs on the person's click opens beside it in its group, before it is shown: `BrowserController`'s window-open handler, `#onDesk`, `createTab({ beside })`. A desk turn holds its group for as long as it runs (`holdGroup`), so this goes on after the person leaves the desk or passes it to another group mid-turn. Otherwise the desk would see an active tab not its own and leave.)

**The desk block.** Each turn's message carries the desk as it stands, read from the shell at the turn's start: the group's name, every window (tab id, title, address, box as percent of the desk, stacking, which is in use), the group's tabs not out, and the context's items (id, kind, name or text). A few hundred tokens; pages are read with the page tools as ever.

**Desk tools** (group `desk`, host-supplied, only on a desk turn):

| Tool | Does |
| --- | --- |
| `desk_state` | The desk block, fresh. |
| `desk_arrange` | Tile or cascade every window; place windows in a zone (halves, quarters, center, full) or a box; put windows away into the sidebar; bring the group's other tabs out. Focus and the keyboard never move. |
| `desk_note` | Pin a short note to a window's frame, or clear it. |
| `desk_ungroup` | Move tabs out of the group (they stay open). Asked of the person first. |
| `context_read` | Read a context item: a fact's text, a text file's text, an image or PDF as a file the model sees. |
| `context_save` | Save a fact or snippet to the group's context. |

Desk turns always take the browser path (no answer-path routing, route basis `skipped`): the desk tools and pages are the point of a desk. `DESK_RULES` join the browse prompt: work from the desk block, stay on the desk, arrange confidently (Undo exists) and say so, note sparingly, read and save context, ask before ungrouping, name windows by title.

## 3. How it works

**Desk session** (`RunController`). The shell tells main when a desk is up and for which group (`deskConversation` enter/leave, passing on a switch; `choose`, `new`). While a session is up: the open thread is the group's (bound in `desk-conversations.json`, group → thread, `DeskConversationStore`), every turn in it is desk-scoped, a thread started in it is marked with the group (`RunSummary.groupId`, on `ThreadListItem` too) and bound to it. A turn is desk-scoped when a desk is up and its group is bound to the open thread (never a cloud run). Main keeps one open thread, as always; the desk only chooses which. A shell that reloads leaves the desk (`did-start-loading`).

**Desk bridge** (`desk-bridge.ts`). Main asks the shell for what only the shell knows (the desk's layout) and to change it: a request with an id on `pistachio:desk-request`, the reply by `deskReply(id, …)`, with a 5 s timeout (a failed reply is an ordinary tool error). Every request names the group whose desk it is for, and the shell refuses one for a desk no longer in view before it changes anything (the person may pass the desk to another group mid-turn). The shell answers from the desk's engine (`desk-requests.ts`, fed by `DeskSurface`), trimming titles and addresses to the reply's bounds (`MAX_DESK_TITLE`, `MAX_DESK_URL`, `MAX_DESK_DOCKED`): `state` (`agentLayout`), `arrange` (`arrangeFor`, which refuses strangers, putting away the window in use, or a person's gesture in progress, and then moves nothing), `bringOut` (waits up to 2 s for the tab to reach the shell's group, then `bringOutQuietly` — under the window in use, no selection), `note`. Each reply is checked in main to be the same group's desk. Before the first change of a turn the shell keeps `layoutSnapshot()`; the answer card's **Undo layout** is `restoreLayout` for that turn. A window still on its way into the sidebar when it is wanted back (Undo, or the agent placing or bringing it out again) turns back (`#recall`, making room first on a full desk) rather than being lost to the dock; one on its way into the Close pad or another group's icon goes on — that was the person's doing.

**The Bar's notch** (`DeskEngine.setNotch`, `setNotchShape`). The idle notch's size, centred on the desk's foot, is what windows under it are cut short of; the notch as drawn now is the hole cut through the well and the windows. The engine's `#usable` and `#reach` are the whole card (until 2026-10-02 they stopped above the Bar's band, `setBarBand`). The agent's zones and boxes are percents of `#usable` (`lib/desk/agent.ts`: `deskZoneRect`, `percentBox`, `boxRect`).

**Group context** (`group-context-store.ts`). One index file and content-addressed files in user data (`group-context.json`, `group-blobs/<sha24>`, owner-only permissions). Synced as two record kinds, sealed with the account's workspace key like notes: `group-context:<groupId>` (LWW: the group's title when written, its items, and the files' ids) and `group-blob:<sha24>` (a file's bytes, only files up to `MAX_GROUP_BLOB_BYTES`; bigger ones stay on the Mac they were dropped on and are listed elsewhere as not here). Tab groups do not sync live between Macs; a group pulled from another Mac keeps its id, so its context is there with it.

## 5. Not done / open questions

- Masking a region of a page (`window_mask`), pointing at an element in a page, and closing tabs are not desk tools yet.
- Cloud runs have no desk: a cloud conversation opened on a desk is not desk-scoped.
- The context's files are read by the model as they are (a PDF as a PDF); nothing is summarised when it is dropped.
- A context brought from another Mac is a copy: the two groups' contexts go their own ways afterwards.
- The Stack's files show as type glyphs, not previews.
- A text or link dragged out of a live page onto the Stack is untested against real pages (the e2e drops a file synthetically).
- No live-model measurement yet of how well the agent uses the desk tools (the e2e and unit tests script the model).
- The cloud browser's record allow-list does not carry group contexts (by design: it has no desk).
- Dictation is transcribed when the person is done, not word by word as they speak (the host's speech-to-text takes a whole recording). The sidebar console's composer has its own copy of the same recording logic; it could move onto `useDictation`.
